D.20State Capacity, Policy Implementation, and Governance ​

In briefBefore asking whether governance has capacity, ask for which task, at which stage, for which population, and within what period. Success inside a procedure cannot substitute for coverage of the entire eligible population.

In the main text, see §7.3.7–7.3.8, §10–11, §14, and §15.3.5. Research entries cover 20.01–20.10.

State formation, fiscal and military mobilization, administrative autonomy, embeddedness, legitimacy of rule, social identification, and public services are distinct processes. Compliance can have several causes. Administrative reach can coexist with gaps in rights. State capacity should be specified by task, level, and organizational resources. One aggregate measure of strength cannot substitute for this analysis.

A policy chain can distinguish entry of an issue onto the agenda, rulemaking, definition of eligibility, making budgets available, frontline implementation, notification and application, determining and paying benefits, actual use, and correction through appeal. The stages have different denominators and timing. Rates for eligible people, applicants, people approved, and actual recipients must not be mixed. Whether the calculation uses households or individuals can also change the result.

Frontline discretion can compensate for rules that do not fit or create bias and exclusion. Assess it in light of task conflicts, resources, and oversight. Polycentric governance requires actual authority and coordination. A large number of institutions does not suffice. Anti-corruption and oversight work must check who appoints, who pays, who selects the sample, and who has authority to implement and review. The value of local knowledge does not establish that local elites represent every resident.

In a teaching example, random inspections improve the completeness of records while remedies for ordinary applicants remain slow. Oversight effects and remedies in individual cases each have their own scope. Temporary aid can improve people’s lives while leaving questions about continuing finance and the right to choose. For an institutional externality, specify which decision accounting excludes it. Harm can be recorded before final legal responsibility is established.

Specify whom autonomy is relative to and what it concerns. Freedom from party control, discretion under administrative rules, and authority for local bodies or users to change rules are different dimensions. Ties to higher levels can provide resources and remedies or control goals and individual cases. Approval rates, the proportion who have ever received benefits, and continuously available services have different denominators and time conditions. Retain local improvements already observed. Successes among people inside the procedure cannot establish coverage of the entire eligible population.

Research topics. 20.01 State formation and fiscal-military states (Tilly and others). 20.02 Legitimacy, domination, and identification with the state (Weber and others). 20.03 State capacity, autonomy, and embeddedness (Mann, Evans, Fukuyama, and others). 20.04 Street-level bureaucracy and policy implementation (Lipsky). 20.05 Polycentric governance and the commons (Ostrom). 20.06 Public policy, implementation, and feedback. 20.07 Central–local relations and scale. 20.08 Corruption, capture, and principal–agent relations. 20.09 Comparisons of democratic, authoritarian, bureaucratic, and technocratic governance. 20.10 Governance failure, state fragility, and reconstruction.

Understanding this section

D.20 State Capacity, Policy Implementation, and Governance

Before asking whether governance has capacity, ask for which task, at which stage, for which population, and within what period. Success inside a procedure cannot substitute for coverage of the entire eligible population.

Brief quotation, translated from Chinese: ‘State capacity should be specified in terms of concrete tasks, levels, and organizational resources.’ State formation, fiscal and military mobilization, administrative autonomy, embeddedness, legitimacy, identification, and public services are distinct processes. Strong implementation in one area does not prove strength elsewhere. Nor can observed compliance be attributed solely to identification: dependence, penalties, or routine practices may also be explanations requiring investigation. [T-main]

The policy chain distinguishes at least problem entry onto the agenda, rulemaking, eligibility definition, budget delivery, frontline implementation, notification and application, determination and payment, actual use, and appeal and correction. Completion of one stage may condition the next, but does not substitute for its actual realization; for example, funds reaching an institution do not mean an applicant can use a service. [T-main]

Denominators must be explicit at every stage. Eligible people, notified people, applicants, approved applicants, recipients, and those with sustained usable access each have their own sets and time periods. The approval rate can truly increase while overall receipt coverage remains low. If eligibility is counted by household and receipt by individual, mapping and duplication must be explained; the two counts cannot simply be divided. [T-main]

Frontline discretion can compensate for poorly fitting rules or create exclusion. Examine conflicting tasks, resources, actual choices, and oversight rather than prejudging every workaround as dereliction or innovation. Polycentric governance also means more than opening additional institutions: each must possess real powers, resources, and coordination. If appointments, funding, sampling, implementation, and review all pass through the same controlling gate, the number of institutions does not prove independence. [T-main]

The usefulness of local knowledge does not mean locally powerful people represent tenants, minorities, nonparticipants, and future users. Connections to higher authorities can provide resources and remedies, but can also control objectives and individual cases. Autonomy must specify relative to whom and in which matter: freedom from party control, discretion within administrative rules, and local or user powers to change rules do not lie on the same axis. [T-main]

The random-inspection example in the source is a teaching scenario. More complete records can constitute a local improvement in oversight without proving faster remedies for ordinary applicants. Temporary assistance can also genuinely improve living conditions while long-term fiscal continuity and freedom of choice remain unresolved. Institutional externalities should identify which decision-making calculation omits which costs. An evidenced harm can be described before a final determination of legal liability. [T-main]

Real-world reference: Recommendation 13.1 of Australia's Royal Commission into the Robodebt Scheme report of July 7, 2023 calls for consultation with frontline staff in designing and implementing new programs; Recommendation 13.2 calls for management to review and respond to their feedback. The additional observation here is that ‘receiving frontline input’ differs from ‘management responding’ within the policy implementation chain. This does not reuse the case as one about legal authorization. Only the recommendation text is checked here; it does not claim that the recommendations were implemented or that applicants received remedies. [P-ROBO]

§§7.3.7–7.3.8 provide interfaces for feedback and the conditional potential field; Parts X–XI trace layers, channels, and events; Part XIV examines normative choice and authorization; and §15.3.5 connects to the institutional module. These connections do not reduce state capacity to a single HV score: specific tasks still require domain evidence. [T-main]

Research navigation: 20.01, state formation and the fiscal-military state (Tilly and others), asks how resource mobilization forms; 20.02, legitimate domination and identification (Weber and others), distinguishes grounds of compliance; 20.03, state capacity, autonomy, and embeddedness (Mann, Evans, Fukuyama, and others), requires specified tasks and relationships; 20.04, street-level bureaucracy (Lipsky), concerns frontline discretion; and 20.05, polycentricity and the commons (Ostrom), concerns real powers and coordination. This lesson explains the book's entry points without claiming to have read these scholars' complete works. [T-main]

20.06, public policy implementation and feedback, connects to the entire policy chain; 20.07, central–local relations, examines scale, resources, and control; 20.08, corruption, capture, and principal–agent relations, asks who appoints, pays, samples, and reviews; 20.09, comparisons of democratic, authoritarian, bureaucratic, and technocratic governance, must hold tasks and populations constant rather than produce overall rankings; and 20.10, governance failure, fragility, and reconstruction, separates functional interruption from subsequent continuation. One failure cannot establish that all state capacities have fallen to zero. [T-main]

How the argument develops

  1. 1

    Fix the task, level, target population, and period.

  2. 2

    List the state of each policy-chain stage and its actual evidence; do not use upstream success to fill downstream gaps.

  3. 3

    Specify denominators, household-to-person mappings, and duration.

  4. 4

    Examine frontline discretion and the structure of oversight and control; distinguish nominal polycentricity from actual powers.

  5. 5

    Retain local improvements and omitted harms; check effects, legality, and normative choice separately.

Teaching construction: do not mix four different ratiosTeaching scenario

AssumptionsA fictional example using the same window, individual counting units, nested populations, and no duplication.

In an artificial eligible population of 100 people, 40 apply, 30 are approved, and 24 actually receive benefits. The approval rate is 30/40 = 75%; receipt among approved applicants is 24/30 = 80%; receipt among all applicants is 24/40 = 60%; and receipt coverage of the eligible population is 24/100 = 24%. All four fractions are correct, but they answer different questions. Without records for subsequent months, this cannot be called sustained 24% coverage.

BoundaryThis represents no real policy effect; household units or different windows require recalculation.

Teaching construction: a limited conclusion about improved oversightTeaching scenario

AssumptionsThe numbers are entirely fictional; the before-and-after difference is not treated directly as a causal effect.

In an artificial example, incomplete records fall from 10 to 2 after an inspection, but no data on appeal processing times have been obtained. The change in the recordkeeping endpoint can be registered. Attributing it to the inspection still requires a causal comparison, and claiming faster remedies requires corresponding timing and downstream evidence.

BoundaryDo not erase the observed improvement in records or use it to prove that all governance is effective.

Does a high approval rate show that everyone receives benefits?

No. Nonapplicants and nonrecipients may be absent from the denominator, and sustained usable access requires a separate check.

Basis: The policy chain.

Why does local participation not automatically represent everyone?

Representative relationships and omitted positions must be examined; the voices of locally powerful people may not cover everyone affected.

Basis: Power and coverage.

Does the existence of a commission recommendation mean it has been implemented?

No. Independent evidence is needed about management changes, implementation, and applicant outcomes.

Basis: The limits of the real-world case.
