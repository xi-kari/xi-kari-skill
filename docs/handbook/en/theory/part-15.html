Part 15 Intervention and Applications ​

This Part puts a chain of constrained conversions between “finding a problem” and “changing reality.” It first separates four distinct tasks, then assigns authorized candidate actions to T0–T4, and finally turns to nine domain modules. This preserves the framework’s explanatory power while preventing its terms from crossing authorization boundaries and conferring authority to act.

This Part sets out the theoretical meaning, protection requirements, and limits of evidence for intervention levels and application modules. Structured contracts[1] can carry registration and validation. Their IDs, names, fields, and rules must be checked separately against the versions actually in use. A file’s existence or a passing format check does not establish that the implementation matches the meaning of the text.

15.1The Four Tasks Must Be Kept Separate ​

The conversion chain has a fixed order. Explanation → Diagnosis → Normative Choice → Intervention.

Explanation asks how the object operates under a specified boundary, scale, time window, and K. It produces descriptive or explanatory propositions, evidential status, a null model, alternative explanations, and the action ceiling warranted by the descriptive account. It cannot produce adjudications of responsibility, value priorities, or commands to act.

Diagnosis[2] asks which problem most warrants attention under the current evidence, what competing explanations exist, and what evidence would overturn the judgment. It must pass through the tool workflow. For a strong judgment it must also preserve the conditions that would reverse the judgment, a repair window, evidence requirements, appeal, anti-retaliation, supplementation of evidence, external review, and withdrawal. Diagnosis itself confers no authorization.

Normative choice asks who may choose what from which options, under what jurisdiction, validity period, and value premises. Evaluations, recommendations, and draft institutional arrangements for deliberation must explicitly register N premises and conflicts, affected positions, costs, and an option to take no action. Actual adoption requires running SEL-AGT or SEL-GOV and passing C12 and O1–O3. Legal validity, normative justification, and J-axis authorization must be verified separately. A majority, stability, persistence, efficiency, and model confidence cannot substitute for them.

Intervention uses only choices and authorization that already exist. It does not create authorization. Real-world action must be linked to a unique selected_action and must have a passing 10-component C12 bridge record, explicit N premises at runtime, O1–O4, the complete protection floor, and currently valid, independently reviewed atomic J authorization. The J tuple must cover the same deciding subject, object, action, and territory, as well as the entire validity period. An unrelated authorization cannot support the proposed action merely because it is valid.

If any requirement for real-world action is missing, execution must stop. The corresponding action requirements may call only for no action, more evidence, preservation, continued deliberation, or a recommendation for external referral. Any actual action in that list still requires applicable, independently valid authority. “We have explained it clearly,” “the problem is serious,” and “the objective is well-intentioned” all skip required steps if given as grounds to act.

15.2What T0–T4 Have in Common ​

T0–T4 are types of intervention task. They do not indicate maturity, a developmental ladder, the relative merit of objects, or a label of identity. Each level fixes classification_kind as intervention_task_type and fixes maturity_rank and mandatory_sequence as false. The numbers set the order of reading only. If safety conditions fail, T4 can be assessed directly without any prior T level. An object with reliable evidence of safety, minimum order, and structure need not perform each level in turn just to complete the form. “Directly” means only that a T-level route has no prior-level requirement. A diagnosis never yields authorization directly. None of the four tasks of explanation, diagnosis, normative choice, and intervention may be skipped, nor may C12, J, or O1–O4. Re-select the task level whenever the object, scale, K, time window, jurisdiction, affected positions, or action changes substantially.

No level may generate its own authorization. Each must record entry conditions, prerequisites, permitted and prohibited actions, evidence, C12, N at runtime, O1–O4, atomic J authorization, PF-1 through PF-10, the action ceiling, stopping, appeal, rollback, failure, and rules for switching levels. Weak signals, low power, inability to exit, and anti-retaliation are strict checks on whether action may proceed.

Every record other than draft must retain an execution_state_log that cannot be erased. Append state events in order, linking each event through the preceding hash and its own hash. An external anchor records the event count, head hash, and current state. Each event retains the complete choice at the time and references to selected_action, C12, the single J authorization, and O1–O4. Stopping, rollback, completion, expiry, and withdrawal are no grounds for deleting the historical authorization chain. rolled_back, completed, expired, and withdrawn are terminal states for the record. Recomputing hashes in the same package cannot return it to active. Only new choice, authorization, and execution records can be created. The current execution state must also agree with the state of the complete 17-field choice record.

A hash chain can check links between stored contents and changes to their bytes. It cannot by itself establish that the whole log was never rewritten, that events actually occurred, or that the anchor is independent. The external anchor’s identity, time of acquisition, and control relationships must also be verifiable. Confirm execution outcomes separately with evidence from the relevant systems and the world. Recording serves traceability, correction, and protection. Cryptographic format cannot substitute for factual investigation.

15.2.1T0 | Carriers of Safety and Trust ​

T0 first makes it possible for evidence, counterexamples, refusal, and requests for help to enter safely. This is neither an exercise in psychological reassurance nor a requirement that affected people stay in dialogue. T0 should come first in cases of complex trauma, closed systems of concentrated power, sustained betrayal, inability to exit, or appeals mediated by the same chain of power. The same applies when diagnosis itself could locate, label, or punish the people concerned.

Permitted actions include anonymous or encrypted submission, submission through a representative, and phased disclosure. They include separating identity from factual material and recording refusal, withdrawal, emergency stops, and independent review. Test receiving carriers for consistent, predictable, noncoercive conduct over time. Preserve necessary evidence and connect people to external professional, safety, or statutory support.

Forced dialogue, mediation, reconciliation, and full disclosure are prohibited. Silence, delayed expression, anonymity, or submission through a representative must not be treated as unreliable. Do not help the party whose power is being questioned locate, label, or punish people affected. Do not invoke love, responsibility, maturity, or the larger interests at stake to demand continued exposure. Without a minimally safe way to receive material, only low-exposure preservation and external referral are permissible. T0 must not be represented as complete.

T0 must verify that the channel can actually be reached, access to identities is controlled, withdrawal and stopping take effect, and external review is independent. It must also establish a baseline for adverse changes after feedback. Weak signals enter a protected area pending review. Low-power positions and dependencies are listed separately. People unable to exit receive representation, local buffers, alternative arrangements to take over, and room for future choices. Loss of power, isolation, stigma, punishment, and changes in resources after feedback trigger anti-retaliation procedures.

Stop contact and propagation immediately if an identity is leaked, retaliation follows submission, the receiving party loses independence, continued contact increases immediate risk, or authorization ceases to hold. Revoke unnecessary access, restore anonymous channels or submission through a representative, remedy new harm, and move the carrier. Appeals allow anonymity, representatives, independent third parties, and emergency stops. Once safety is established, one may remain at T0 or move to T1/T2. If safety cannot be established, one may move directly to T4.

15.2.2T1 | Reversible Minimum Order ​

T1 applies when harm is spreading and basic functions are breaking down while full diagnosis still takes time. Its objectives are limited to stopping identifiable harm, preserving evidence and key supports, restoring minimal communication and essential services, and opening a new observation window.

Permitted actions include safe separation, reducing coupling, preserving evidence, protecting key points of support, and establishing temporary order with a short automatic expiry. All actions must be low-risk, observable, and reversible through rollback. In-depth diagnoses of personality or motives, forced mediation, discipline, permanent assignments of responsibility, removal of eligibility, public stigmatization, and irreversible structural changes are prohibited. Temporary measures must not quietly become ordinary rules through repeated renewals.

Entry into T1 requires specifying the immediate harm, consequences of taking no action, key variables for protection, a reversal path for each measure, and side effects by group. Minimum order must not shift the costs of maintaining function onto positions with the least authority, least resources, or care responsibilities. Near misses, silence, and anomalies at the margins remain weak signals. People unable to exit must not be presumed to consent because they stay. Reporting risk, refusing a temporary measure, or requesting review must not lead to retaliation affecting schedules, resources, services, or reputation.

Stop if the measures produce greater harm, reversibility is lost, the deadline is extended without authorization, costs rise at low-power positions, appeal is suppressed, or J authorization ceases to hold. Rollback must restore the original rights, services, resources, and records and remedy harm caused by the temporary measures. Once harm has stabilized and structural evidence is sufficient, T2 can be assessed. Return to T0 if safety deteriorates. Enter T4 if safe repair is infeasible or irreversible risks increase.

15.2.3T2 | Repair of the Uptake–Return Flow Chain ​

T2 addresses breaks located by evidence in cost-bearing, return flows of resources, transmission of implementation, write-back of bad news, uptake at intermediate levels, responsibility, and bounded authorization. Abstract labels such as “poor relations” or “poor execution” do not identify those breaks.

Before entering T2, establish a return-flow map for costs and benefits, a chain of responsibility and authorization, carriers for uptake at intermediate levels and channels for bad news, a plan to operate alternative channels in parallel, distributional impacts, and a repair baseline. Dismantling old channels must not outpace the establishment of alternatives. Permitted actions include repairing oversight and feedback, supporting uptake at intermediate levels, replacing nodes within limits or reconfiguring them gradually, testing return flows with the smallest verifiable change, and reallocating resources, responsibilities, and authorization within an explicit scope.

Do not dismantle old channels before alternatives operate. Contact, understanding, slogans, and project completion must not be treated as structural repair. Authorization for reform or dissipation must not be indefinite. Repair costs must not be concentrated on people with little power, people unable to exit, or people already exhausted. Short-term aggregate metrics and accounts from the center must not be used to establish that return flows close the loop.

Evidence must include chain nodes, direction of flow, delay, effects by group, resources that actually reach the downstream end, capacity at intermediate levels, pre-registered criteria for success and failure, and side effects. Workarounds at the downstream end, near misses, samples from the margins, and material omitted from the central summary enter as weak signals. Aggregate gains cannot hide net losses at low-power positions. People unable to exit must have participation through a representative, alternative arrangements to take over, and local buffers. Those who give feedback, object, or stop a trial are protected against retaliation.

Stop if alternative channels fail to operate, benefits do not return to positions under pressure, bad news cannot change the plan, margins decline at intermediate levels and the downstream end, harm to a group exceeds a threshold, or authorization ceases to hold. Rollback includes stopping expansion, restoring old channels or running them in parallel, reversing unauthorized reallocations, correcting eligibility and records, compensating for harm, and comparing options again. Assess T3 only when there is a stable closed loop and long-term uptake is in place. Enter T4 when repair is infeasible, power blocks it, or net harm grows.

15.2.4T3 | Deep-Time Maintenance and Higher-Order Updating ​

Use T3 only when there is already sustained evidence of safety, minimum order, and key return flows. It addresses maintenance debt, breaks in succession, dependence on a single center, slow variables, institutional aging, and recovery margins. It does not declare that the system has entered an “advanced stage.”

Permitted actions include establishing deep-time early warnings, monitoring slow variables, developing successors and allowing bounded trial and error, creating uptake across multiple centers, maintaining redundancy and alternative paths, conducting periodic reviews and vulnerability exercises, and writing back counterexamples and versions. Do not divert T1–T2 resources for safety, minimum order, or repair in the name of building the system. A higher-order steady state must not be recorded as a final state, a level of maturity, or a superior identity. Succession, exercises, and early warning must not be used to expand monitoring or make permanent eligibility judgments.

Evidence for entry includes data by group across cycles, maintenance debt, succession and substitution outcomes, recovery exercises, independent audits of vulnerabilities, adoption of counterexamples, and changes in versions. A narrative of long-term stability must not turn near misses, successors’ objections, or deviations in slow variables into noise. Account for maintenance costs by generation, territory, role, and resource position. People unable to exit have local exemptions, representation, and alternative arrangements to take over. Reporting an institutional vulnerability must not affect eligibility for succession, resources, or reputation.

T3 must stop when T0–T2 resources are squeezed, monitoring becomes unauthorized control, succession becomes reproduction of power, costs are shifted to low-power or future positions, or counterexamples cannot change rules over a sustained period. Rollback includes suspending long-term mechanisms, minimizing monitoring data or shutting off the data flow, restoring basic resources, withdrawing obsolete rules, and starting retirement or replacement. T3 can return to any task of protection or repair or move to T4. It is not the endpoint for every object.

15.2.5T4 | Exit, Transfer, and Preservation of Evolutionary Memory ​

T4 applies when staying or repairing is no longer safe, feasible, or justified. T4 can be assessed directly as a candidate without any prior T level if T0 cannot be established, anti-retaliation and appeal repeatedly fail, repair is blocked by power, the object’s anchor has effectively been lost, the net harm of staying exceeds the cost of transfer, or outside arrangements for taking over are safer. Actual exit, transfer, or handling of memory still requires normative choice, C12, J, and O1–O4.

Permitted actions include designing safe exit, local buffers, staged transfer, and alternative arrangements to take over. Preserve evidence, skills, lessons from failure, and attempts at repair only with consent and to the minimum extent necessary. Settle care, compensation, and outstanding responsibilities. Protect those who stay and those unable to exit. Verify the new system taking over and retain an option for a second transfer. A costly strategic action can be considered only if the subject participates voluntarily and with knowledge, can bear the cost, explicitly records the experience, and does not exhaust all their remaining strength.

Do not romanticize exit as courageous, loyal, mature, or the only right course. Do not help responsible parties use exit to evade compensation, care, or legal duties. Do not require people affected to publicly prove their circumstances. Individual material must not be entered into public memory without consent. Do not assume the new system is inherently safe. Do not conscript others’ love and sacrifice.

For people unable to exit, the goal is to reduce harm, preserve subjecthood, restore minimal choices, and establish local buffers and a future way out. It is not to “complete an ideal exit.” The wish to leave, hesitation, wariness about the new carrier, and anomalies among those who stay are weak signals. T4 must not become a unilateral escape for those with more resources. If exiting, refusing a transfer, preserving evidence, or appealing is followed by withdrawal of supplies, tracking, stigma, or retaliation against those who stay, review protection and halt the steps that increase danger.

Suspend or redirect the affected steps of transfer, contact, or data handling when risks in the new arrangements increase, the subject withdraws, exit provokes retaliation, memory is used beyond the permitted purpose, obligations are evaded, or authorization ceases to hold. Suspension does not require a subject to remain exposed to danger. Continue necessary support on independently valid grounds. Restore rights and resources that can be restored, revoke data uses outside the permitted scope, correct/delete records, and remedy harm to the subject and to those who stay. On entry to the new system, register T0, scale, and K for the new object. Do not carry over the old authorization.

Exit involves more than deleting an object from a table. Ending an organization, changing jobs, separating within a family, relocating, and retiring a tool can leave responsibilities for wages, care, debts, compensation, knowledge handover, and data custody. Obtain separate evidence that the new arrangements actually operate, dependents receive support, those who leave are protected against retaliation, and historical responsibilities have been handled. The name of an alternative or a handover document cannot substitute for this evidence. 〔Studies 16.08–16.09, 23.04, 24.06, 28.08, 29.08〕

Assessing success or failure in T0–T4 first requires fixing the function and conditions of support at issue. The ability to express oneself with continuing assistance, availability of a service with a temporary power supply, or continuation of a task by replacement staff can all be accurately limited outcomes. If the goal includes independent completion, stability over the whole period, or full recovery of living conditions, check separately what happens after support is withdrawn, what resources are available afterward, and whether the relevant endpoints are reached. An uncompleted broader goal does not cancel a narrower one already achieved. Achieving the narrower goal does not establish full recovery.

When work is taken over after an exit, distinguish who carries out tasks, who arranges and verifies them, who is on call, and who bears the costs. If the person previously responsible still arranges and checks tasks after handover, acknowledge the reduction in execution work and retain the coordination burden that has not been transferred. Safe exit does not require affected people to supply a perfect replacement first. Where bounded responsibilities still have independent grounds, parties with authority and resources must state the scope and duration of the arrangements for taking over and the gaps that remain. The need for continuity must not be rewritten as a requirement that the original party stay indefinitely.

15.3Common Checks for the Application Modules ​

A domain name does not supply an object contract. For each application, first register the D0 object, nine-axis scale SP=<A,X,T,O,C,R,I,N,J>, K, time window, sources, and affected and low-power positions. Then enter the seven-gate tool workflow. An application module can change only the questions asked, risk checks, and action ceiling. It cannot substitute for domain investigation, professional judgment, legal procedures, or authorization.

All nine modules retain PF-1 through PF-10. They register affected and low-power positions, provide anonymous submission, submission through a representative, and low-exposure submission, and preserve refusal and stopping. They also require effective appeal, anti-retaliation, evidence supplementation and record correction, and external independent review. The remaining requirements are withdrawal, rollback, repair and version write-back, exit and transfer with protection through representatives when exit is impossible, and the action ceiling, prohibited actions, and stop triggers.

Each module below specifies the scope of domain evidence and how to downgrade outputs for gaps. It states what judgments the available materials can support, what facts and verification are still missing, and how those gaps limit the output. Each supported proposition has its own claim_id, material references in source_refs, and a value for max_inference from the closed set. A module title or list of materials cannot support a whole passage of conclusions.

Partial coverage can still be useful. The output ceiling must fall in line with the gaps. Every source_gap_effects entry must select from the closed enumerations “lower confidence, suspend the proposition, require external verification, or record only a gap in the inventory” and “no external action, require a domain protocol, narrow authorization, or suspend intervention.” It must not be none or ignore. The effects of gaps propagate along the dependencies of each proposition. A gap in one item does not invalidate all analysis. Argument evaluations, procedural critiques, and conditional recommendations under explicit premises can be retained separately when they have independent grounds.

15.3.1APP-REL | Relationships ​

The object is the interaction, shared commitments, boundaries, division of responsibility, and return flows within a particular time window. It does not encompass either party’s personality. A relationship, an individual’s psychology, family institutions, and events involving violence or coercion are distinct objects. Register scale and K again when the object changes.

Major risks include romanticizing one-sided effort as love, assuming both parties want repair, exposing people again through forced dialogue, underestimating dependence and the cost of exit, and weaponizing the framework’s terms. A lightweight entry asks first about felt safety, whether responsibility runs only one way, whether effort is reciprocated, whether both parties want to work toward repair, and whether boundaries can be respected. In the presence of violence, coercion, sustained humiliation, control of resources, isolation, continuing deception, or inability to exit freely, stop the joint diagnosis and turn to safety and external support.

Weak signals include hesitation, silence, avoidance, informal requests for help, and repeated boundary crossings. Register low-power positions in terms of dependence on housing, resources, care, identity, and reputation. People unable to exit may submit through a representative, disclose in stages, and use local buffers. Threats and changes in resources after setting a boundary, seeking help, refusing dialogue, or expressing a wish to leave enter anti-retaliation monitoring.

The tool can produce at most plain-language observations, escalation of risk, needs for external support, and candidates for T0–T4. It authorizes no mediation, disclosure, separation, or disposition of property. It does not diagnose personality or illness or substitute for psychological, medical, or legal judgment.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (partial coverage). The framework’s rules support a lightweight entry into relationship analysis, protection in informal settings, and protection for people unable to exit. Independent causal contracts for relationships, systematic cases, and evidence of intervention effects are still missing. The output can therefore include only observations, escalation of risk, and needs for support. It cannot determine personality, causal responsibility, or the effectiveness of intervention on that basis. Where violence, coercion, or consequences for property, residence, or guardianship are involved, hand the matter to the appropriate professional procedure and narrow the action ceiling.

15.3.2APP-FAM | Families ​

The family module checks co-residence, recognition of kinship, affection, property, care, labor, and legal eligibility separately. It assumes no membership boundary that assigns all rights and duties at once. People who live together need not jointly control resources. People who provide care need not acquire inheritance rights. Legally recognized family members may live apart for long periods. The nuclear family can be a particular statistical category and must not be assumed to be the only real form or the normative standard. A family as a whole, a relationship between two people, a chain of care, an individual safety event, and an external institution are distinct objects. Birth, death, illness, migration, and changes in guardianship or economic dependence may require rechecking the corresponding boundaries and K.

Major risks include recording children and dependents as consenting when they cannot exit, low visibility of care work and health costs, treating control of resources and isolation as private matters, overriding individual rights in the name of the family as a whole, and conscripting love, filial duty, responsibility, and sacrifice. Children, older people, patients, people with disabilities, economically dependent people, and primary caregivers must be listed separately.

Weak signals may be expressed in behavior, indirectly, through a representative, or after a delay. Members with little power must not bear the costs of maintaining the appearance of family stability. When people cannot leave, provide temporary safe places, backup resources, external consultation, and the option to refuse direct dialogue. Withdrawal of supplies, threats, monitoring, and stigma after seeking help trigger anti-retaliation and stopping procedures.

The module can generate only gaps in care or resources, safety boundaries, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize entering a household, contacting children or dependents, changing property or care arrangements, or making decisions about guardianship. It does not substitute for procedures for child protection, medical care, guardianship, or domestic violence.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (partial coverage). The framework’s rules can identify shared living arrangements, the privatization of costs at major life transitions, and protection needs where exit is impossible. The module’s evidence and protocols are incomplete for children, older people, people with disabilities, economically dependent people, and other subdomains. If materials are insufficient, record only risks and facts to verify. Do not presume consent, assign care responsibilities, or propose guardianship or disposition of resources. Child protection, medical, guardianship, or domestic violence procedures must provide further verification.

15.3.3APP-ORG | Organizations and Projects ​

The object is the chain of objectives, resources, positions, execution, feedback, and responsibility in a specified organization or project. Register the organization, project, department, position, and individual separately. When the object of analysis changes among individual performance, the function of a position, a process, a project, and the organization’s institutional arrangements, rebuild the K mapping.

Key risks include metrics displacing objectives, distortion in summaries at intermediate levels, pushing responsibility downward, invisibility of maintenance and care work, retaliation through performance evaluation against people who give feedback, outsourcing long-term costs through temporary projects, and using framework labels in personnel decisions. Beyond basic diagnosis, midsize organizations should add the chains of return flow and transmission, implementation incentives, carriers for uptake at intermediate levels, frequent small return flows, and channels for disputes that cause little disruption.

Central summaries must not silence frontline workarounds, near misses, maintenance debt, informal complaints, or signs that people may leave. List separately positions weak in contract terms, rank, resources, evaluation, or ability to exit. Where exit is blocked by the industry, income, visas, career history, or care responsibilities, provide representation and external review. Changes in performance ratings, schedules, promotion, contracts, or resources after dissent enter anti-retaliation monitoring.

The module can identify risks, breaks in uptake or return flow, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize scoring, appointment, discipline, layoffs, contract changes, or financial changes. Once diagnosis is used in a personnel decision, the corresponding action must be put on hold, people who provided feedback must be protected, and independent appeal and rollback must be available.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (partial coverage). The framework’s rules support checks on translation across organizational roles and levels, return flows, uptake at intermediate levels, responsibility chains, and concentrations of power. Evidence about the specific industry, position, labor relations, and project cycle still needs external supplementation. If on-site records, data by group, or independent review are missing, findings of fact, causal attributions of responsibility, and eligibility for real-world adoption that depend on them cannot be established. One may propose audit questions, recommendations for reversible trials, and assessments of texts, procedural critiques, and conditional plans that have independent grounds. These do not permit scoring, appointment, discipline, layoffs, or changes to contracts.

15.3.4APP-PLT | Platforms ​

The platform object is the chain of written rules, parameter rules, interface rules, models, distribution, behavioral responses, and data write-back at a frozen version. A public rule alone does not specify it. Register the platform, model, product interface, content network, user groups, and external social impacts separately. Revalidate K when the objective function, parameters, entry point, or model version changes.

Risks include recommendation loops that train on the world they shape, popularity bias, declining diversity, capture by proxy objectives, dual ledgers across text, parameters, and interfaces, automation bias, gates on visibility and eligibility, and control of appeals by the same chain of power. Audit the objective function, parameters, default entry points, human review, behavioral feedback, and effects by group that the current claim requires. Claims about internal mechanisms require the corresponding version and implementation material. Assessing a limited total effect supported by a comparison that meets the requirements does not require access to all internal parameters. Check training write-back only when model retraining actually occurs. Fixed distribution rules can also form feedback.

Material from low-traffic positions, minority groups, people wrongly excluded, and people who left must not be downweighted by count alone. List platform workers, small creators, people without an alternative platform, and people subject to automated decisions separately. Where platform lock-in exists, provide data migration, appeal through a representative, and human review. Throttling, bans, or changes in income after appealing, refusing a data use, or reporting an error are signals for anti-retaliation checks.

The module produces only audit questions about parameters or interfaces, effects by group, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize data access, post deletion, throttling, account bans, changes to models or parameters, or decisions on eligibility for work. Stop expanding use and roll back the version when the object version changes, harm to a group exceeds a threshold, human review is inaccessible, or rollback capability is lost.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (substantial coverage). The rules specify where to check networks, diffusion, conditional potential fields, parameters, recommendation loops, and automated governance. Specific platform mechanisms and effects still require independent evidence.

Research on recommendations should distinguish behavioral records, preference representations within the system, independently measured preferences, and actual consequences. Drift in representations during simulation cannot by itself establish changes in people’s internal preferences or real-world harm to groups (see Mansoury et al., 2020). Changes supported by other independent materials should still be retained. The existence of feedback, amplification of bias, and lock-in that is difficult to correct also require separate evidence.

Suspend an assertion about a parameter or internal mechanism if evidence on the relevant version, parameters, or implementation is insufficient. Randomized or quasi-experimental studies with clearly defined interventions and valid comparisons can support limited effects within their samples, treatments, outcomes, and time windows, even if researchers cannot obtain all internal parameters. Effects of the entire configuration cannot be recorded as independent contributions of its parameters or extrapolated to other versions. Without evidence about effects by group and effective review, do not certify fairness, safety, or eligibility for broader action.

15.3.5APP-INS | Institutions and Public Governance ​

The object is the structure of institutional rules, execution, resources, eligibility, feedback, responsibility, and public commitments within a frozen jurisdiction and time window. Register legal texts, policies, implementing institutions, budgets, informal rules, public objects, and affected groups separately. A state, institution, organization, and policy are distinct objects.

Major risks include shared sources for key channels, closure around concentrated power, filtering of bad news, backlash after appeals, pushing responsibility downward, oversight captured by beneficiaries of a rule, paper compliance standing in for reality, public commitments extracting sacrifice, majorities and stability overriding rights, and romanticizing multiple centers or autonomy. Legal validity and normative justification must be kept separate. Public benefits must be broken down by group, region, generation, and ability to exit.

Aggregate metrics must not silence downstream positions, minorities, regions at the margins, informal practices, and absence signals. List separately positions weak in rights, resources, eligibility, information, or ability to exit. People unable to exit a public institution or essential service need representation, alternative services, and external review. Administrative, occupational, service, eligibility, and reputational changes after reporting wrongdoing, mounting a defense, or appealing enter anti-retaliation audits.

The module can produce institutional risks, distributional impacts, gaps in procedure, stress tests, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize legislation, law enforcement, budgets, eligibility decisions, regulation, or disposition of public resources. Stop and roll back if appeals and review are controlled by the same chain of power, rights floors are breached, temporary measures become permanent, or the framework is used as a veneer of legitimacy.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (substantial coverage). The rules cover checks on concentrations of power, production and distribution, public commitments, multiple centers, bureaucracy, and misuse. Specific institutions still require case registration, comparison across jurisdictions, and causal evaluation. If evidence about applicable law, implementation, budgets, or affected groups is missing, stop each legal finding, certification of effects, or real-world adoption that depends on the gap. The framework cannot be used to prove a political position or issue authorization for public action. Independently grounded critiques of texts, institutional evaluations under explicit normative premises, and complete drafts of charters and plans for review can still stand. State the conditions of applicability, costs, and matters awaiting review for each item.

15.3.6APP-OPI | Public Opinion ​

The object is a process of public interpretation with the event, issue, groups, propagation network, platform conditions, and time window frozen. Facts, evidence, recognition, interpretation, responsibility, identity, and action must be kept at separate levels. Popularity does not establish who the public is or what is representative of it.

Major risks include substituting popularity for facts and agreement, creating false agreement by deleting comments or throttling, labeling critics as mechanisms of destabilization, substituting apologies for compensation, platform rewards for conflict, simple diffusion producing wide attention with little uptake, and forcing people affected to prove their circumstances publicly. The purpose of responding is to reduce factual confusion, obscured responsibility, loss of trust, and gaps in repair. It does not aim to suppress attention.

Low-attention material, expression from the margins, deleted material, silence, and absence enter as weak signals. List separately positions without agenda-setting or public relations resources and those bearing identity exposure. People unable to exit public exposure receive representation for speaking, reduced exposure, and correction of records. Online abuse, throttling, and changes in employment or resources after speaking up enter anti-retaliation monitoring.

The module produces only levels of clarification, gaps in evidence, questions about repair, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize deleting comments, throttling, controlling comments, publishing individual material, or classifying the public as a whole. Stop and publish a traceable correction when a factual version changes, the response increases exposure, a platform action exceeds authority, or corrections cannot be written back into the propagation chain.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (partial coverage). Record separately material eligible to enter the candidate content set, actual presentation, reading or contact, private judgments, public expression, and verifiable actions. Deletion and editing, media choice, shared events, and existing relations can all change the visible distribution. A change in exposure with no detected change in attitudes does not cancel the effect on exposure or establish permanent ineffectiveness. Popularity and participation counts use denominators for platform behavior. Public opinion requires its own population and sampling. Public legitimacy also requires independent normative and institutional grounds. If materials are insufficient, suspend claims about representativeness, mechanisms, or effects that depend on the gap. Independently grounded text evaluations, local facts, and conditional recommendations can be retained, with traceable correction available.

15.3.7APP-SPC | Space and Channels ​

The object is a network of space and channels with coordinates, functional meanings, population/users, time window, and layers of dependency frozen. Register physical locations, functional networks, administrative boundaries, service areas, and infrastructure layers separately. A connection on a map does not establish adequate capacity, substitutability, or actual access.

Risks include concentration of key nodes, overload at the center and disconnection at the margins, infrastructure failures with common causes, inadequate capacity and alternative routes, and changes to space shifting exposure and relocation costs. Verify the continuous path from actual points of departure to use of a service. Check road connections, times of day, fares, eligibility, physical and informational accessibility, and conditions for remaining at or using the site. Counts of facilities or geometric distance cannot substitute for this. Track original residents before the change, current residents, those who moved out, new arrivals, and those waiting to enter separately. A rise in an area average may result from population replacement and cannot be retroactively attributed to gains for original households. Check plans, conditions on site, and lived experience against one another. For local participation, also check who is absent and who can change the decision.

Breaks at the margins, maintenance workarounds, local congestion, and near misses are weak signals. List separately positions with limited mobility, health, income, digital access, or ability to speak. People unable to relocate or take a different route receive minimum services, alternative channels, and local safe places. Reporting infrastructure risks and refusing unsafe passage are protected against retaliation.

The module produces only requirements for maps, questions about protecting key channels, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize road closures, population relocation, land requisition, allocation of public resources, or engineering changes. Stop and restore channels and services if on-site conditions conflict with the map, alternative routes are not operating, or the changes increase harm at the margins.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (substantial coverage). The rules cover the spatial expression of potential fields, bottlenecks, centers and margins, layers of infrastructure, and the geography of recovery. Engineering safety, capacity thresholds, and on-site usability still require appropriate professional evidence. If a key parameter is unknown, suspend any judgment about channel safety or engineering feasibility that depends on it, and specify the site investigation and measurements needed. Other distributional facts, disputes over rights, or procedural evaluations with independent grounds remain. A map cannot authorize closures, relocation, or resource allocation, and on-site measurements alone do not determine the justified objectives of a change.

For urban development, housing, or reconstruction after a disaster, establish protected links among pre-disaster addresses, current locations, and destinations. Record separately building safety and renewed exposure, services households actually receive, the same residents’ relationship networks, local memory, and whether there is adequate information, feasible choice, and access to appeal and remedy. A well-kept streetscape, open facilities, signs of mail delivery, occupied plots, or an area average cannot substitute for the return of original residents, affordable housing, restored relationships, or authorization to represent others.

For comparisons across locations, group original residents, people who moved out, newcomers, renters, owners, caregivers, people waiting, and people unable to move. Record costs, times, capacity, alternative routes, risks at the destination, and who can change the plan. Connections on a map do not authorize relocation, land acquisition, closures, or resource allocation. If key on-site parameters are missing, the output can specify only the required site investigations and evidence. It must not conclude that a channel is safe or an engineering plan feasible.

15.3.8APP-EMG | Emergencies and High-Pressure Situations ​

The object is a system under high pressure within a specified incident phase, with defined hazards, critical functions, the command chain, information chain, and resource chain, affected areas, and a recovery window. Register the hazard, incident system, response organization, critical functions, and residents’ living conditions separately. Rescue is judged by whether time-sensitive protection of life and against serious harm reaches people. Stabilization is judged by the spread of harm and minimum services. Repair is judged by whether specified functions are usable under explicit conditions. Reconstruction separately checks longer-term facilities, livelihoods, and renewed exposure. Tasks can run in parallel or recur. They cannot share a single success criterion, deadline, or authorization.

In recovery, register rescue, stabilization, functional repair, reconstruction, and long-term recovery of living conditions separately. Building completion, occupancy of an area, and facility operation are distinct endpoints from service availability, housing burden, relationship networks, local memory, and residents’ decision-making authority. “Partial recovery” describes only an observable combination in which some facilities or housing have improved while services, affordability, social connections, or rights are still lacking. It must not be described as a feedback trap without evidence across periods and consideration of competing explanations.

Time pressure in an emergency does not cancel protection of people affected or confer on-site authority on the model. External statutory duties and professional procedure cards determine life-saving, evacuation, dispatch, and engineering actions. The framework only records phases, delays, people unable to evacuate, and gaps in care and appeal. When action exceeds authority, exceptional powers expire, or on-site information overturns assumptions, stop expanding the action and restore ordinary procedures.

Risks include cascades from complex interactions and tight coupling, alert fatigue, accumulation of workarounds and near misses, drift into failure under pressure for efficiency, multiple chains of command, indefinite extension of emergency powers, shifting the costs of narrowing functions, and breaks in the chain of recovery after rescue. The framework recognizes professional emergency authority first. Structural analysis must not delay life-saving action.

Near misses, on-site dissent, low-level alerts, and interruptions of supply at the margins must not be ignored because disaster has yet to occur. Prioritize recording people who cannot decide whether to evacuate, people with impaired mobility, people with limited information, and low-authority frontline positions. People unable to evacuate receive minimum supplies, alternative arrangements for taking over, and representation. Reporting danger, refusing unsafe orders, and appealing afterward are protected against retaliation.

The module can support recording risks, narrowing functions, and continuity of recovery. It does not substitute for medical, firefighting, engineering, or on-site command procedures, or confer authority to evacuate, dispatch, or take action. Temporary centralized coordination can align objectives, information, and resource interfaces. On-site discretion still requires an explicit scope of delegation, professional boundaries, and feedback on anomalies. Check separately the handover of command, winding down of coordinating bodies, termination of each statutory exceptional power, continuity of essential services, and long-term recovery of living conditions. Completing one step does not establish completion of the next, and unfinished recovery does not automatically extend all exceptional powers. When professional information from the site overturns the framework’s assumptions, the framework should return to recording and reviewing events. Expiry, actions beyond authority, and restoration of ordinary procedures must be handled individually under the corresponding powers and tasks.

Recovery can reach different endpoints in phases. If a temporary teaching site, transportation, teachers, and the term budget are all in place, education for this term can genuinely be restored. The original school building need not already have been rebuilt for that outcome to be real. But if no one is in place to take over the site rent and care work next month, opening classes today does not establish stability for the entire term. A later shock also does not automatically negate an earlier limited outcome. “False recovery” first describes a claim exceeding its evidence. It does not presume a motive to deceive. 〔Studies 31.10–31.11, teaching scenario〕

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (substantial coverage). The rules cover normal accidents, near misses, drift into failure, narrowing of functions, command, and chains of recovery. Specific disaster types, danger thresholds, statutory command, and professional operating cards must come from the relevant domain systems. Without on-site confirmation, a responsible professional, or valid emergency authorization, the framework only records information and flags risks. It must not delay life-saving action, substitute for command, or generate operational orders.

15.3.9APP-ULS | Ultra-Large Scale ​

The object is a nested system with boundaries, levels, jurisdiction, time window, key subsystems, resources/transmission/return flows, and external responses frozen. Register the whole, regions, groups, organizational levels, infrastructure, and external systems separately. Each move from local to aggregate, short-term to deep-time, or organizational to institutional requires reassessing object conversion, the nine-axis partial order, and K. An increase in scope of influence on the I axis does not expand authority to act on the J axis.

Major risks include using structural language to overestimate actual execution, insufficient resources and competing objectives, distortion of downstream transmission and feedback, obstruction by vested interests, reflexivity of metrics, fast risks crowding out slow variables, local failures spreading across levels, capture of governance, and large-scale irreversible side effects. A complete plan does not establish that resources are in place. Intentions at the center do not establish action at the downstream end. Short-term stability does not establish healthy slow variables.

Large-scale spatial and institutional options must also check whether aggregation mixes parcels, households, areas, and destinations into one denominator. Aggregate improvement can coincide with rising burdens on original residents, renters, people who moved out, or regions at the margins. Counts of new construction cannot be used to fill in relationships, memory, service eligibility, or actual choices. Preserve representation, minimum services, alternative arrangements to take over, and local options that can be rolled back for positions unable to exit or take another route.

Anomalous regions, minority groups, samples from the downstream end, nontextual experience, and absence signals must be brought back into the record through independent sampling. Disaggregate impacts by region, group, level, generation, and ability to exit. People unable to exit an institution, territory, or basic system receive representation, local exemptions, and minimum services. Changes in budgets, eligibility, employment, or services after submitting bad news enter anti-retaliation monitoring.

The module can produce only areas where judgment remains open, stress tests, trials at different levels, independent review, and candidates for T0–T4. It does not authorize a blanket prescription for the whole system or governance across domains. Stop expanding use and withdraw level by level if harm spreads across groups, resources and implementation capacity fall below the premises, metrics systematically conflict with field conditions, local appeals cannot be written back, or rollback capability is insufficient.

Scope of domain evidence and downgrading for gaps (substantial coverage). The rules cover blind spots, applicability switches, stress tests, and intervention priorities. Unified data, case registration, causal identification, representativeness, and methods of governance across domains still need to be established for the particular object. Without stratified samples, evidence of resource capacity, the execution chain, or evidence of rollback, do not extrapolate from the local to the aggregate, infer execution from intent, or propose a blanket option. Downgrade the output to areas where judgment remains open, a plan to obtain more evidence, and proposals for small-scale reversible trials.

15.4Minimum Output for an Application Record ​

Each application must retain at least the module ID, object, scale, K, and time window. It must record every supported proposition and its external evidence, coverage status and effects of gaps, material and tool inputs, domain risks, and affected, low-power, weak-signal, and unable-to-exit positions. It must include the protection floor, diagnosis and action ceiling, and how evidence gaps lower confidence and the level of judgment. Where a choice is needed, retain normative choice or a comparison with no action. Where intervention is needed, retain the candidate T level. Give reasons for inapplicable items. If proceeding to real-world action, attach the complete 17-field choice record wrapper, structured selected_action, C12, explicit N premises, O1–O4, the single atomic J authorization, stopping, anonymous appeals or appeals through a representative, anti-retaliation monitoring, rollback, and the execution state log.

In all module records, including those used only for diagnosis, references to evidence, positions, channels, and responsible parties must resolve to the runtime registry. When context is omitted, summary placeholders such as GHOST, none, self, or self:* must not substitute for external records. The parties responsible for appeal, independent review, and rollback must be distinct from the deciding subject.

An output missing these fields can serve only as a reading aid and cannot proceed to real-world action. An application proposed for real-world intervention must retain a bounded chain of action that can be changed by counterexamples, input from safe positions, and real-world consequences. A task limited to explanation or diagnosis ends at its corresponding evidential boundary.

15.5Starting Dynamic Analysis in the Application Modules ​

When dynamic analysis is needed, all nine application modules must additionally declare the main actor, primary circle, neighboring circles, relation types, M/Ψ conditions, five kinds of clock, event, simple baseline, and outcome write-back. The relationship and family modules focus on role activation, inability to exit, and slow, intermediate-timescale, and fast states. The organization and platform modules focus on metrics, bridging, propagation, and institutional write-back. The public governance, public opinion, and ultra-large-scale modules focus on representation, positions with low visibility, losses in aggregation across levels, and calibration drift. The space and emergency modules focus on material channels, capacity, thresholds, delay, and emergency authorization.

No module may bypass an event’s factual status because “the situation is urgent,” or bypass privacy and protection of people affected because “the model is only making a suggestion.” An emergency permits only minimum protective action under external emergency rules, with review, appeal, and rollback restored as soon as possible. Scenarios generated by the model retain hypothetical or simulated status and must not be written back as observed.

If an application only needs explanation, do not require multi-circle foresight. If a simple baseline is already sufficient, retain a simpler module record. If foresight lacks registration or calibration, the output stops at path prompts. If authorization is incomplete, stop real-world execution and retain requirements for action, analytical recommendations that confer no execution permission, and drafts of institutions and plans. The existence of a complex workflow must not create pressure to take real-world action.
