# Records, learning, and additional historical information

Past experience can change present skills, rules, resources, or relationships. Those **current carriers** can retain the change and affect later work. The historical-increment question is narrower: does history add information or an effect **beyond the explicitly stated current representation**?

## Why no extra increment need not mean no learning

Consider the book’s relation H → S → Y: earlier experience H forms current skill S, which affects a later outcome Y. If S already captures the relevant information, adding H may not improve prediction of Y. That finding limits the additional historical increment conditional on S. It does not erase the formation of S or the effect of skill on Y.

The reverse inference is invalid too. Failure to support a historical increment does not by itself establish that a carrier formed or that learning occurred. Carrier-based learning needs its own evidence: a locatable update, retention or renewal, actual later use, a specified task comparison, and appropriate causal support. A training certificate, archive, single successful attempt, or new rule cannot substitute for that chain.

Do not deliberately omit a known current carrier to manufacture a “historical” improvement. A limited representation may genuinely leave historical predictive information available, but the result belongs to that representation, target, and window.

## What the case evidence preserves

In the [London taxi-trainee study](../cases/london-taxi-learning), both qualified and unqualified trainees outperformed controls on a London-landmark task at follow-up. Significant posterior hippocampal gray-matter changes appeared only in qualified trainees. Qualification, task performance, and imaging are distinct outcomes; the study did not test an extra historical increment after conditioning on an adequate present state.

In [Flint’s follow-up audit](../cases/flint-water-feedback), a new hotline procedure was documented and accepted as responsive to a recommendation. That is an actual rule change. The audit did not supply repeated tip-level outcomes establishing this framework’s narrower learning mechanism or a net improvement in drinking-water safety.

## A useful reading check

Ask four questions separately: What formed? Where was it retained? Was it used later? What changed in the later task? Ask the additional historical-increment question only when the claim actually concerns information or effects beyond the stated current representation.

Read [§4.5](/en/theory/part-04#section-4-5), [§6.3](/en/theory/part-06#section-6-3), and [C.11](/en/theory/appendix-c#section-c-11). Domain evidence can support ordinary learning without first being formally determined, under this framework, to be a case of feedback-mediated learning.
