# Formation, maintenance, recovery, and replication are different outcomes

**Evidence that an arrangement started does not settle whether it lasts, recovers, or can be replicated elsewhere.**

Formation concerns how an arrangement comes into being. Maintenance concerns what keeps it operating. Recovery concerns a specified function or identity after disruption. Replication concerns the result under a declared new realization or setting. Each needs its own target, conditions, window, and evidence.

A successful launch may rely on unusual founding resources. Routine operation may depend on maintenance labor that the launch record never measured. A recovery may meet a limited function while changing components. Replication may change the treatment itself when scale, implementation, or the object changes.

When replication fails, distinguish failure of the scheme itself, including the possibility that the original result was not caused by the scheme; deviation from the implementation specified beforehand; absence of a previously stated enabling condition; and a scale change that alters the treatment or object. Both implementation content and enabling conditions must be written before results and independently checkable. Inventing a missing condition after every failure shields the claim from correction.

An implementation deviation or missing enabling condition suggested only after failure remains a candidate for a later test. It does not revise the present result record. Whether an arrangement should be maintained, recovered, or replicated is also a separate normative judgment.

## Keep these judgments separate

| A new result | A wider mechanism claim |
| --- | --- |
| Evidence for the specific formation, maintenance, recovery, or replication task. | Requires the evidence and conditions of that additional task. |

## Teaching example: a pilot works, a larger program does not

Suppose a pilot uses a declared staffing ratio. A later rollout uses a different ratio and process. Record the actual implementation change; do not call it an identical treatment or invent an unrecorded condition after failure.

**Assumptions:** Hypothetical comparison; the original conditions and actual difference are available.

**Boundary:** The distinction alone does not identify which mechanism caused the failure.

## Check your reading

**Can a failed replication be excused by any condition named afterward?**

No. A relevant enabling condition must have been stated beforehand and be independently checkable; other explanations remain candidates requiring evidence.

## Limits

- Formation does not prove maintenance, and recovery does not prove unchanged identity.

- Retain successes within their supported task even when another outcome fails.
