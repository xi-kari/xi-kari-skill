# D.14 Social Theory, Power, Communication, and Institutional Reproduction {#section-d-14}

In the main text, see §3.3, §7.6, §10–11, §14.5, and §16.2. Research entries cover 14.01–14.09.

Formation, maintenance, function, social recognition, legal validity, and normative justification are distinct questions. Functionalist accounts of consequences, the meanings of action in interpretive sociology, the order of encounters in interactionism, temporal processes of structuration and morphogenesis, studies of power and knowledge, public communication, social systems theory, and actor–network theory cannot all be reduced to one measure of “structural strength.”

Visible compliance can arise from endorsement, habit, dependence on income, threats, or lack of alternatives. The same behavior does not suffice to identify a motive. Understanding a message does not amount to agreement. Establishing a legal rationale in writing does not mean services have reached people. The causal effects of objects and technologies do not make them bear human moral responsibility. Rules can constrain action and can also make coordination and claims to rights possible.

Criticism must trace concrete channels of classification, training, recordkeeping, rewards and penalties, and decision-making authority. Participation can correct mistakes or increase expression without changing permissions. A reasoned refusal of a request through an ordinary procedure can still have value for respect and knowledge. For adoption within the framework, GOV-02 requires each item to be addressed with reasons, independent review where needed, and verification of actual changes. Token acceptance cannot establish `applied`. The continued presence of objections must not invariably be treated as a permanent veto.

In a teaching example, an appeal is heard and receives a reasoned response. The original decision stands because it has sufficient evidence. This can establish that a response actually took place, but does not establish that the applicant’s request was granted. Another appeal actually changes rules and resources. The change alone does not establish that the distribution is normatively justified. Record causal feedback, ordinary participation, and authorization to adopt a new version within the framework at three separate levels.

Institutions can be realized repeatedly through skilled practice, budgets, tools, informal commitments, and personnel arrangements. They need not rewrite their charters each time. Reorganization of actual processes while documents stay the same, and changes to documents while execution stays the same, both have mechanisms that can be studied. Upholding a correct decision after review and leaving a decision in place without genuine review can look the same. They remain distinguishable by reasons, handling of material, and authority. The rate of changes cannot substitute for judging quality.

Research topics. 14.01 Social facts and functionalism (Durkheim, Parsons, and Merton). 14.02 Interpretive sociology (Weber). 14.03 Symbolic interactionism and everyday order (Mead, Blumer, and Goffman). 14.04 Structuration, morphogenesis, and agency (Giddens and Archer). 14.05 Power, knowledge, discipline, and governmentality (Foucault). 14.06 Critical theory and public communication (the Frankfurt School and Habermas). 14.07 Social systems theory (Luhmann). 14.08 Conflict, integration, and institutional reproduction. 14.09 Actor–network theory and nonhuman positions of action (Latour).
