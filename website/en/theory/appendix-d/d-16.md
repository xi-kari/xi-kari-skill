# D.16 Organizations, Work, Leadership, and Labor Protection {#section-d-16}

In the main text, see §6.3–6.5, §7.6, §8, and §15.3.3. Research entries cover 16.01–16.09.

Formation, growth, differentiation, exploration and exploitation, resource dependence, institutionalization, and exit in organizations need to be connected to tasks, knowledge, resources, permissions, and actual chains of work. Organizational life-cycle models can raise questions about conditions at different stages. They cannot put all organizations into a necessary sequence. Legal entities, work networks, knowledge, and services need not begin or end together.

For organizational learning, distinguish plan formation, record creation, actual use, durable change, and results. An unfinished test of one branch of feedback-mediated learning does not cancel a process of learning in general that can already be traced. A training session or adoption into institutional rules does not automatically show that later tasks improved. Isomorphic rules can support coordination and protection. Decoupling can occur in texts, implementation, or outcomes, and does not always amount to deception.

Professional and informal networks can make up for gaps in formal procedures or exclude people without connections. The role of leadership in an organization should be traced through agendas, resources, interpretation, authorization, and correction. Individual traits cannot account for every success or failure. Evaluation of labor must distinguish working hours, pace, skills, income, health, hidden maintenance, and care. Output cannot stand in for the quality of the work as a whole.

When comparing working-time data, check statutory limits, overtime pay thresholds, schedules, and hours actually worked separately for the populations, units of time, stages of implementation, and exceptions that apply.

Worker codetermination must apply to specific matters. Distinguish raising an issue, obtaining information, consultation, bargaining, joint agreement, adjudicating disputes, and making rules autonomously within an organization. Workers can develop their own mutual aid and working rules. Authority to change an employer’s or platform’s rules must be checked separately. Members, owners, employees, and those represented need not be the same people. One vote cannot substitute for nomination, accountability, and internal dissent.

In a teaching example, output recovers after a change in leadership, but a few people still fill the gaps by working overtime. Higher output is a real result. Sustainability, distribution, and continuity still need to be assessed. Exit, splitting, merger, and collective bargaining require checks on contracts, knowledge, the scope of representation, actual implementation, and anti-retaliation. Eligibility under the framework’s analysis must not be made a condition for workers to organize or appeal.

Failures of organizational memory can occur at different points in storage, retrieval, awareness, handling, and consequences. If old information has been retrieved but does not enter decision-making or action, calling the problem “forgetting” will miss an organizational blockage. The shock caused by the departure of a person in charge can identify a specific dependency. It cannot on its own describe the world in which that person was never there. Organizational persistence, the capability to do the work, procedural improvements, and workers’ living conditions are separate outcomes. Evidence for one does not establish the others.

Research topics. 16.01 Organizational life cycles (Greiner and Adizes). 16.02 Exploration and exploitation, and organizational learning (March, Argyris, and Schön). 16.03 Resource dependence and institutionalization (Pfeffer, Salancik, and Selznick). 16.04 Institutional isomorphism, decoupling, and organizational legitimacy (Meyer, Rowan, DiMaggio, and Powell). 16.05 Bureaucracy, professional organizations, and informal networks. 16.06 Leadership, entrepreneurship, and collective generation. 16.07 Labor processes, metrics, incentives, and burnout. 16.08 Exit, splitting, merger, and organizational memory. 16.09 Worker organizing, collective bargaining, and labor protection.
