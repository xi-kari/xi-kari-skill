# D.12 Religion, Comparisons Across Civilizations, and Relational Knowledge {#section-d-12}

In the main text, see §2.7, §5.10, §7, §10, and §16.15. Research entries cover 12.01–12.13.

Greek philosophy, Judaism and Christianity, Islamic thought, South Asian traditions, Buddhism across cultures, secularization, African thought, Indigenous knowledge, Latin American liberation thought, and Southeast Asian and Pacific experience must not be treated as civilizations with a single will. Preserve textual doctrines, institutional rules, personal beliefs, state power, reform movements, and practices of everyday life separately.

Relational knowledge can include detailed environmental observation, skills, and arrangements for responsibility. Religious and philosophical arguments can also contribute understanding and normative value of their own. A skill can genuinely work without researchers first identifying every mechanism. Its success does not establish every cosmological explanation associated with it. Moral, political, and empirical claims are subject to criticism on their own terms.

Being able to locate knowledge, being able to make it public, having permission to reuse it, and having authority to represent its source community are distinct qualifications. Non-disclosure for protection does not mean the knowledge is absent. Understanding a term does not give an outside researcher permission to publish sensitive material or make decisions for the community. A translation may suffice for one task while leaving some parts unaligned for another.

In a teaching example, a community’s navigational practices reliably help with a kind of task, and an outside model also explains some of the trajectories. Both sides can exchange knowledge and examine their own errors. Explicit equations do not give the outside model authority over decisions in daily life. A cultural identity does not exempt the local method from concrete checks on safety and effectiveness. Local institutional reform can coexist with continuing exclusion. Comparison across civilizations must not be turned into an overall ranking.

Comparability across periods depends on the particular item and use. Changing the wording or mode of one survey item can limit its direct comparison while leaving other trends that have a basis for bridging. Following up on the same random assignment adds information over time, without increasing the number of independent trials. A statistically significant result in one period and a nonsignificant one in another cannot substitute for a test of the difference between periods. Limited improvements in skills or services can be real, while control over one’s own agenda, the ability to refuse, and access to remedy still require separate evaluation.

Research topics. 12.01 Greek philosophy (Plato, Aristotle, and the Stoic tradition). 12.02 Jewish and Christian traditions. 12.03 Islamic thought and political community. 12.04 Indian thought (Buddhism, Hinduism, Jainism, and others). 12.05 The spread of Buddhism across cultures. 12.06 Secularization, religious reform, and religious revival. 12.07 African thought and traditions such as Ubuntu. 12.08 Indigenous and relational knowledge. 12.09 Latin American liberation thought and decolonial criticism. 12.10 Comparisons across civilizations and points of nonalignment in translation. 12.11 Caste, social hierarchy, and egalitarian reform in South Asia. 12.12 The autonomous histories and intellectual practices of Southeast Asian societies. 12.13 Pacific island societies, navigation, and relational knowledge.
