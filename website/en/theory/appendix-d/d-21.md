# D.21 Law, Authorization, Procedure, and Responsibility {#section-d-21}

In the main text, see §2.4.3, §5.5.5 and 5.5.7, §11.3, §13.5, §14, and §15.2. Research entries cover 21.01–21.08.

Distinguish the validity of a source of law, its application to an individual case, moral reasons, actual authority, procedures for remedy, and what reaches people in practice. Natural law, positive law, legal realism, constitutionalism, and research on legal pluralism help analyze these questions in different ways. The existence of an institutional text does not establish implementation. A rule’s legal status does not automatically determine its normative evaluation.

Consent, authorization, agency, representation, and the right to bring a claim are distinct. Some public or statutory procedures do not depend on individual private mandates. This does not establish consent by those concerned. Personal consent also cannot substitute for the public authority an institution requires. A model’s recommendation, actual implementation, and later confirmation must be recorded separately. The fact that an observed act was unauthorized is no reason to erase it from the record.

Hearing people, respecting procedure, correcting evidence, changing a decision, paying compensation, and revising rules can occur at different times. A remedy in one case does not establish that the institution has been corrected throughout. Upholding a decision also does not necessarily mean that no one was heard. Punishment, prevention, compensation, truth, nonviolent coexistence, and forgiveness are distinct and cannot offset one another. The intervention level “Carriers of Safety and Trust” (§15.2.1) prohibits forced reconciliation. It does not rule out all lawful evidence gathering.

In a teaching example, an accurate annual income figure does not suffice to determine income in each short benefit window. Correct arithmetic cannot make up for a gap in timing information that matters under the law. Responsibilities for obtaining additional evidence, defaults in cases of absence, and suspension of implementation require specific rules. Organizational conditions can explain harm without automatically absolving individuals. An individual error also does not erase the role of upstream design, resources, and management.

Authority can pass lawfully between steps of a composite procedure. Each individual authorization must still be valid at the step where it applies. Checking each authorization separately does not mean that procedures can never connect. Several invalid fragments also cannot be assembled into valid authorization. Explanation, correcting source data, notification, changing a decision, compensation, and the person concerned receiving it freely are distinct endpoints. If the original record is reliable but the time conversion is wrong, correct the faulty inference and the decisions derived from it. There is no need to delete all reliable material.

Research topics. 21.01 Natural law, positive law, and legal realism. 21.02 Constitutionalism, separation of powers, and institutional checks and balances. 21.03 Rights, authorization, consent, and representation. 21.04 Procedural justice, evidence, and appeals. 21.05 Punishment, prevention, repair, and transitional justice. 21.06 Attribution of responsibility and organizational harm. 21.07 Legal pluralism, customary law, and transnational rules. 21.08 Algorithmic decisions, digital rights, and appealability.
