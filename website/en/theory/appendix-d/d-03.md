# D.03 Research Design, Measurement, Causation, and How Records Are Produced {#section-d-03}

In the main text, see §2.4–2.5, §4.1–4.6, §5.5.1, and §12.3–12.7. Research entries cover 03.01–03.09.

Randomized experiments, natural experiments, observational studies, process tracing, comparative history, and oral and archaeological materials each address some problems of identification. First specify the treatment, comparison object, time, outcome, and unit. The average effect for people invited, the change among actual participants, the effect of the policy as a whole, and one person’s outcome cannot stand in for one another merely because they share a program name.

Identification of a total effect does not require every mediator to have been identified. An account of a mechanism does not guarantee that there is a total effect. Controlling for the current state sometimes removes common causes, but it can also block a channel that formed over time or introduce selection bias. When H acts through S to affect Y and, given S, H adds no information, report the total effect and conditional residual separately. Do not take this to mean that history had no effect or that G3 is automatically satisfied.

Measurement must distinguish the object, observational indicators, constructs, and classification rules. Indicators that reflect a latent state and components that jointly define an index cannot use the same argument for validity. Changes in scale, denominator, measurement method, or rules for entering the sample can all change the trend. Better indicators can result from genuine improvement, narrow training, sample selection, or manipulation of records. Check these causes separately. The direction of the change does not settle its cause.

In a teaching example, the approval rate rises after an application process is simplified. That may indicate fewer procedural barriers. To judge whether people’s living conditions are better secured, also examine benefits actually delivered, service quality, and people who did not apply. Absence of objections in an archive has force as counterevidence only if relevant objections would have been recorded reliably and the records were preserved and accessible. The dates, uses, ownership, and actual power relations associated with material remains likewise cannot all be determined from one find.

Observation records are produced jointly by the object and the processes of collection and reporting. Fewer publicly recorded positive cases may result from lower actual risk, a response preventing outcomes, or a lower detection rate. If the available material is compatible with these processes, retain several explanations. Positive average effects on two segments in the same population do not suffice to establish a positive effect for the whole chain. The two segments may work for different people. Identify total effects, particular mediators, joint responses, and reporting mechanisms separately for the question at hand.
