# D.06 Human Nature, Evolution, Habits, and the Capability to Act {#section-d-06}

In the main text, see §7, §8.1, §9, and §14.2–14.3. Research entries cover 06.01–06.08.

Present function, historical adaptation, drift, cultural transmission, individual intent, and consequences for welfare answer different questions. A behavior that is beneficial now was not necessarily produced initially for that function. Retention of a cultural form does not automatically show that every member endorses it. Kinship, reciprocity, reputation, partner choice, and institutional constraints can each produce cooperation. Specify who observes, who responds, and who bears the cost.

Personality differences can appear as conditionally stable tendencies. Slow change does not establish that a tendency is inherited, fixed, or the person’s essence. Repeated practice, embodied skills, opportunities in the environment, and contextual cues jointly shape behavior. Looking at the particular situation first can be an order of investigation. Comparisons of personality, situation, and their interaction must use comparable information and evaluation conditions. Evidence across situations and time still has to be distinguished from selection into the settings and the methods of measurement.

Action under constraints can still be a decision made by an actor. This does not make it freely given consent. A judgment should separate actual behavior, information available at the time, local control, available options for refusal, consequences, and attribution of responsibility. In a teaching example, an employee signs a record under a credible threat of punishment. The employee did in fact sign it. The people who designed the organization, the supervisor, and those who benefit may have distinct responsibilities. Saying “it was their own choice” cannot erase the chain of control.

High costs do not automatically establish sincerity, love, or identification. Goodwill at low cost also does not lose its value because it fails to meet the conditions of the human variable “Open-Ended Undertaking Action” (§7.3.11). Practice can incorporate a skill into the current state. Task performance and evidence of how the skill formed can support a claim of learning in general. Whether there is also a historical increment beyond the current representation is a separate question for the historical-increment test (§4.5), whose conditions are narrower. Neither natural selection nor filtering by social systems directly establishes what ought to be done.

The explanatory contributions of situation, personality, and their interaction may overlap. A comparison must not assign the overlap entirely to one and then take a zero remainder for the other as proof that it does not exist. Behavioral differences produced by a treatment must be explained with reference to the entire set of conditions manipulated. Do not relabel them as the independent effect of one component without grounds. Whether someone under constraints actually acted, could safely refuse, validly consented, and bears a particular responsibility are still four distinct judgments.

Research topics. 06.01 Theories of human nature, including *xing shan* 性善 (human nature as good) and *xing e* 性恶 (human nature as bad), and situationism. 06.02 Natural selection, adaptation, and drift. 06.03 Cultural evolution and dual inheritance (Robert Boyd, Peter Richerson, and others). 06.04 Evolution of cooperation, kinship, and reciprocity. 06.05 Personality differences and interaction with situations. 06.06 Intent, freedom, responsibility, and the capability to act. 06.07 Habits, embodied practice, and shaping by the environment. 06.08 Action beyond incentives and resistance to reductionism.
