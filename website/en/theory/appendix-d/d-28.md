# D.28 Technology, Infrastructure, Platforms, and Artificial Intelligence {#section-d-28}

In the main text, see §10–11, §13.8–13.11, §15.3.3–15.3.5, and §16.23–16.24. Research entries cover 28.01–28.08.

The material properties of technology, protocols, deployment configurations, institutions, maintenance, and actual use jointly determine specific outcomes. This does not mean that technology and society each account for half. Bandwidth, materials, and interfaces can each impose constraints in their own right. Default configurations and commercial arrangements can also change who is reached. Evaluate compliance with written standards, interoperability between implementations, consistency of business semantics, reliability under stress, and whether people can actually use the technology at their respective levels.

Maintenance depends on spare parts whose quality can be verified, skills, budgets, permissions, and ongoing labor. Several sets of equipment may share one upstream power supply or the same defective software. Numbers alone do not guarantee independent redundancy. Platform matching and flexibility in working hours can bring real benefits. Pricing, eligibility, future opportunities, and appeals can also be centrally controlled. The presence of an algorithm cannot by itself determine all aspects of legal employment status.

For AI, distinguish the attribution of generated content, support in the source text, external facts, and eligibility for the intended use. Retrieval helps obtain material and provides no guarantee of its truth. Internal consistency can help detect one class of errors and can also reproduce errors reliably. Suggestions, tool requests, execution of a procedure, changes to external state, and consequences are five distinct stages. Human oversight requires access to the relevant material, capability, time to review beforehand, and a manageable workload. A rejection signal must actually reach the actuator.

Teaching example. A user clicks Reject after the task in the backend has become irrevocable. Approval in name only does not provide a genuine veto. For privacy, distinguish collection, inference, reuse, retention, and downstream deletion. Issuing a deletion instruction does not establish that deletion has been completed. Assess actual possession of data, permitted use, recognition for accounting purposes, transactions, and eligibility to supply the data across borders separately. A lack of recognition as an asset for accounting purposes does not imply a lack of use or value (see Ministry of Finance, 2023). It also does not lift applicable restrictions on use. When studying technological transitions, track tasks, independent skills, career opportunities, and meaning separately. Tool proficiency and occupational exposure do not stand in for long-term outcomes. Before removing an older support that still serves a necessary function, check the availability of a substitute and the consequences of disruption. Stopping activity and taking protective measures in the face of immediate danger are handled separately under the applicable procedures.

Comparisons of technological necessity must specify what is held fixed. This could be the product name, the binary, the configuration, or the complete deployment including training, permissions, and maintenance. If the later items in that list change, the comparison can no longer be described as a change in institutions alone. The search for alternatives must also hold the task, resources, time limit, and necessary protections constant. Assess attribution of AI-generated content, the existence of bibliographic entries, metadata, source-text support, facts about the world, and permission for the use in question individually. A limited reanalysis or a recalculation using synthetic data can correct a technical quantity. It cannot be represented as evidence that actual users have experienced the corresponding improvement.
