# D.32 War, Revolution, Empire, Development, and Historical Paths {#section-d-32}

In the main text, see §2.4–2.7, §8.1, §10–12, §14, and §16.4. Research entries cover 32.01–32.08.

Identify the state as a legal entity, the government, the military, organizations, and the populations that bear the consequences separately. Realism, institutionalism, and constructivism focus on resource security, the monitoring of commitments, and constituted status. These concerns can support different explanations of the conditions at work. Do not combine them into a single will of the state. Defensive intent, actual effects on security, the other side’s assessment, future capability to honor commitments, and normative justification are also distinct. Communication can reduce a type of uncertainty about information. It does not automatically resolve future incentives or enforcement.

In mobilization, distinguish initial participation, repeated participation, sustained organizing, obtaining concessions, transfer of power, and subsequent living conditions. Supporting without signing up, signing up without taking part, dropping out, and continuing to participate are distinct positions. A later victory does not establish that the outcome was inevitable at the time. Analyses of empire and decolonization retain separate channels for trade, conquest, cultural exchange, and unequal dependence. Formal sovereignty, administrative capability, economic choices, and citizenship rights cannot serve as evidence for one another. Histories centered on leaders cannot stand in for local actors or internal differences.

For historical dynamics, retain Khaldun’s account of cooperation and the reproduction of ruling coalitions, Toynbee’s account of responses to conditions, Tainter’s question about the costs of complexity, and Turchin’s feedback hypothesis. These do not add up to a unified civilizational cycle. Diminishing marginal returns do not imply negative marginal returns, much less negative net returns or imminent collapse. The cost of maintaining a complex model must also be kept distinct from the costs of actual social organization. Retain separate versions for the original prediction, later models, retrospective assessments by the authors, and independent tests.

Modernization, industrialization, and global connections are distinct from one another and do not make up a single development ladder. Actual effects on industry cannot stand in for outcomes for workers, caregivers, or people who cannot move. Peace encompasses at least the cessation of violence, procedures for handling disputes, political arrangements, responsibility, recovery of living conditions, and reconciliation. Cooperation between groups does not establish individual psychological recovery. In a counterfactual, limit changes to nodes for which there is evidence and reassess downstream effects. Retain the conditional roles of historical figures, ordinary people, and implementation at the end of the chain. Do not write the counterfactual as another history whose course is already known.

When comparing historical quantities, first check the target and the range of common support. Preserve disagreements that remain. The total number of violent deaths and the violence-related component of excess deaths overlap substantially and are not identical. Renaming them cannot remove the gap, and the intervals from different studies cannot be stitched together into a common confidence interval. Diagnostic completeness, assumptions used to correct for underreporting in practice, and adjustments for missed interviews belong to different steps. Signing, approval, funding commitments, receipt, settlement, and consequences for people’s lives each require their own evidence. An accurate answer about the documentary record can stand before the overall causal share has been established.
