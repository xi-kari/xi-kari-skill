# Part 2 Boundaries and Methods {#part-title-2}

The ultimate object domain of this framework is the broadly conceived empirical structural world. Natural processes, technical systems, ecological processes, human actions, organizations, and institutions can all enter structural explanation if they can leave comparable materials and allow researchers to state candidate objects, states, relations or transitions, interfaces, time windows, scale profiles, and identity criteria. The human structured world is not a synonym for the world as a whole. It is a specialized domain within that world, requiring additional human-domain clauses, normative commitments, and authorization procedures. Purely metaphysical assertions, objects for which no empirical elements can be given, and “systems” established solely through naming or narrative coherence do not enter empirical judgment. They may be discussed, but they must not borrow this framework's levels of empirical conclusions.

The current scope of application must still be stated separately each time: the object; the scale, time window, and identity criterion; the region or environment; the data window; the extrapolation unit; and exclusions. That an object belongs to the broadly conceived empirical structural world means only that it can in principle be defined. It does not imply that any conclusion can be directly generalized across windows, objects, or domains.

This choice of scope also sets the framework's limits. The framework seeks to explain how objects form identifiable structures, how they operate, how they change or undergo dissolution under the effects of conditions, and how descriptions at different scales can be converted. It does not presuppose a unified purpose for the world, project human intentions onto nonhuman processes, or infer “how one should choose” directly from “what is.” Real-world choices are taken up separately by normative premises and operational procedures.

## 2.1 Two-Stage Object Admission {#section-2-1}

Structural modeling begins with candidate objects. It does not presuppose that a gain from the grouping has already been supported, nor does it let the definition of a candidate object substitute for grounds for object facts. When the support of a formal test is sought within this framework, object admission has two stages whose order must not be reversed. This procedure governs strong empirical claims: claims made in the name of the four empirical questions (§4.3–4.6), and the claims of the conclusion rules (§4.10) and conditional mechanisms (Part 6) that depend on them. It is not a universal truth check that all domain knowledge, historical records, or everyday understanding must pass.

The first stage is defining the candidate object. The analyst sets out the candidate object clearly in terms of the seven elements of a structural object (§3.1): boundary, state variables, relations or transition constraints, interface, time window, scale profile, and identity criterion, written S*=&lt;B,X,R,I,T,SP,K&gt;. The asterisk indicates that the object remains a candidate in the analysis. Having all seven elements establishes only that the question has been stated clearly. It does not establish that the grouping has relatively independent empirical standing in the world. When elements are missing, only exploratory questions may be proposed.

The second stage is the formal test. If the analyst claims a grouping gain, a channel effect, a historical conditional increment beyond the current representation, or scale closure in the name of the four empirical questions, and applies for eligibility for formal support within this framework, a corresponding specific test must be established for the claim, and its pre-registration must be completed before the results are viewed. Pre-registration means setting down the target, criteria, and rules before seeing the results. The items to be fixed in advance here are the following (§4.1 explains each): the null model, that is, a comparison model without the claimed factor; the subtype, of which only one is chosen; the success criterion, of which only one is chosen from those the subtype admits; controls; models; samples and extrapolation units; evaluation metrics; the positive threshold; the decision rule; the null-conclusion rule; falsification conditions; and suspension conditions. When a test has multiple targets or multiple models, the primary target or aggregation rule, the rule for model selection or ensembling, and the rules for multiple comparisons also need to be fixed. Once fixed, these items must not be changed, and a record that can be checked needs to show that they were indeed fixed before the results were seen; otherwise, changes made afterward could not be verified. This support eligibility belongs to the specific test that passed, not to the definition of the candidate object, nor to the four empirical questions in the abstract.

Support eligibility requires three pairs to be kept apart: exploratory and confirmatory work, positive results and null conclusions, and completion as finalized in advance and post hoc deviation. Only a confirmatory or replication test that was finalized before the evidence was seen and completed as finalized can provide positive support; a falsification test supports only the pre-registered falsification judgment or null-model judgment. An exploratory test, a test whose pre-registration is still a draft, a test that deviated from its pre-registration after the evidence was seen, and a test with a deviation that was not disclosed can only be used to propose candidates, record deviations, or keep matters unresolved. They must not use the eligibility of a formal test to support branches of conclusion rules that require that eligibility. Failure to pass the positive criterion does not automatically support the null model either: unless equivalence or sufficiency, statistical power or sensitivity, and tolerance have been pre-registered and passed, the conclusion can only be “not positively supported” or “unresolved.” Thus, “the candidate object has been defined,” “the positive empirical claim is not supported,” “the null model is supported,” and “a test receives limited positive support” must remain distinct states.

The two stages are meant to prevent two errors that run in opposite directions. One is treating the analyst's naming, clustering, or choice of boundary as meaning that a grouping gain or object facts have already been supported. The other is piecing scattered materials together after the fact into an unfalsifiable “system” before the object has even been defined.

### 2.1.1 Domain Evidence and Eligibility for Formal Tests {#section-2-1-1}

Existing studies, archives, oral accounts, archaeological materials, and locatable source texts can support the relevant descriptions, explanations, or limited causal conclusions according to their actual research designs. When these materials are used, their status should be preserved accurately: whether they are pre-registered confirmatory studies, post hoc findings, exploratory work, historical reconstruction, or other research. A pre-registration from before the results must not be fabricated for them after the fact. These materials cannot thereby acquire the support of a formal test automatically, nor do they lose their originally supported content merely because they have not yet been converted into a formal test.

If an existing finding is to be used in a new formal test, it may first serve as a source of questions and models, then be tested using independent materials, a later window, or an appropriate replication design. Data used for exploratory work must be separated from data used for confirmatory work, and model selection must likewise be separated from final evaluation. Where no feasible new test is available, retain the domain conclusion and the relation awaiting testing; do not fabricate confirmation.

Formal proofs hold under their axioms, semantics, and rules of derivation. Textual interpretation is open to criticism on the basis of editions, contexts, and argumentative materials. Normative claims disclose their value premises and reasons. They can contribute to analysis, but must not masquerade as one another's empirical mechanisms, factual truth, or real-world authorization.

### 2.1.2 Connecting Formal Conclusions and Empirical Cases {#section-2-1-2}

Formal propositions first specify the state domain, mapping, target, admissible operations, and logical premises; proof then establishes their conclusions. Exhaustive enumeration of a complete finite domain can test every case within that domain. Numerical computation must also specify precision, how boundaries are determined, and implementation error. Proof, exhaustive enumeration, and numerical checks can cross-check one another, but together they do not generate real-world samples, and more computations do not increase independent empirical support.

Applying a formal conclusion to a real-world object requires a separate account of how real-world quantities correspond to model variables, which premises are already supported by materials, whether the errors and applicable ranges are acceptable, and what counterexamples would invalidate this correspondence. Two things can be acknowledged together: the proposition holds in the given model, and whether the real-world object satisfies the model remains undetermined. A mathematical counterexample is already sufficient to refute the unconditional inference it addresses. The absence of a completed real-world experiment does not erase this formal answer. Conversely, formal feasibility, the existence of an algorithm, computability within budget, timely availability of data, correct execution in practice, and authority to adopt are six judgments, each requiring its own basis.

</div>

## 2.2 Proposition Roles and the Burden of Argument {#section-2-2}

Formal judgments first distinguish the roles of propositions. Six roles are commonly used: definitions, empirical questions, epistemic constraints, human-domain clauses, normative commitments, and operational procedures; conditional formal propositions provable within a model count as one more. If a single sentence of everyday language mixes definitional, empirical, formal, methodological, or normative commitments, it must be separated into its components, with the basis for each stated separately. The identifiers serve to distinguish burdens of argument; they do not mean that all kinds of proposition have the same truth conditions.

Definitions (written D). These specify how terms are used within the framework; they are not empirical facts about the world. Definitions can make judgments consistent, but cannot by themselves establish the existence of objects, mechanisms, or historical effects.

Empirical questions (written G). There are four, concerning grouping gain, channel effect, historical conditional increment beyond the current representation, and scale closure, respectively (§4.3–4.6). Each specific test must take the form of a factual assertion with an object, target, conditions, comparison, and mode of failure—for example, whether a specified difference exceeds a threshold. Whether a fact is true and whether evidence is sufficient to support it are two different questions. Pre-registration, review, and release rules govern eligibility for formal support; they do not stipulate whether an effect exists in the world.

Epistemic constraints (written E). These specify the valid scope of observation, measurement, inference, and cross-scale transfer, requiring that blind spots, competing explanations, and residuals be retained.

Human-domain clauses (written H). There are six (§7.1), and their roles differ. “Meaning and Coordination,” “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity,” and “Historical Trace Carriers” are empirical hypotheses requiring case-specific evidence; “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility” is an operational classification rule; “Institutional Write-Back” is a classification rule, where write-back means that feedback changes rules, records, resources, or roles, and the change is actually carried out; and “Open-Ended Undertaking” is a normative boundary. “Human-domain clause” is a marker showing that these clauses adapt the framework to the human domain; it is not another logical type mutually exclusive with definition, empirical content, or normative content. These clauses must not be treated as an unclassified set of “axioms of human nature,” nor, in reverse, be written as universal empirical questions for the nonhuman world.

Normative commitments (written N). These explicitly declare what the framework chooses to protect and constrain. They must not be disguised as factual propositions that empirical data could prove.

Operational procedures (written O). These specify how the following steps are carried out: fixing descriptions, checking evidence, stating normative premises, authorization, comparison of options, constrained execution, review, stopping, withdrawal, and remedy.

Conditional formal propositions (written F). Conclusions are established by proof under explicit state domains, mappings, targets, operation sets, and mathematical assumptions. Formal counterexamples can refute universal inferences that exceed those conditions. Whether real-world objects satisfy the premises still requires independent evidence. For the five propositions “Task Recovery,” “Closure under Dynamics and Admissible Interventions,” “Common Feasible Actions under Information Constraints,” “Task Preservation through Successive Conversions,” and “Error Propagation under Approximate Closure,” see §4.18; for task families and dynamic refinement, see §4.19.

Conclusion rules (written C) are not an eighth kind of fact. They delimit how a result may be used and combine the mechanism conditions it requires; among them, “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1) belongs to normative and procedural bridging. The three common method constraints (§4.17) govern how representations, evidence, and inference are used. They do not serve as additional laws of the world.

Descriptive conclusions cannot generate normative conclusions on their own, nor can explanation or diagnosis[^tn-diagnosis-02] generate authority to act on their own. The minimal bridge that real-world action requires can be written as:

A descriptive or explanatory conclusion + explicit normative premises + current evidence + lawful authorization + an action ceiling + conditions for stopping or withdrawal

If any element is missing, real-world action should stop. The associated action requirements then remain at the level of description, candidate explanations, obtaining further evidence, non-action, or proposing a reversible probe; theoretical language must not be used to bypass authorization.

## 2.3 Four Kinds of Dependency Must Be Kept Separate {#section-2-3}

There are four different relations between propositions, and they must not be merged into an ambiguously defined “dependency.”

| Kind of Dependency | Meaning | May It Enter the Inferential Acyclic Graph? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inferential premise | A hard premise without which the conclusion cannot be obtained | Yes |
| Method requirement | Methodological requirements concerning evidence, causation, analogy, sources, or domain classification | No |
| Domain restriction | Restricts a universal proposition to a particular domain, object, or subtype | No |
| Objects of application | Specifies which objects, propositions, or actions a norm or procedure applies to | No |

Method requirements determine whether a judgment may be released, but do not thereby become additional empirical causes. Domain restriction narrows the scope of application, but does not turn classification rules into factual evidence. That a norm or procedure applies to an object constrains action, but does not in turn prove a description true. Only hard inferential premises enter the inferential acyclic graph that leads from premises to inferred conclusions.

</div>

### 2.3.1 Layered Responsibilities of the Common Method Kernel {#section-2-3-1}

This framework and *World Model* may share some methodological responsibilities, while each text retains its own objects, arguments, and way of being used. Neither serves as an authoritative source for the other, nor do they constitute double empirical evidence. When the two texts are used side by side, definitions, method constraints, formal propositions, empirical questions, conclusion rules, and governance requirements need to be checked one by one. A shared name cannot substitute for a correspondence.

| Layer | Question Answered | Limits of Its Force |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definitions | What objects and terms refer to | Specify meanings that can be decided; do not establish that something holds in reality |
| Common method constraints | How representations, the status of materials, and inference are governed | Constrain how things are recorded and used; are not laws of the world |
| Conditional formal propositions | What necessarily follows under explicit mathematical premises | Established by proof; correspondence with reality requires separate verification |
| Empirical questions | Whether a specific empirical relation holds | Specific tests give the object, comparison, mode of failure, and evidence |
| Conclusion rules | What the existing basis permits to be determined and used | Valid only within their grounds, additional conditions, and applicable windows |
| Governance invariants | How to release, evaluate, suspend, and act | Limit power; do not serve as empirical causes |

The letter identifiers of the kinds of proposition provide stable references. The common kernel's Chinese identifiers serve only to align the two texts. They cannot replace the complete conditions in the body, nor can they obtain double support merely because the other text uses the same name.

## 2.4 Conditions for Causal Claims {#section-2-4}

A causal claim first states the specific version of the treatment or cause, the outcome, the object and population, the time window, the scale, and which effect is being asked about. It then explains why the comparison made can identify that effect. Common inputs, selection, attrition, changes in measurement, reverse causation, and interference between units must all be addressed where relevant. A claim about a specified structural channel or a complete mediation mechanism must also provide the mechanism chain, temporal sequence, and discriminating evidence at that level: whether blocking, replacement, or natural variation meeting identification conditions changes outcomes as expected; whether alternative channels can explain the difference; whether cross-layer conclusions are bridged by scale transformation (§3.4) and the epistemic constraint “Testing Cross-Scale Transfer” (§4.8.5); and what observations would make the mechanism claim fail.

Temporal sequence, correlation, and a coherent story cannot by themselves identify causation. A design may identify the total effect for a bounded population and window under explicit assumptions while still leaving the mediator undetermined. In that case, it should report “limited identification of the effect; the specific mechanism is unresolved.” Unsupported structural effects must not be filled in. Conversely, the separate existence of several local links does not automatically establish that the chain is connected as a whole in this case or that the net effect holds. Opposing paths may cancel one another out, and a total effect near zero does not establish that all mechanisms are absent. Causal strength, mechanism resolution, and the scope of extrapolation are evaluated separately.

</div>

### 2.4.1 The Estimand and Mechanism Responsibilities {#section-2-4-1}

Offering someone an opportunity, that person actually receiving an intervention, and expansion of an entire institution are different treatments. A population average effect, the local average treatment effect for a particular kind of compliers, and each individual's outcome are also different estimands. When an identification assumption is impaired, downgrade only the conclusions that depend on it. For example, an interpretation based on a particular instrumental variable may no longer be reliable without automatically invalidating the conclusion, drawn from credible random assignment, about the effect of “receiving an opportunity.”

More variables do not make observational control more reliable by default. Controlling for a mediator may change the total effect being asked about; controlling for a variable jointly determined by different causes may introduce bias. A larger sample cannot compensate for explanations that cannot be distinguished, either. When point identification is unavailable, supported bounds, sets of possible directions, exclusions based on necessary conditions, or specific process knowledge may be retained, with a clear statement of what these conclusions have not answered.

“Offering an opportunity” and “actually accepting, remaining, or exiting” are different treatments. The text of an agreement, verification, execution, and everyday use are also different outcomes. An announcement, public understanding, arrival, action at a border crossing, and full clearance must each be evidenced separately along the event chain. Neither a total effect nor the existence of an agreement can fill these gaps in mediation and distribution.

</div>

### 2.4.2 Local Effects, Joint Responses, and the Chain as a Whole {#section-2-4-2}

When local channels are combined, the correspondence between an earlier output and a later input, the identity of the same case and its temporal sequence, the common scope of support, joint responses, and terminal outcomes should be retained. Even when two studies concern the same population, their two average effects generally cannot be multiplied directly: the people whose mediator changes may be precisely those whose outcome would not respond to that mediator. Accurate averages on both sides do not guarantee that the common conditions needed for the sequence have been retained.

A finite counterexample is a population composed of two equally weighted types, with type denoted by s∈&#123;0,1&#125;, binary treatment by a, and mediator by m. Let m(a)=a(1−s), with outcome y(a,m)=sm. The average effect of treatment on the mediator and the average effect of directly setting the mediator on the outcome are both 1/2. Yet in each type, the effect of treatment on the final outcome is 0, and the effect of the chain as a whole is 0. Multiplying the two averages gives 1/4, which is not the effect of the chain as a whole. This is a counterexample under given functions. It neither estimates a real-world effect nor denies a total effect independently identified by a qualified design.

“Complete” in a claim about the chain as a whole is relative only to the stated outcome, window, and route relied on. If the purpose is to identify a particular total effect with a specified scope, there is no requirement to exhaust all microscopic processes first. If the purpose changes to manipulating a mediator, transferring across domains, or allocating responsibility, supply the connections on which that purpose actually depends. Shared resources, offsetting paths, and feedback can change the result of the connection. Separate evidence for each node cannot substitute for a joint test in the same case.

### 2.4.3 Constitutive Relations and Causal Relations {#section-2-4-3}

Some relations specify what a thing counts as under a given set of rules: whether an expression becomes a commitment, whether a document becomes a valid authorization, whether a person holds membership or eligibility to apply. Constitutive relations of this kind answer whether a standing holds. Their grounds are the source of the rule, the party entitled to perform the act, the required form, and the argument that the rule applies to the present case; they are not established by estimating effects. Causal questions ask what actually happened after the standing came to hold: whether rights were honored, whether commitments were fulfilled, whether eligibility changed opportunities and behavior.

The two kinds of question are evidenced separately, and neither can substitute for the other. A causal role that has not yet been identified does not cancel a standing that has already come to hold under the rules; nor does the holding of a standing prove that the corresponding effects have occurred. When analyzing the same arrangement, keep at least four items separate: whether the constitutive conditions are met, whether the parties understand it this way, whether it is executed and honored in practice, and whether it is normatively justified. A right may already be valid and not yet be honored; understanding a request is not the same as acknowledging that it has the standing to enter the decision.

A constitutive standing holds under the rules of the object's own domain and is supported by sources of law, documents, and records of acts by those entitled to perform them; the status of these materials is handled under §2.1.1. It is different from the role of “definition” in this framework (§2.2), nor does it belong to the empirical question of channel effects (§4.4). Pointing out that a standing is constituted by rules does not mean that all objects arise from convention, nor that the set of rules making the classification is thereby neutral or justified. Section 5.5.5 separates the de facto existence of an institution from its legal validity; the latter belongs to the constitutive question here. For the corresponding distinctions concerning forms of meaning and authorization, see §7.3.3 and §14.6. The “constitutive relation” of this section refers only to standing under rules. Where the book says that components “constitute” an object, that several materials “together constitute” an explanation, or that support is a constituent part of a task, the sense is that of making up; where it says that a material or a practice “constitutes” or “does not constitute” evidence, repair, or delivery, it means whether the item counts as such under the criteria stated at that place. Only where the criteria come from the rules of a domain, for example whether an utterance suffices to constitute valid authorization, does the matter belong to the constitutive question of this section.

## 2.5 Conditions for Evidence {#section-2-5}

The evidence chain distinguishes sources, materials, observation records, evidence, counterexamples, judgments, and authorization to act. Each layer needs its own justification for conversion. State the object, data-generation process, research design, measurement and selection, uncertainty, competing explanations, and limits of extrapolation according to the specific task. For formal tests and foresight[^tn-foresight-02], also state null models, thresholds, and evaluation under their respective rules. A material's having a source does not permit omitting the support relation between that material and the current claim.

Absence and silence are not negative evidence by default; inability to observe is not the same as nonexistence. Justified opacity must state its reason, minimum scope, duration, and trusted reviewer, with the strength of public conclusions reduced accordingly. If counterexamples or residuals are found after the evidence cutoff, the failure record should be retained, and the claim downgraded, suspended, or withdrawn as agreed in advance. The object, subtype, threshold, or success criterion must not be rewritten after results appear.

</div>

### 2.5.1 Measurement Objects and Data Generation {#section-2-5-1}

Directly recorded quantities, composite indicators defined by construction, measurement of latent attributes, formative conditions, and predictive proxies carry different explanatory responsibilities. A test score can accurately reflect performance within the current set of questions while not yet representing long-term capability. Usage can fall while the required services are still insufficient. More diagnoses may also reflect easier access to testing. First state what is to be measured and how the materials are generated, then decide which errors and counterexamples are relevant to the current use.

Comparisons across groups or periods require comparability proportionate to the task. Estimating latent means generally requires explaining the unit and origin of the measurement scale. Person-specific threshold decisions must also address individual error and consequences. An identical scoring mapping does not establish identical psychological strategies. If the common anchors themselves drift, more samples may not distinguish genuine change from changes in the measurement scale. External anchoring should be sought, or sensitivity reported.

Changes in an indicator can result from genuine improvement, genuine adaptation to a narrow target, changes in the sample that enters or is reported, or falsification of records. These mechanisms can coexist and require separate investigation. Nor does the fact that two legitimate indicators answer different questions and point in opposite directions mean that one must be wrong, much less justify directly concluding that there has been manipulation.

</div>

## 2.6 Conditions for Analogy {#section-2-6}

Analogy is only a tool for generating questions. Every use must be explicitly marked as an analogy and specify the source domain, target domain, shared structure, key differences, nontransferable conditions, the correspondence between variables, and discriminating tests. Similar words, similar diagrams, compelling narratives, or similar outcomes do not constitute evidence for a bridge across domains.

Analogy can help raise questions such as “which channel, boundary, or competing explanation should also be checked.” It cannot replace a formal test, nor can it transfer causal relations, normative evaluations, or action plans directly from the source domain to the target domain. If contrary conditions have not been specified, the conclusion from the analogy is only a heuristic candidate.

</div>

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 2.6.1 Analogy, Formal Instances, and Tested Comparisons {#section-2-6-1}

A heuristic analogy, a formal instance satisfying explicit mathematical premises, and a target-domain relation tested against independent materials are three different states. For the first, state similarities and differences; for a formal instance, check variables and assumptions; for empirical transfer, test operations, formation processes, and alternative explanations in the target domain. A comparison supported by independent evidence can contribute to a specific argument. Its support comes from those materials and bridges, not from the word “like.”

Historical and archaeological comparisons must also distinguish the physical formation of traces, the functions of activities, and social relations. Similar tool marks do not automatically imply the same institutions of power; kinship does not automatically imply relations of rule; and accurate dates do not automatically yield an event's full meaning. Respect for different sources of knowledge does not require giving them equal weight on every question. Different materials may each supply content that cannot be substituted for the others.

## 2.7 Conditions for Sources {#section-2-7}

Every source record must specify, item by item, the domain the source belongs to, the case type, time and region, scope of support, and limits of extrapolation, and must record counterexamples. Original materials, secondary interpretations, and this framework's own inferences are recorded separately. Judgments in the text and the original locations of the materials need to be findable from each other: readers can return from a judgment to the materials, and can see from the materials which limited claim they have been used to support.

Conceptual definitions, correspondences between elements, and method descriptions specify only what is to be tested, how it is to be recorded, and where inference stops. They must not be counted as records of empirical evidence and used as support for object facts or mechanisms. Internal terms, identifiers, examples, and the framework's own formulations cannot replace external materials. In other words, a method definition is not the same as empirical support.

Empirical support must come from independently recorded case materials, which include the object and scale, time window, research design, null model, outcomes, uncertainty, the state of review or reproduction, and evidential level. Without corresponding external case materials, specific empirical assertions under the four empirical questions cannot acquire formal empirical support. The conclusion rules are used separately for three purposes, determining outcomes, combining mechanism conditions, and normative bridging; judgments about real-world mechanisms under the conditional mechanisms still need the corresponding case evidence. A question catalog, the conclusion rules, and the framework's own descriptions cannot replace the missing materials.

</div>

### 2.7.1 Relations between Sources and the Scope of Actual Use {#section-2-7-1}

Reading a table of contents, an abstract, relevant chapters, or the full text should be recorded separately. Finding a link, completing a download, and understanding an argument are also different actions. Relations between sources should be retained for different analyses of the same data, a preprint and its published version, and an original report and an institution's account of it. Citation counts must not be added up as independent reproductions. When a version changes outcomes, samples, or reporting definitions, first explain the changes, then compare conclusions.

A theory's historical adoption, mere mention, explicit restriction or rejection, compatibility with the current text, and a new connection now proposed by the analyst are distinct matters. Failure to find a record of adoption is not the same as its never having been considered; transcribing a catalog is not the same as checking the original document. Failure to obtain a document within one research window does not mean that other researchers or the project as a whole lack it.

When restating and evaluating someone else’s theory, retain its claims at the strength of the original text, and retain the qualifications it has already stated: do not weaken or strengthen either on the author’s behalf, and do not treat qualifications the author wrote as omissions. Distinctions the original work has already drawn are not recorded as contributions of this framework or of later writers; the analyst’s own reconstruction is not written as something the original already contains. When raising a criticism, state whether the standard used comes from the other side’s own premises, from premises the evaluator has adopted separately, or from the factual materials that the other side’s claims involve: the second is an external evaluation, which can stand but is not the same as pointing out an error internal to the other side’s argument; the third is evaluated according to the evidential status of the materials (§2.5).

The generation, preservation, destruction, opening, and retrieval of archives, as well as narrative choices, can each cause absence. Absence has the corresponding force as counterevidence only when it is clear that a certain kind of event should have left records that could be found. Inaccurate dates in oral accounts still need correction, while the way the account is told may also be important material for understanding memory and experience. Neither cancels the other.

### 2.7.2 Evidence Independence and the Propagation of Defects {#section-2-7-2}

Independence must be judged relative to the claim being supported. Adding later outcomes to the same random assignment can add temporal and outcome information. Testing the same data with another algorithm can uncover implementation or interpretation problems. Neither increases the number of independent randomizations or original cohorts. Conversely, studies on the same subject but with different underlying samples must not be merged merely because their names are similar. Records should state which data are shared, which measurements are common, what has been carried over from earlier versions, and which observations are genuinely new, rather than turn a file count into an evidence score. If the classification, dating, selection, or meaning of materials is taken from the very explanation or claim they are meant to support, the agreement so obtained shows only that the selected materials can be read coherently under that explanation; it does not constitute independent support for it. Independent support comes from records whose classification, dating, selection, and meaning do not depend on that explanation. Where the dependence is partial, state in which item it lies and to what degree, with the granularity of the annotation proportionate to the use of the conclusion (§1.5.2). This does not reject the research practice of moving back and forth between an overall conception and particular materials, nor does it deprive other materials that have independent records of their supporting force.

Traceable dependencies should be retained between source values, transcriptions, model inputs, derived statistics, and judgments in the text. When a local discrepancy is found, first distinguish conflict concerning the same object, legitimate differences between different objects, and apparent inconsistency caused by display precision. Then suspend the precise conclusions that actually depend on that discrepancy. Reconciliation of the component sum with the total does not by itself validate every component, nor does a source anomaly reveal the cause of a discrepancy on its own. Other judgments with independent support should continue to be used within their actual scope.

</div>

## 2.8 Core Terminology Locks {#section-2-8}

Once the following terms enter a judgment, they must satisfy their own specialized conditions. Their broad everyday meanings must not be substituted for those conditions.

Cost. First state the quantity's type, unit, time window, accounting boundary, and cost-bearing position. Shifting may be traced only for quantities of the same type, or quantities of different types with an explicit conversion and accounting correspondence. “Resources are occupied” is not the same as the existence of a unified cost across domains, nor does it establish any conservation principle.

Externality. First establish a cross-boundary causal channel and the positions across which its effects are distributed, then an institutional or accounting rule stating what is excluded from decision accounting. Without the latter, the effect can only be called a cross-boundary spillover, impact, or accounting gap. “Externality” must not be used to assign responsibility in advance.

The exclusion rule here may come from an explicit economic model or actual institutional and accounting arrangements. It does not require an advance determination of legal liability. The current inability to identify who bears legal liability does not prevent recording cross-boundary harm for which evidence already exists. The conclusion rule “Local Improvement and Cross-Boundary Spillover” (§4.12.4) addresses cross-boundary spillovers and the positions across which they are distributed under its specified conditions. Where changes of scale are actually involved, check separately under scale transformation (§3.4) and the epistemic constraint “Testing Cross-Scale Transfer” (§4.8.5); a claim of closure failure, or of object conversion or intervention conversion, then calls for the corresponding scale-closure test (§4.6). This rule does not cover all economic externalities, nor propagation in general.

Repair. The target identity criterion or the target functional criterion (§3.2) must be disclosed before results appear. Repair is not the same as recovery of a historical state, nor is it the same as improvement in value. Whether the target is justified, who decides it, and who may carry it out require separate review under normative premises and operational procedures.

Selection and choice. First distinguish systemic selection, decision-making by a subject, and collective governance (§14.2). A systemic process such as variation, differential retention, and reproduction does not possess intent as a matter of course. A subject's decision is not the same as environmental selection. Choice in collective governance additionally requires normative premises and authorization.

Responsibility and authorization. Uptake carriers, bearers of costs of the same type, beneficiaries, rights to stop, and responsible parties are stated separately. Actual execution, the greatest capability, being most affected, being most visible, or possessing knowledge cannot by themselves establish responsibility. Nor can an expansion of observational scope or greater confidence in an explanation expand authority to act.

Bearing obligations requires a separate account of the relations among commitments, roles, causal contributions, benefits, capabilities, and applicable norms. These reasons may act together; no one of them can automatically settle the whole matter. Disclosing bounded obligations and their boundaries does not make anyone a person required to take up obligations without limit, unilaterally, and without the possibility of exit.

Condition. This word is used broadly in this book: the prerequisites a mechanism needs in order to hold in an individual case (§3.5.8), circumstances that make an outcome more likely, the premises of a derivation or the terms taken as given, requirements under rules (§2.4.3), requirements of method, and criteria set down in advance may all be called conditions; expressions such as “material conditions” refer to the circumstances in which something is situated. When a judgment claims that one thing is a “condition” of another and the context does not show which relation is meant, it must be stated whether the thing is causally needed or contributory, statistically given, required by rules or method, or normatively presupposed, and whether it is necessary, sufficient, or only contributory.

</div>

## 2.9 Missing-Data and Visibility States, Downgrading, Suspension, and Exit {#section-2-9}

Every item of a definition must state its content or fall within one of the four states below, rather than be filled in with vague wording. The four states explain why an item states no content: the content cannot yet be obtained (unknown, unobservable), the material exists but should not be disclosed (withheld for protection), or the item has no counterpart in the current definition (not applicable). Content that has been established is stated as it is and is not classed as a missing-data state, for example a verified “the rules make no provision for this”; stating it as it is does not waive the downgrading or suspension to which that content gives rise.

Unknown: knowable in principle, but current evidence is insufficient. Where the cases can be told apart, note whether the item is not yet collected, subject to conflicting sources, or still indistinguishable after attempts have been made. Before recording conflicting sources, first exclude under §2.7.2 legitimate differences between different objects and apparent inconsistencies caused by display precision; where the conflict does concern the same object, retain each side of the conflict and the provenance of each (§10.8). Conflicting sources means that a value cannot be settled because qualifying sources conflict with one another; a hypothesis or inference for which the supporting and opposing evidence or explanations conflict is recorded as “disputed” (§9.3) and is not classed as a missing-data state.

Not applicable: the item has no valid counterpart in the current object's definition; a reason must be stated.

Unobservable: materials cannot currently be obtained because of limits in measurement capability, the window, the channel, permissions, or safety conditions; this must not be rewritten as nonexistence. Materials that are already on record but whose disclosure would violate an explicit protection belong to “withheld for protection” below; materials that the analysis cannot obtain because of permissions or safety conditions are recorded as unobservable even if they exist elsewhere.

Withheld for protection: materials exist, but disclosure would violate privacy, security, or other explicit protections. The basis for protection, minimum scope of non-disclosure, duration, and a trusted review path must be recorded.

When key variables are unknown, conditions under which the object is not applicable remain unaddressed, core channels are unobservable, or materials withheld for protection cannot receive trusted review, conclusions are downgraded, according to how what is missing affects the proposition, to candidate hypotheses, lists of questions, or description only, with the reason for downgrading to that level stated. When authorization to act is unclear, potential harm is irreversible, appeals and stopping cannot be exercised, or the limits of professional capability would be exceeded, real-world action should be suspended and referred to an appropriate procedure. Downgrading is not analytical failure. It keeps the evidential ceiling, boundaries of power, and what is still unknown visible. Downgrading proceeds proposition by proposition, stating what each proposition depends on and how what is missing affects it. Other judgments with independent support are not downgraded along with it; when a global source, a common premise, or the object's boundary fails, all related outputs need to be blocked.

</div>

## 2.10 Methodological Boundaries for Multi-Circle Objects {#section-2-10}

Circles[^tn-circle-02] are first of all candidate groupings, not natural entities established by naming. Families, organizations, platform groups, occupational communities, or cultural categories should specify boundaries, members, roles, channels, time windows, and identity criteria. Documents, relationship records, or exposure records can support the relevant facts about objects. A claim that the grouping brings explanatory or foresight gains must then be compared against suitable simple baselines, such as no grouping or a single circle. The formal support given by a grouping-gain test (§4.3) does not replace other grounds for the object's existence. Parallelism, nesting, and overlap are judged along dimensions stated in advance. Potential bridging, actual transmission, and effects of transmission require separate evidence. “Competition” specifies constraints on the available set; “temporariness” specifies a time limit or evidence concerning duration. Both can be combined with other relations.

Multi-circle analysis must not turn every background factor into an entity. Before a circle is added, the necessary question it answers, the distinction that would be lost if it were removed, and the measurement and privacy costs should be stated. If it is used for explanation or propagation, the relevant channels and competing explanations must also be specified. Where its current purpose cannot be stated, retain it as an environmental condition or candidate variable. When a complex model lacks stable comparative support, reduce the relevant gain claims and give priority to an adequate simple model. Retaining an object to describe supported responsibilities, differences in membership, or authority over matters does not first require proving that it increases predictive accuracy.

</div>

## 2.11 Methodological Boundaries for Personality and Actor States {#section-2-11}

Personality enters only as a slow-variable hypothesis with an evidential level, not as an ontological definition of a person. A single behavior, a single account given by the person, a single description by someone else, a scale score, or a model-generated cluster cannot by itself establish a stable personality. Analysis must also retain competing explanations such as role activation, material constraints, immediate emotions, strategies, cultural norms, observer position, and measurement bias.

Actor variables are divided by timescale into slow, intermediate, and fast bands. For each, the observation window, source, counterevidence, circle conditions, privacy, and permitted uses need to be stated with it. The framework does not perform clinical or psychiatric diagnosis, nor does it record silence, refusal to disclose, or unobservability as pathological evidence. A model may propose candidate variables and minimal tests; it must not automatically fill in real-world values.

## 2.12 Methodological Boundaries for Foresight and Projection {#section-2-12}

Path projection may use observed, reported, planned, hypothetical, and simulated events, but must retain the event type. Reported content is not automatically true; a plan is not the same as execution; and hypothetical and simulated events must not in turn be written as real-world facts. Each projection fixes in advance the joint snapshot, the time of the evidence cutoff, mechanisms, parameter ranges, clocks, propagation rules, and stopping conditions. Information obtained after the results may enter only a new version of the projection.

Conditional foresight must register, before results appear, the target, horizon, scope of objects and circles, the time of the input cutoff, simple baseline, paths, form of expression, calibration metrics, early signals, contrary signals, suspension, retirement, and outcome write-back, that is, appending the outcome to the original registration once it is known. Unregistered post hoc explanations do not count as successful predictions. Changing the target, extending the horizon, narrowing the population, or merging paths all require a new record. For reopening on stated grounds, see §2.14.

## 2.13 Limits on Autonomous Variable Refinement {#section-2-13}

“Autonomous variable refinement” permits only three actions: proposing multiple competing candidates from residuals; stating which channels a candidate might act through and what observable implications it might produce; and designing a minimal test with acceptable risk. Candidates move in sequence through the states proposed, under test, supported candidate, rejected, or retired. They must not jump directly from a model suggestion to an observed fact, a personality conclusion, a supported circle, or a causal mechanism.

If a candidate concerns privacy, health, safety, legal standing, or a position with little power, the necessity of obtaining the information, its use, and its harms should be reviewed first. When the value of the information is insufficient to outweigh the risk, keeping it “unknown” is more appropriate than actively filling it in.

</div>

## 2.14 Fixing and Reopening a Question {#section-2-14}

An analysis fixes the boundaries of the object and its membership, the definitions of the target and of loss, the admissible operations, the horizon, and the resource and permission constraints treated as given, so that its judgments can be checked. Fixing the question does not give the person who posed it a monopoly over it. Two kinds of reason can require reopening. One is that the current representation is insufficient to answer the original question, so that distinctions need to be added or the task narrowed. The other is that someone contends that one of the items above was itself set improperly—for example, the interests the target represents, who counts as a member, what counts as a loss, or which constraints are treated as given. Challenges of the latter kind may come from affected and excluded parties, and also from the analyst.

Whether or not a request to reopen is accepted, record who raised it, the reasons, and the grounds on which it was handled. When a question is reopened, retain the original question and its results. A failed original prediction is not recorded as a success because the question has changed, and an unfinished original task is not treated as finished because a new task has been completed (§12.7, §4.19). The new question is established separately, with a statement of why it holds, whether it replaces, runs parallel to, or refines the original question, and which of the existing evidence still applies under the new object and target. The analyst may answer both questions side by side, stating the grounds for each. For indicators, eligibility, agendas, or resource allocation internal to an institution, whether to change them is decided by procedures with the corresponding authority; objections from affected parties are handled under §14.5.

The reason for reopening must not be merely that the result is unfavorable. A result can expose an insufficient representation, and it can also expose losses, positions, or members that were not previously counted; these are all reasons. Changing the target only because the result is unfavorable amounts to selecting favorable results after the fact (§2.12, §4.1). Reopening does not rewrite the original question; it establishes a separate question under the changed given items. The formal conclusions of §4.19 hold only for the original state, target, action, and horizon; for the new question, the representation and evidence must be checked again.
