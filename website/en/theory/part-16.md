# Part 16 Governance {#part-title-16}

Using the framework to explain other objects does not exempt the framework itself from structural problems. Documentation can grow unwieldy, concepts can harden, tools can be captured by power, and users can come to depend for their identity on language they have invested in. Governance supplies practical arrangements for downgrading concepts, suspending assertions, blocking publication, restoring versions to their prior state, and replacing the framework. Decorative principles cannot do that work.

This Part governs four distinct objects: the current text and templates, the relations among concepts, the actual practices of users, and the community of maintainers, critics, people who provide counterexamples, and people affected. They must not be collapsed into a single, undifferentiated “framework.” A consistent document does not establish that there is no misuse in practice. An active community does not establish that the four empirical questions (Part 4) have been validated.

The framework cannot use its own language to establish that it is safe. Internal consistency, passing stress tests, passing automatic checks, and complete reports establish only that certain outputs of a process are complete. They do not establish real-world safety, effective repair, or consent from affected people, and do not confer authorization to act in reality.

When tools put these boundaries into practice, they should turn them into verifiable governance constraints. Internal statuses cannot establish what holds in reality by their own declaration. Governance records do not confer real-world authorization. Terminal records do not erase history. Formal adoption requires grounds in external review or approval suited to the intended use. Merely substituting an alias, claiming independence for oneself, or citing records circularly cannot satisfy these requirements. Whether these constraints have been implemented in a particular program, and whether they are actually being followed, must be verified separately. The requirements in the text do not constitute evidence of implementation.

This Part’s status and permission rules apply to the framework’s own text, modules, formal assertions, governance changes, and related high-impact uses. The rules do not claim to replace legal procedures in different jurisdictions or treat an external institution as nonexistent or invalid merely because it has not adopted these record formats. The legal validity, normative justification, and actual protection of an external institution, and whether the framework endorses its use, must still be judged separately.

## 16.1 Framework Self-Diagnosis[^tn-diagnosis-17] and Separation of Objects (GOV-01) {#section-16-1}

When the framework examines itself, the first step is to specify what is being examined. A blanket judgment that the whole framework is “healthy” cannot serve as the starting point.

For the document, check whether the text, table of contents, definitions, templates, and rules contradict one another.

For the concepts, check whether the four empirical questions, universal variables, the human adaptation layer (Part 7), normative choice, or procedures for action exceed their respective limits.

For practice, check how users actually diagnose, manage, teach, make public statements, and use AI.

For the community, check who controls naming, merging, interpretation, and publication, and who bears the consequences of misuse.

Each self-diagnosis needs to record at least the following: which kind of object is being examined, which version it concerns, who the rights holders are, where counterexamples come in, what harm has already been observed, what the falsification conditions are, and where the ceiling on judgment lies. If dependence on jargon, cognitive lock-in, an expert monopoly, accommodation of commercial interests, or a veneer of AI compliance has already caused harm, the affected module may be restricted, suspended, or assessed for replacement.

## 16.2 Consensus Procedure and Authority Over Versions (GOV-02) {#section-16-2}

“Everyone agreed in the end” does not establish effective consensus. For consensus to be effective, differing views must be able to enter safely, be recorded and answered, and materially change the text and practice. The procedure has at least seven steps.

Put the matter on the agenda. Introducing a concept, substantially rewriting the text, adjusting the four empirical questions, and reporting misuse or counterexamples must first become traceable issues.

Disclose the material. List cases, counterexamples, user feedback, external research, failures in practice, and ethical risks.

Identify what is affected. Specify the users, people being diagnosed, low-power subjects, and settings that the change would affect.

Provide access for dissent. Users with little power, people being diagnosed, nonspecialist readers, and external critics must all be able to submit objections.

Distinguish decision levels. Minor revisions, changes to core concepts, and changes to the four empirical questions require distinct authority.

Preserve minority views. Objections that are not accepted must not be deleted. They enter the record of retained counterexamples and unresolved questions.

Write back to the version record. State what was introduced, removed, or downgraded, the reasons, and what evidence would lead to withdrawal of the change.

If the institutional arrangements already rule out the possibility that dissent could bring about a necessary correction, the procedure may be recorded only as “in name only.” It cannot be used to restore a suspended module. Selection of participants, terms of office, time limits, and statutory powers must be separately pre-registered for the particular organization, jurisdiction, or community, that is, set down before the results are seen. Uniform numbers must not be copied without regard to the organization, jurisdiction, risk, and resources.

Each objection must have a decision on how it is handled, grounds for that decision, and the evidence used, together with a statement of the effects it had on the text, procedure, or action ceiling. If an objection is said to have been accepted, it must be possible to point to the change actually made as a result. Objections that are declined for stated reasons must remain on record. Recording a governance change as “adopted” does not require token acceptance of at least one objection. It requires that reasons capable of changing a key judgment, protection, or permission all be addressed in a way suited to them. Suspend the affected adoption if any such objection is still unresolved, required independent review is still missing, or any requirement of the rights floor or any nonwaivable requirement of applicable law has yet to be met. Whether an objection is decisive is determined under criteria published in advance, open to appeal, and independently reviewable. The proposer or a majority cannot establish that on its own.

“Adopted” indicates only that the governance change meets the framework’s conditions for internal adoption. It does not establish that the change works in reality or confer permission for external action. Upholding a correct decision for stated reasons can constitute an adequate review. If correction is needed and does not actually occur, a record of responding cannot count as completion. The value of participation in general, the causal effect of feedback, actual change to the text, eligibility for internal adoption, and real-world protection are judged separately. Emergency duties are still exercised under their own independent authority, scope, validity period, and review conditions.

## 16.3 Correcting Empirical Assertions and Choosing Which of the Four Empirical Questions to Keep (GOV-03) {#section-16-3}

Correction starts by distinguishing three objects. A proposition in a specific test can be overturned by counterevidence of suitable weight. A generalization or a claim about coverage formed across several tests can be limited by counterexamples within its scope. The four empirical questions, as a classification of questions, have no independent empirical truth value. Whether to keep the classification, narrow it, or remove it from the core should depend on whether it omits important questions, whether it reduces confusion, how great a burden it imposes in use, and its net benefit compared with a baseline such as a domain method or a simpler method. Positive results in several tests do not establish that the four questions exhaust reality or that the classification is optimal.

The four empirical questions and their application modules can be in one of the following five management statuses. Management statuses express policies for use and maintenance and do not replace the evidential status of any specific proposition.

Normal use. Continue use for stated purposes. The claim made in each specific test is still judged on its own evidence. Frequent use does not establish that the classification adds value.

Boundary narrowed. Narrow the scope of use where the classification misses important distinctions or adds burdens for particular objects, tasks, scales, or institutional settings.

Suspended. Suspend affected uses and retain existing independent facts when the classification or module continues to produce identifiable misjudgments or high-risk misuse.

Replacement comparison. Compare the classification with domain methods, simpler methods, and other classifications of questions on the same tasks and materials with resources suited to the comparison. Check judgment quality, omissions, unwarranted abstention, and total burden separately.

Removed from the core. The question classification no longer constrains the shared core. Material that remains useful can be retained as a domain tool or a historical record. Preserve the known results of the specific tests separately according to their evidence.

A public assertion passes through three statuses in turn: internal draft, open assertion, and publishable. At any of these steps it may move to “downgraded,” “suspended,” “publication blocked,” “withdrawn,” or “replaced.” The framework as a whole can likewise be in only one of these statuses: in force, boundary narrowed, suspended, replacement comparison, replaced, or retired.

“Removed from the core,” “withdrawn,” “replaced,” and “retired” are terminal states. Once one of them has been entered, there can be no further move to “in force” or another in-service status, and restoring a prior state cannot be used to bring back old authorization. A return to service under a successor method requires a new identifiable identity, fresh external authorization, and preservation of the history of the old terminal state.

Each of the following triggers a review of whether to suspend: counterexamples recur; a kind of subject is consistently misjudged; the scope of explanation keeps expanding; refutation is evaded by saying only “the other party is not a system,” “there has not been enough time,” or “the evidence is not deep enough.” Counterexamples must be handled in a way suited to their role in the argument. A valid local counterexample changes the corresponding boundary. Repeated systemic failure triggers a replacement comparison. Misuse causing serious harm suspends the affected use. If review finds that a counterexample does not refute the original claim, preserve the reasons and the conditions under which they would cease to hold. A correct claim must not be changed just to show responsiveness. Counterexamples must not all be set outside the theory.

The consequences of a counterexample should match the claim it actually bears on. First check the object, measurement, auxiliary conditions, mechanism, and scope of extrapolation. Then decide whether to make a local correction or replace the core. Do not attribute every anomaly to the core or reclassify every anomaly as falling outside it. A local revision requires independent grounds and new testable consequences. Conditions must not be added after the fact merely to explain a known failure.

## 16.4 Tracking Cases, Counterexamples, Assertions, and Forecasts (GOV-04) {#section-16-4}

There are five kinds of authoritative record. They are linked, but each serves its own purpose and cannot substitute for another.

Counterexample record. Record the object of the counterexample, scale and window, observable facts, reasons it is not noise, original and alternative explanations, requirements for handling it, and subsequent signals.

Case record. Preserve successes, failures, early dispersals, unfinished processes, counterexamples, indeterminate cases, and excluded cases together. For excluded cases, record “whether the insufficiency of material is itself a blind spot of the framework.”

When using a set of cases to compare mechanisms of formation, failure, or dispersal, or to estimate the proportion of a result, state the object, the outcome, the temporal and spatial scope, and the rule by which cases enter the set. Preserve actions and processes that had a relevant opportunity to occur but did not. Do not put all unrelated nonoccurrences into the same denominator. Assess the possibility of occurrence and the conditions for detecting it in the material separately. Lack of a record that can be found cannot be entered directly as zero. A theoretical expectation cannot be the sole reason to exclude a real counterexample. If a boundary is disputed, preserve the judgments under each of several reasonable scopes and how the judgments change with the scope. Interpreting a single case does not require defining a population for frequency estimates. A professional case-selection record that meets the same requirements can be cited directly (Mahoney and Goertz, 2004, authors’ manuscript).

Assertion record. Record the assertion’s text, type, object, scale, affected positions, evidence, counterevidence, alternative explanations, withdrawal conditions, action ceiling, and publication status.

Forecast Registration (§12.2). Record the prediction, time window, triggering conditions, early signals, contrary signals, competing predictions, and outcome at expiry. What it records is conditional foresight. It does not determine anyone’s fate.

Version log. Enter counterexamples, narrowed boundaries, downgrades, suspensions, blocks on publication, withdrawals, replacements, retirements, restorations, and remedies in a public ledger.

A formal judgment made in the framework’s name must have a corresponding entry in the assertion record to qualify for publication or high-impact use under the framework. Ordinary discussion, independent domain research, and well-grounded local opinions remain responsible for sources, correction, and use according to their own tasks. They do not lose their standing as knowledge because they are not entered in the framework’s records. More cases on record do not establish that the phenomenon is more widespread. Internally selected cases do not amount to external validation. Once a judgment enters organizational action or automated decisions, the requirements for strong judgments (§13.5) and the conditions of authorization still apply.

Governance changes also need seven further kinds of record that can be checked: the established identities of participants, evidence, relationships of interest, authorization, external review, objections, and the version log. Every item of evidence a change cites must correspond to an evidence record that actually exists and whose integrity has been verified. Reviewers, members who take part in the decision, and subjects of authorization must each appear under a single established identity. Check each one’s relationship to the proposer for funding, evaluation, identity, promotion, control, and commercial interests. Aliases can be used only to look someone up and cannot substitute for identity: the same person under another name does not count as another person. The person who issues an authorization must not also be its holder, nor the proposer.

## 16.5 The Pre-Concept Gate and Ceiling on Conceptual Validity (GOV-05) {#section-16-5}

The framework must not force something into the category of “structural domain” (§7.3.1) and then use its own concept to prove that classification. Before formal mapping, answer a few questions in plain language. Can the parts be observed together? Is there a minimum boundary? Do actions within it repeatedly affect one another? Is there a shared problem, direction, risk, or constraint? Who bears the costs of maintaining it? Can results or bad news change subsequent practice? This set of plain-language questions is the pre-concept gate.

If the pre-concept gate is not passed, the output is limited to describing phenomena, recording risks, or analyzing narratives. Even after it passes, the concept may be used at only one of five levels of strength.

Expressive level. Helps only to state feelings or similarities clearly.

Exploratory level. Can be used only to raise questions and observe signals.

Diagnostic level. External observable material permits judgments with low to moderate confidence.

Strong-judgment level. The five gates for object, evidence, scale, responsibility, and observation (§13.4) have all been passed.

Action level. Additionally requires external review, appeal, anti-retaliation, compensation, and withdrawal, and still has to go through normative choice under Part 14, itemized authorization, and the operational procedure (§14.6).

## 16.6 Absence Signals, Protective Opacity, and Suppressive Opacity (GOV-06) {#section-16-6}

What is not visible can still carry information. Absence of an anchor may mean no shared orientation ever formed. Absence of feedback may mean there is no channel or people are afraid to speak. Absence of conflict may come from fear, dependence, or punishment. Absence of repair may mean there is no route to repair. Absence of exit may mean the cost of leaving is unbearable. Absence of language may mean people lack the means to express themselves.

Opacity has three kinds.

Protective opacity. Protects vulnerable subjects, privacy, witness safety, experience that is still taking shape, and feedback from low-power positions.

Functional opacity. Reduces information overload and preserves room for necessary professional judgment, ritual, buffering, or undisturbed decision-making. It must be reviewed afterward, and responsibility must remain traceable.

Suppressive opacity. Obscures responsibility, blocks feedback, evades review, produces fear, preserves a monopoly on power, or prevents people who were harmed from proving the harm.

Classifying an opacity must answer four questions. Who is protected? Who benefits? Who can review independently? When is it lifted? If the four answers, independent review, time limits, or conditions for lifting it are incomplete, the opacity must not be counted as a healthy boundary. Suppressive opacity directly triggers measures against capture and external review, and blocks public release.

The time limits and conditions for lifting opacity govern continued review of the grounds for confidentiality. They do not require sensitive material to be disclosed automatically on a particular date. Review may continue protection or disclose only what is sufficient for a limited judgment. Inability to confirm that an opacity is justified does not establish suppression. Conversely, a confidentiality label cannot be used to conceal falsified records, evade responsibility, or block credible verification.

## 16.7 Operating Limits of the Prototype of Adaptive Dynamic Balance and Stopping Observational Recursion (GOV-07) {#section-16-7}

This clause concerns only the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance (§8.3.7), one of the seven state prototypes in Part 8. It applies only to an object that independent records confirm to be a “human intentional collective” (§8.1) and whose state includes this prototype. It does not apply to individual humans, nonhuman systems, or human collectives that have yet to meet this prototype. The prototype of adaptive dynamic balance meant here is no universal level of the world broadly conceived.

The prototype of adaptive dynamic balance does not transcend entropy increase or describe a perfect system. For a specified function, object, and limited time window, it requires evidence of problem detection, correction of errors, recovery of function, and continuity when others take over. It must also meet the conditions of applicability in Part 8. Record the lag in detecting problems, the lag in repair, recurrence rates, resource margins, and load distribution separately. Processes measured in different units must not be combined into a single speed. Current operation that is going well can support a judgment about the corresponding function. It is not enough by itself to determine that the object fits this prototype. Genuine recovery achieved under continuing support does not automatically cease to count because support has yet to be removed.

Observational recursion may expand only when a new response changes boundaries, identity, resources, channels for action, uptake relationships, feedback write-back, risk, or public memory, or supplies a new signal that is costly to produce. Recursion must stop if any of the following occurs: two consecutive rounds yield only the same kind of response; the object begins manipulating diagnostic rules; continued tracking would increase exposure; the informational gain is less than the disruption to the structure; or the diagnostician can no longer tell which is the object’s state and which is a product of observation.

After stopping, do not keep asking “what the object really is.” State only that a particular response pattern can be confirmed under current observation conditions. Further judgment requires external review or new external evidence that distinguishes the alternatives.

## 16.8 Malicious Compliance and Checking for Selective Evidence (GOV-08) {#section-16-8}

Malicious compliance occurs when forms, evidence chains, reviews, and appeals are completed selectively while sources are still manipulated, weak signals filtered, inaction dressed up, or responsibility obscured. It differs from open violation. Check each of ten signals. There is only one source. Counterexamples are dismissed as noise or emotion. Open assertions become labels. Appeals never change outcomes. Supposedly independent parties are controlled by the same chain of power. AI reports lack original materials and external verification. Diagnostic language is used to justify inaction or avoid accountability. Potential fields, entropy increase, and stages are used to dress up inaction. Love, mission, the larger interest, or professional expertise are invoked to continue extracting from people with little power. People who appeal are subsequently described as irrational because they appealed.

Three or more signals block strong judgments. At five or more signals, record a high risk of malicious compliance. Only a risk record, a request for external review, and exploratory steps for protection may then be given. The party being checked cannot itself confirm that the reviewer is independent.

Three and five are procedural thresholds for starting protection and review. They are neither constants read from nature nor calibrated probabilities of malice. A trigger means a strong judgment should not proceed under present conditions. It does not establish that anyone deliberately deceived others. Review must state dependencies among the signals, the respective costs of false alarms and misses, and the particular circumstances. Any change to the thresholds should disclose its grounds through the version procedure. Thresholds must not be changed on the spot after a case outcome is known.

## 16.9 AI Boundaries and What Process Outputs Can Establish (GOV-09) {#section-16-9}

AI can do the following: organize materials and verify their identity; propose and compare mechanisms; form grounded judgments and critiques that can be refuted; recommend options under disclosed objectives, normative premises, and constraints; and draft institutional arrangements, plans, and commentary for deliberation. Its outputs should state the current choice and its key grounds, its costs, the strongest opposing case, and the conditions that would be enough to change the choice. They should also present in full the substantive analysis already conducted.

AI must not invent sources or protection conditions. It cannot use fluent text, internal scores, or the completion of a procedure to establish the commissioning party’s good faith, effective anti-retaliation, or that actual repair has been achieved. Nor can it substitute for independent review or for bearing responsibility. AI cannot independently make a high-risk real-world decision or substitute the claim that “the procedure has passed” for a decision by a subject with authority. A recommendation made in analysis does not amount to actual adoption. High-risk decisions and real-world execution still require the applicable procedures for strong judgments, protection, normative choice, and authorization.

AI outputs should check the following materials against the specific task and the conclusion proposed: original facts; testimony from affected positions; counterexamples; feedback from low-power positions; external review; records of appeals that changed outcomes; who controls the evidence; and the AI’s own selection and rewriting of material. For each, state how it relates to the conclusion, whether it is available, and what limits follow from its absence. The number of missing items alone cannot determine the ceiling for the whole output. If determining a fact lacks decisive evidence, make no such determination. If identifying causation lacks discriminating evidence, retain competing explanations. If the conditions for protection and review are inadequate, do not certify actual safety, consent, fairness, or repair, and do not certify eligibility for high-impact adoption. Evaluations of arguments in texts, local factual judgments, and conditional recommendations with stated premises can each stand, provided the gaps do not affect them. Any judgment that an item is not applicable must give reasons. “Not applicable” must not be used in place of “unknown,” nor to bypass a real risk. Outputs still governed by the provisions on strong judgments and real-world action remain subject to the corresponding requirements.

Process outputs do not establish what holds in reality. Fluent prose, automatic scoring, internal stress tests, consistency tables, passed automatic checks, report counts, and fully completed forms cannot establish repair, consent, safety, or effectiveness.

## 16.10 Six Components of Weak-Signal Protection (GOV-10) {#section-16-10}

Protecting weak signals cannot depend only on a diagnostician being “willing to listen.” Weak-signal protection has six components, and each must be stated explicitly.

Anonymous or low-exposure submission. People can submit facts without first disclosing their identity.

Independent parties to receive and take up the material. Signals must not go only to the chain of power being questioned.

Anti-retaliation records. Record loss of power, isolation, stigma, punishment, and changes in resources that occur after feedback is given.

Calibration by group. Overall satisfaction or average performance must not hide deterioration in the situation of groups at the margins.

Delayed disclosure. When safety is at risk, protect those providing signals before discussing disclosure.

Evidence of write-back. After a signal is taken up, actual changes to rules, resources, responsibilities, or authoritative records must be visible.

If any of the six components is missing, the output may say only that “the risk to weak signals has been recognized.” It must not claim that “weak signals have been protected.” One weak signal does not directly establish a conclusion. It also does not automatically disappear because it is infrequent, anonymous, emotional, or incomplete in form.

## 16.11 An Intermediate Path Without Institutional Infrastructure (GOV-11) {#section-16-11}

Families, intimate relationships, friendships, small teams, and temporary communities often lack independent reviewers, stable channels for appeal, and complete evidence chains. They can still involve ongoing harm, control of information, relational dependence, and risks to reputation or safety. The intermediate path for such situations does not simulate institutions that are absent, require people affected to take risks gathering evidence, or equate “cannot be reviewed” with “no facts can be recorded.”

This path can give only a protective open assertion. State what material is currently available, the continuing pattern that most warrants concern, what uses are prohibited, which signals call for escalation, which call for withdrawal, and a low-risk next step. The objective of action moves from “proving who is wrong” to reducing exposure, preserving records, restoring support, seeking an outside perspective, and observing subsequent signals. It must not be used to punish, to place something in a dossier, to decide eligibility, to classify someone publicly, or to deprive anyone of resources.

## 16.12 Protection of Subjects Unable to Exit (GOV-12) {#section-16-12}

Children, people who depend heavily on others, people whose income sources are controlled by others, incarcerated people, people bound by caregiving relationships, or people with extremely limited resources may be unable to exit. Remaining does not amount to consent. Subjects unable to exit need six safeguards: prioritize safety; provide trusted representatives; restore minimal choices, information, records, and connections to support; keep records with low exposure; provide local buffers and alternative ways out; and prohibit romanticizing inability to exit as love, loyalty, maturity, sacrifice, or service to the larger interest.

People should not have to remain exposed to prove their circumstances. Allow submission through a representative, delayed expression, phased disclosure, outside arrangements to take over, preservation of evidence, and refusal of direct dialogue. The objective of protection under conditions that prevent exit is to reduce harm, preserve subjecthood, and expand future choices. An ideal exit must not be forced.

The tasks taken on by a trusted representative must be specified. Record the grounds and scope separately for assisting the person’s understanding and expression, submitting materials for the person, handling a matter the person has delegated, and making a decision in the person’s place. A representative’s statement cannot automatically count as the person’s own expression or consent. Preserve means of communication the person can use, conflicts of interest, conditions for the person to refuse or change support, review of objections, and continuity of necessary support. If the person’s wishes remain unclear, state the support efforts made, the grounds for interpreting the wishes, and what remains uncertain. Substitute decisions and emergency measures in records or institutions also require separate checks on their institutional basis and normative disputes. Kinship, professional qualifications, or convenience of representation do not establish full standing to represent. Requirements for records must not be used to interrupt necessary support or force disclosure. See the Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, General Comment No. 1 (2014) and its 2018 corrigendum. For representation and authorization, see §5.5.7 “Delegation or Representation” and D.21.

## 16.13 Complex Trauma and the Absence of a Healthy Baseline (GOV-13) {#section-16-13}

Some structures have never had a healthy state. First distinguish four situations: a structure that was once healthier and later departed from that state; a nominal anchor that remains but has hollowed out in practice; a newly formed structure, which never developed a healthy anchor; and a trauma-built structure, whose rules, identities, boundaries, and relations were formed through trauma from the start.

For the latter two, do not ask “how to get back to the original state.” Ask instead what minimum safety means now, whether the subject is receiving predictable support for the first time, whether they can express themselves without inviting punishment, whether small acts of trust can be repeated, which survival strategies once protected them but are now becoming constraints, and whether a new anchor is waiting to be found or needs to be built together.

Treatment, clinical care, caregiving, child protection, or legal tasks requiring particular qualifications should be handled under the corresponding professional procedures. Expertise and credentials do not confer authority over the whole of someone’s life. Goals the person values, communication the person can understand, and the person’s objections and refusal must still be addressed in the relevant procedure. Emergency duties and authority over particular matters require independent grounds that apply to the case. The framework cannot infer them into existence. State their scope, validity period, review, and continuity of support separately. The framework cannot use its theory to invent a healthy past. See §1.3, §2.8, and §14.2 for limits on authority, and §15.3.8 for emergency tasks.

“Trauma-built” is a candidate description of the formation history of some structures. It does not designate complex post-traumatic stress disorder or another clinical diagnosis. Harm that calls for protection now can be recorded first. The formation history, particular psychological mechanisms, and professional classification each need evidence. Present harm cannot be used to infer that every relationship was formed by trauma. People do not have to establish that an ideal past once existed to receive support.

## 16.14 Keeping Open-Ended Undertaking, Normative Premises, and Metaphorical Drift Separate (GOV-14) {#section-16-14}

Love must not be treated as “the remainder that structure cannot explain” or as a favored label for a motive. The human variable “Open-Ended Undertaking Action” (§7.3.11) is used along several routes. To describe a bounded voluntary undertaking, take the route “a description of bounded voluntary undertaking.” First check the action, identifiable costs, direction, voluntariness, and genuine conditions for refusal, exit, or negotiation. Also check competing explanations such as interests, fear, benefits of identity, inertia, dependence, and coercion. The result can be realized, unrealized, harmful, or unknown. Whether the structural consequences succeed is not a condition for entering this route. Failed attempts must not disappear by definition.

To judge the causation of structural consequences, take the route “a causal judgment about structural consequences.” Whether the action changes safety, the ability to refuse, appeal, resources, or later possibilities for affected positions must be judged separately under the corresponding channel-effect test (§4.4) and the conditions for causal claims (§2.4). A beneficial result cannot establish voluntariness retroactively. Sincerity and high costs cannot substitute for evidence of effects. Normative approval of the action also requires disclosure of the normative premises adopted (§14.3), the protection floor (§14.8), and the costs borne by others affected. Whether the description holds, whether the effect holds, and whether the action deserves approval are three questions, each of which can be answered affirmatively or negatively. The variable also has a route, “a record of action and cost” (§7.3.11), which records only the facts of the action, costs, direction, risks of coercion, and gaps in the right to stop. Risks it reveals can generate requirements for protection directly, without waiting for the other two routes to receive support.

These routes constrain the framework’s judgments about particular undertakings and their consequences. They do not exhaust the meaning of love, care, or goodwill in general. Kindness at low cost, private companionship, creative attempts that did not succeed, and the value of ordinary relationships can matter in the appropriate context. Failure to meet the conditions of one of these routes does not negate the actor’s inner life or personality. Voluntarily bearing a limited cost does not create an unlimited duty to continue. Necessary arrangements for others to take over and release from responsibility must be justified separately for the matter at hand.

A broad utility representation can incorporate concern for others, identity, or commitments. Representing a choice as optimal does not by itself identify the reasons for acting or replace a judgment about its normative standing (see Sen, 1977). Filling in benefits after seeing choices so that any outcome becomes optimal cannot distinguish competing explanations. Exploratory extensions can be retained. A claim of new explanatory capability must state which outcomes the new model can distinguish relative to the old one, on what grounds, and what materials would make the explanation fail. See §2.4 and D.15.

Normative premises must be disclosed and referred to Part 14. The framework openly commits to protecting subjects with little power and rejecting uses of structural language that erase responsibility. It favors access to appeal and withdrawal and the continued development of successors able to take up the work. They do not follow automatically from facts as neutral conclusions.

A concept borrowed from another domain must have its status marked as metaphor, analogy, candidate mechanism, corroborating empirical evidence, formal model, or verified rule. “Resembles” must not be rewritten as “follows.” Cases from physics, biology, or animal behavior do not directly establish conclusions about human organizations or institutions. Each metaphor must specify similarities, differences, risks of misuse, and evidence that would lead to stopping its use.

## 16.15 Knowledge Lineage and Source Transparency (GOV-15) {#section-16-15}

For each concept that can be traced, record its intellectual affinities, specific source and version, what was retained, adapted, or abandoned, the risks of using it across cultures and domains, and what the source can and cannot support. Acknowledging intellectual affinities does not weaken the framework. It reduces mystification and dogmatism.

Theoretical material cannot substitute for case evidence, a single case for a universal rule, an internal summary for original records, or an AI summary for a traceable source. The framework establishes relations with other theories that allow them to be connected and used side by side. It does not present itself as an overarching framework that absorbs them all.

Connections among bodies of knowledge should preserve where disagreements arise. Some theories estimate different outcomes. Some propose competing mechanisms for the same outcome. Some adopt different value premises. Common naming must not erase these differences. When retaining a method, state what it adds to the current task that can be distinguished. If a domain method already suffices, it should be possible to use only that method.

## 16.16 Usage-Threshold Debt and Accessibility Review (GOV-16) {#section-16-16}

Greater complexity can help prevent crude application and can also create six kinds of usage-threshold debt. Learning requires time and cognitive resources. Translation between plain language and terminology loses information. Evidence can be difficult to collect safely. External review may be unavailable. The more people invest, the harder it can be to exit. The identity of someone who “knows the framework” can bring interpretive authority and social capital.

Accessibility requires more than adding a shorter version to the complete text. Provide plain language, ceilings for different levels of use, expression through a representative or with assistance, ways to submit nontextual material, equal weight for appeals, and a way for people to protect themselves before learning the whole framework. State explicitly when appropriate that people can first protect themselves, record facts, and seek support without learning the framework.

Without equivalent routes for appeal, protection, and withdrawal, the framework must not be used for substantively high-impact purposes or embedded in automated decisions. Public dissemination requires closer scrutiny in light of the object, factual allegations, identifiability, and consequences. Ordinary analysis still requires accessible means of correction and withdrawal. Mastery of terminology, complete reading, internal certification, or paid training does not constitute external eligibility.

Usage-threshold debt also includes the labor of maintainers and collaborators. Filling in more items, making more charts, or reading the whole text does not automatically produce understanding and fairness. The value of this labor must show up in clearer questions, counterexamples that are addressed, better limits on transfer, and the actual ability of people affected to correct results. People who need assistance expressing themselves should not first have to demonstrate independently the full ability to express themselves before receiving support that makes expression possible.

## 16.17 Tool Use, Commercialization, Weaponization, and Risks of Forking (GOV-17) {#section-16-17}

Courses, consulting, AI tools, organizational software, scorecards, review forms, and public standards that use the framework must retain evidence tracing, downgrading of judgments, protection for low-power positions, access to appeal, anti-retaliation, human review, stopping, and withdrawal. They must not do any of the following: present a lightweight template as a complete diagnosis; present internal certification as external review; present AI output as an independent investigation; treat customer satisfaction as evidence of effectiveness; force a strong diagnosis to make a delivery; let the paying party alone control evidence, feedback, and public framing; or let “framework certification” become a new monopoly on eligibility.

Users with sustained disagreements over core commitments may create a fork. A fork does not amount to betrayal. Actually using different frameworks under the same name without acknowledging a fork creates a greater risk. Each fork must state which of the four empirical questions it retains, which boundaries it abandons, which concepts it changes, and what new risks of misuse arise.

## 16.18 Retiring Open Assertions Captured by Power (GOV-18) {#section-16-18}

An open assertion is only a target for correction under uncertainty. It must be retired if any of the following occurs: it is repeatedly cited as a conclusion; it enters performance evaluation, records, discipline, eligibility, resource allocation, or public memory; it is used to suppress appeal; media, organizations, or AI reduce it to a label; under the name of “observe first,” it turns into prolonged exclusion and deprivation of resources; or the content of the judgment is retained while its withdrawal conditions are lost.

After retirement, state explicitly that action on its basis must cease. Restate the evidence limits, alternative explanations, and withdrawal conditions. Delete or flag records that have already been misused. If harm has occurred, proceed to repair, compensation, and examination of the chain of responsibility. If misuse cannot be stopped, the framework must decline to endorse the use.

## 16.19 Version Write-Back and the Feedback Loop (GOV-19) {#section-16-19}

The version log is the authoritative place where counterexamples and governance decisions are written back. It is not an appendix to release notes. For every counterexample, narrowed boundary, downgraded judgment, suspension, block, withdrawal, replacement, retirement, restoration, or remedy, record the event, time, responsible party, which entries are affected, previous and new statuses, evidence, counterexamples, consequences for the text, consequences for the procedure, consequences for the action ceiling, and downstream write-back.

Archiving counterexamples without effective review does not complete a correction loop. A valid counterexample should change the affected text, procedure, or action ceiling. A counterexample that does not hold can be declined for reasons, but those grounds and a way to review them must be preserved. A prolonged absence of actual handling of feedback may trigger a review of whether the channel has failed. Absence of changes in the log establishes neither perfection nor, by itself, failure. If the log contains errors, correct them with new entries and retain the old entries and traces of failure.

An entry in the version log cannot be merely a line of text that mentions some change in general terms. Every entry must correspond clearly to one specific governance decision and state what the decision concerns, from which version to which version the change goes, what change of status occurred, what the previous and new statuses are, and what kind of event it is. The starting point of a change must be the version actually in force at the time. The version after the change must actually be newer than the original. Material within the same batch cannot prove that a change took place through cross-references among gaps, aliases, or fabricated versions. For a change to be recorded as “adopted,” it must also include at least one actual modification of the text, a procedure, or the action ceiling, and it must be possible to verify that the content before and after the modification actually differs. This shows that actual write-back has taken place. Review, the effects of objections, and downstream repair must all correspond to the same log entry.

The following must be recorded separately, and how they correspond needs to be verifiable: the identity of the text, the version of the theory, the version of the structured conventions, the version of the software, and the version actually read in use. Renaming the theory or moving to a new major version does not reset the software version automatically or establish that installed content has migrated with it. Writing back the text, building the software, reading the version in use, producing answers that meet requirements, and benefiting a real task have distinct points of verification.

The endpoint of governance feedback should match the problem. A typo correction concerns the authoritative text and its propagation. Correcting a numerical value in the source also requires checking the calculations that depend on it. Use beyond authority must be traced to actual execution and remedy. Upholding a correct decision for stated reasons can constitute adequate review. If an erroneous decision is overturned but the change has yet to be carried out, only the changed decision is confirmed. The version log and other records of changes that can be checked can show where these changes occurred. They cannot substitute for the meaning of the changes or for their real effects.

## 16.20 External Review Packages and Checks Before Public Release (GOV-20) {#section-16-20}

Internal stress testing is an internal wind tunnel for finding misuse of terms, misjudgment of paths, and inconsistencies. It must not be used to establish that the framework is reliable in reality or better than alternatives, or for marketing or certification.

An external review package includes the assertions to review, a traceability table, publication status, known defects, review questions and criteria, reviewer identities and conflicts of interest, feedback write-back, and protection for reviewers. Review types include source tracing, review by people in affected positions, review by domain experts, red-team review for misuse, review of public language, and review of the checks before public release. If a kind of review remains absent for a long time, especially if review by people in affected positions stays empty, the absence itself blocks publication.

Reviewers cannot establish that they are “external” by claiming it themselves. Reviewers, members who take part in the decision, the bodies that make the decision, the people who issue authorization, the scope of authorization, subjects, the version concerned, validity periods, and relationships of interest must all be confirmable in records that can be checked. External approval also needs to be given explicitly for this particular governance change: what it concerns, which version the change leads to, how the status changes, and what objections existed at the time all need to match this change. For a change to be recorded as “adopted,” its only basis can be an external review whose conclusion is “approved.” An alias for the same person, an internal stamp, or authorization issued to oneself cannot substitute for it.

Every strong judgment for public release must pass each of the nine checks before public release: complete traceability, source quality, handling of counterexamples, affected positions, shifts in burden, adversarial testing for misuse, public language, external review, and the strong-judgment kit (§13.5). If any item is not passed, the judgment must not be published as a strong judgment. Passing all of them yields only the status “publishable.” It still confers no authorization to act.

### 16.20.1 Organizing Methods and Reviewing a Sample {#section-16-20-1}

Instructions for use and reference entries organized from the original text may be reviewed by sampling within a stated scope. Before sampling, state the population, categories, selection basis, and question to be judged. Cover different mechanisms, objects, and output types. Include entries concerning high-impact use, conversion across scales, or theoretical boundaries. A small convenience sample cannot represent categories it does not cover.

If review finds omitted concepts, widened scope of application, or confusion between the original and derived material, expand review of the relevant categories and trace the methods and conclusions that depend on the entry. Sampling results must distinguish entries actually reviewed from those not reviewed individually. When a decision is made to adopt the entire batch, give the grounds and scope of that decision separately. Permission to sample, passing procedural checks, or finding no errors so far in the sample does not amount to item-by-item confirmation by the author. These cannot substitute for substantive review of real-world high-impact judgments.

An empirical judgment offered by the author that serves as a theoretical method should be written into the relevant part of the original text, with its applicable objects, grounds, counterexamples, and stopping conditions. Editorial suggestions that have not been written into the original text remain candidates. Supplementary method entries must not be used to create a separate theory attributed to the author. Being written into the original text changes only its status in the text. It does not automatically turn personal experience into a universal empirical proposition or proof of effectiveness.

## 16.21 Benign Demise of the Framework and Transition to a Replacement (GOV-21) {#section-16-21}

Maturity for a framework includes knowing when to exit. Explaining everything is no criterion of maturity. Benign demise need not mean that the entire text disappears. A concept, a module, or the four empirical questions can leave the core. Five kinds of situation can trigger it. A module is persistently superseded by a simpler, more falsifiable method with less unintended harm. A concept increases the cost of understanding without improving diagnosis. The four empirical questions continually rely on exceptions in a key domain. Users suppress outside theories or real counterexamples to preserve the framework. Gains from the identity, commercial value, or power that the framework brings exceed its gains in judgment quality.

When replacement occurs, the following must be stated: how far this framework can judge, where it starts to fail, what the successor method takes over, how the two cross-check each other, what records and unresolved harms need to be handed off, who is responsible for the handoff, and when it takes effect. The decisions that can be made are: narrow the boundary, suspend, enter a replacement comparison, replace, retire, or hand off. The framework’s creator, maintainers, or commercial beneficiaries must not veto replacement unilaterally or preserve it indefinitely by adding concepts without limit.

The stronger the framework’s explanatory power, the more important it is to preserve withdrawal. Greater publication capability requires greater protection for counterexamples from low-power positions. Greater automation requires treating AI output as auxiliary material. No structural diagnosis can erase a person’s particular circumstances or dissolve responsibility into complexity, stages, potential fields, entropy increase, or love.

## 16.22 Multi-Circle Complexity and Governance of Foresight (GOV-22) {#section-16-22}

Multi-circle capability creates five kinds of governance debt. Object debt arises when circles are treated as entities because they have names. Variable debt arises when candidates drawn from residuals are automatically filled in as facts. Complexity debt arises when variables and paths keep multiplying without out-of-sample gains. Calibration debt arises when forecasts are published without their results being recorded. Power debt arises when predictions are used for screening, monitoring, punishment, or manipulation. If any kind of debt exceeds the threshold set in advance, the framework should reduce the model, downgrade the output, suspend high-impact use, or retire the relevant module.

Forecast Registration must retain all results: successes, failures, unresolved results, invalidated targets, and results that cannot be evaluated. Do not publish only hits. External reviewers can check model versions, input cutoffs, simple baselines, metrics, and outcome write-back. A claim of foresight capability is automatically downgraded if any of the following occurs: calibration deteriorates over the long term; errors concentrate in particular groups; the complex model ceases to outperform the baseline; or results continue to depend on variables added only after the fact.

## 16.23 Governing Personality, Privacy, and Overreach by Automation (GOV-23) {#section-16-23}

Personality hypotheses carry a high risk of misuse. Governance must check collection purposes, authorization, privacy level, permitted uses, whether there is propagation across circles, appeals by people concerned, deletion or correction, model versions, and downstream impacts. The following uses are prohibited without exception: clinical diagnosis, adjudicating the essence of a personality, discriminatory screening, unauthorized risk scoring, and using silence as evidence.

AI can propose and compare candidate variables and paths, verify the identity of materials, form judgments that can be refuted, and offer recommendations under publicly stated normative premises. It cannot certify reality through its own generated content, diagnose personality, decide values for others, or issue permissions. If any automated system promotes “candidate” to “observed,” promotes “simulated” to a real-world event, or promotes foresight results to authorization, it must be blocked from carrying out further actions, and a record that can be checked must be kept. Human review is effective only under these conditions: reviewers can see evidence, counterexamples, and uncertainty; they have the relevant capability, time available in advance, and a manageable workload; and they can actually refuse, modify, or suspend an action. The refusals, modifications, and suspensions that reviewers issue must also reach the point of actual execution in time to change what is done. A click in an interface alone is insufficient.

Whether human oversight is effective should be assessed by observing together whether correct recommendations are adopted appropriately, whether incorrect ones are intercepted, whether problems without prompts are detected, and whether affected people can correct results. People overriding machine recommendations more often is not necessarily better, and complete compliance does not establish reliability. Some narrowly scoped actions are faster than people can judge them one at a time. For such actions, constraints checked by machines and minimum permissions can be studied. Responsibility for choosing goals, for constraints that were omitted, for taking over when something abnormal occurs, and for remedies must still be explicit. System-wide risks must not all be shifted to the person who clicks last.

## 16.24 Model Degradation, Retirement, and Replacement (GOV-24) {#section-16-24}

The framework must have mechanisms for retirement and replacement. If a simpler method consistently performs better in explanation, foresight, protection, and accessibility, hand off cases, definitions and conventions, records of failure, and appeals under the handoff arrangements set out in the governance clause “Benign Demise of the Framework and Transition to a Replacement” (§16.21). Preserving the existing framework is no objective in itself. At retirement, retain necessary source chains, records of responsibility, and remedies for harm. Stop using the retired model for new decisions and disclose which conclusions remain valid, which are downgraded, and which cannot be transferred.

Replacement comparisons must assess at least judgment quality and total burden together. First fix the task, materials, input cutoff, resources, and method of evaluation. Retain a sufficiently strong domain method and accurate direct citations for comparison. Then check misjudgments, omissions, unwarranted abstention, time, disclosure, and maintenance labor separately. Entry of a candidate into the text establishes only editorial adoption. If several candidates from the same source share one change, the gains from adoption cannot be counted once per candidate. Line breaks, character counts, and file counts can be verified. Those counts cannot be used to measure reader costs or work outcomes.

Without an actual comparison, do not claim that the framework is better than alternatives, that the two have been shown to be equivalent, or that the framework is useless. Preserve existing historical comparisons according to their actual versions, tasks, and denominators that count failures. Local improvements can hold while regressions in other dimensions and delivery failures still have to be shown. When new theory, software, and materials change together, a comparison can evaluate the combination. Differences that the combination produces cannot all be attributed to one theoretical statement or one form of packaging.

## 16.25 Context-Specific Parameters and Missing Information {#section-16-25}

External procedures in each setting must determine the following parameters: who participates in consensus and its time limits; the observation windows, samples, and decision makers needed to resume use of the four empirical questions; safe channels, custodians, and remedial institutions for weak signals; risk weights among the items missing from AI outputs; and criteria for replacement comparisons across disciplines, cultures, and jurisdictions. Whenever these parameters are set down, the organization, jurisdiction, object, and risk level to which they apply must be stated, together with the evidence they rest on, their validity period, and the conditions for review. Numbers from one setting must not be copied into universal thresholds. If this information is missing, the conclusion remains downgraded, suspended, or under replacement comparison. The framework must not fill it in itself.

A missing parameter blocks only judgments and uses that depend on it. Other conclusions with independent grounds are retained within their own scope. Keep unknowns preserved for protection, lack of the ability to observe, genuine inapplicability, and outstanding evidence needs separate. In actual use, proportionality should also be assessed by comparing two things: whether the additional records can change the judgment and reduce harm, and how much delay, exposure, and maintenance cost they create.
