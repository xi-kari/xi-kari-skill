# Part 13 Interfaces and Tools {#part-title-13}

This Part converts the objects, propositions, scales, mechanisms, actors, multi-circle joint states, and dynamic projection requirements defined in the preceding 12 Parts into limited judgments that can be observed, questioned, reviewed, and withdrawn. It adds no facts about the world. Completing a form does not establish a fact. A D0 candidate object record and a nine-axis scale record must exist before the tool workflow begins. If the object, boundary, scale, time window, identity criterion, or intended use changes, existing judgments must not simply be carried over.

The tool workflow has two levels. The seven gates are the only main workflow. The five gates and 13 steps elaborate the diagnosis[^tn-diagnosis-14]. The seven gates determine whether a judgment can proceed to the next level. In complex cases the five gates and 13 steps spell out the material that must be submitted. They do not issue a separate “pass” that competes with the seven gates or produce a second set of workflow statuses.

The tool layer confers no authorization. Even when the workflow, evidence, and strong-judgment kit are all complete, the outputs are limited to descriptions, candidate mechanisms, comparative judgments, strong-judgment candidates, or requirements for action. Real-world action still requires a separate structured selection record, the 10-component C12 bridge, one and the same valid atomic J authorization, and O1–O4. A tool output cannot substitute for any of them.

The following workflow governs structured judgments formed under this framework and their handoff. Reading, checking against the original text, and clarifying a low-impact question can use concise records suited to the task. Skipping the full tool workflow is no basis for declaring that established facts in the domain do not exist. Formal diagnosis, foresight, or high-impact use requires the corresponding full contract[^tn-contract-14]. Renaming an output cannot reduce the responsibilities it carries.

</div>

## 13.1 Four Conversions {#section-13-1}

Each conversion adds a responsibility for recording. Replacing terms from the theory with labels for real-world situations does not accomplish this. If any step fails, the outputs that depend on it stop before that step. A later step’s completed fields cannot make up for the failure. Other independent judgments are reviewed separately on their own grounds and intended uses.

| ID | Conversion | Required Result | Where to Stop If It Fails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IT1 | Structural variables to observable signals | A record of signals that specifies the object, scale, observation position, time window, measurement protocol, and missing-data status | A conceptual mapping or a gap in the material |
| IT2 | Observable signals to strength of evidence | An evidence record that separates sources, observations, comparison conditions, coverage, counterexamples, alternative explanations, and uncertainty | Ungraded material or a hypothesis to test |
| IT3 | Strength of evidence to level of judgment | A judgment with a proposition contract, limits of applicability, contrary conditions, falsification conditions, and withdrawal conditions | A candidate explanation or an observational assessment |
| IT4 | Level of judgment to requirements and ceiling for action | A list of affected positions, the protection floor, prohibited actions, stopping, appeal, rollback, and outstanding N/J/O/C12 requirements | Description, a need for more evidence, or a requirement to take no action |

IT1 prevents a narrative about structure from being treated as an observed fact. IT2 prevents citations, materials, or declarations of position from being treated directly as evidence. IT3 prevents correlation, analogy, or one narrative of how a mechanism works from being elevated to a conclusion. IT4 prevents a leap from a factual judgment to action. Passing all four conversions leaves authorization status unchanged.

If any IT conversion fails, dependent later conversions may only be marked `not_run`. The propositions, comparative judgments, strong-judgment candidates, and requirements for action that they support must be stopped or downgraded. Downstream conclusions that share the same gap must not regain their ceiling through renaming, splitting, or opening another branch. A later `not_run` only preserves the chain of failure. Completing the kit cannot repair the break. A judgment may retain its original standing if it does not depend on the failed step, has independent grounds, and its use meets its own requirements. If the gap affects the overall source status, a shared factual premise, the object boundary, or lawful conditions for use of the entire task, stop or downgrade all dependent outputs together. If a gate required for high-impact adoption fails, calling the same judgment an ordinary opinion does not allow it to retain its original use.

## 13.2 Using the Universal Primitives and Human Variable Interfaces (11+11) {#section-13-2}

HV01–HV11 in Part 7 are the 11 interfaces for the human structured world. U01–U11 in Part 3 are the universal structural primitives. The tool layer can use both sets in the same case. Matching numbers, equal counts, or similar questions do not establish one-to-one equivalence.

At minimum, record the universal or human interface ID, effective object, nine-axis scale, selected support route, instance evidence, alternative explanations, missing-data status, judgment ceiling, and action ceiling. H1/H4/H5 may enter the specified routes only through the corresponding H-instance in each case. G1–G4 can support empirical claims only through a G-instance frozen before results. An interface card can raise a question. Completing its fields cannot establish facts about the object.

## 13.3 The Seven Gates as the Only Main Workflow {#section-13-3}

Run the seven gates for each specific proposition in dependency order. After one gate fails, dependent later gates may only be marked `not_run`, and the corresponding judgments and requirements for action stop or are downgraded. A later `not_run` cannot raise the ceiling lowered by the failed gate. Acknowledging that the object or evidence failed a gate while allowing dependent later procedures to report their own pass is also prohibited. Judgments that do not depend on the failed step must retain independent evidence and undergo review of their intended use. Another proposition passing cannot conceal this proposition’s failure. If overall source status, a shared factual premise, the object boundary, or lawful conditions for use of the task cease to hold, all dependent outputs stop together. When the object, evidence version, affected positions, or intended use changes substantially, rerun the workflow from the earliest gate affected.

| ID | Gate | Core Question | Output Ceiling If It Does Not Pass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| IG1 | Effective Object Gate | Do the object, boundary, nine-axis scale, time window, and K support the current judgment? | Redefine the object, leave the scale unknown, or review object conversion |
| IG2 | Evidence Traceability Gate | Are sources, materials, observations, evidence, counterexamples, cases, and judgments distinct and traceable? | Organization of materials, candidate questions, or hypotheses to test |
| IG3 | Affected Positions Gate | Which positions will see their circumstances change through direct, indirect, second-order, cross-domain, or intertemporal effects? | Supplement the accounting of impacts. Suspend strong judgments and high-impact uses |
| IG4 | Power and Anti-Retaliation Gate | Can people in low-power positions safely add evidence, rebut claims, refuse, stop, and appeal? | Draw no adverse inference from silence. Lower the levels of publication and judgment |
| IG5 | Mediation and Reflexivity Gate | Do metrics, platforms, AI, public evaluation, or the act of diagnosis change the evidence and the object? | High reflexivity remains unresolved. Restate the judgment as a conditional state transition |
| IG6 | Strong-Judgment Procedure Gate | Have all SJ1–SJ8 items been submitted for the proposition whose intended use and foreseeable impacts trigger the strong-judgment procedure? Public dissemination requires closer scrutiny. It does not by itself elevate all analyses to strong judgments | Stop the relevant strong judgments and high-impact uses. Review separately any comparative judgment that stands independently and meets the requirements for its use |
| IG7 | Action Ceiling Gate | What is the most that may be required? Which actions are prohibited, and when are stopping, appeal, and rollback called for? | Description, gaps in evidence, no action, or continued deliberation |

Passing all seven gates indicates only that the applicable recording requirements have no gaps. It does not establish that the conclusion is true. Empirical propositions are still judged under their proposition contracts and instance evidence. Normative objectives still require explicit N premises. Real-world action still requires choice, C12, J, and O procedures. In particular, IG7 can only record whether the external normative and authorization layers meet their requirements. The tool workflow cannot mark an action as “authorized” on its own.

IG6 has a specific applicability rule. An ordinary L1/L2 output may mark it `not_applicable`, but the record must state why the strong-judgment procedure is not triggered in this case. This does not block IG7. Once IG6 is marked `passed`, the same run record must link a complete SJ1–SJ8, with every item marked `passed`. A “checked” string or an empty rationale cannot substitute for the eight results.

The scope of identification for IG3 is determined under §14.4.1. Positions that section requires to be listed in full must be listed in full; for other positions, the record must state how far they were traced and why tracing stopped. For explanation and diagnosis tasks without a comparison of options, the test is whether further tracing could change the conclusion or its grade, the scope of use, or protective arrangements.

## 13.4 The Five Gates and 13 Steps for Elaborating a Diagnosis {#section-13-4}

Run the five gates and 13 steps when the object is complex, responsibility is high, reflexivity is high, or the seven gates call for more detailed evidence. The five gates are local checks within this elaboration. They do not generate a second set of main workflow statuses.

| ID | Elaboration Gate | Material That Must Be Developed | Corresponding Main Gates |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FG1 | Object Gate | Object, boundary, scale, and time window | IG1 |
| FG2 | Evidence Gate | Cost of signals, sources, weights, and review | IG2 |
| FG3 | Scale Gate | Nine-axis scale, objects and evidence at the scale, and changes in scale | IG1, IG2 |
| FG4 | Responsibility Gate | Effects on rights/resources, contrary conditions, repair windows, appeals, and review | IG3, IG4, IG6 |
| FG5 | Observation Gate | Baseline before observation, reactions during observation, reactions after publication, and a ceiling on recursion | IG5 |

The 13 steps form an auditable sequence from the real-world question to write-back.

| ID | Step | Minimum Deliverable |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DS01 | Start With the Question | A real-world question in language outside the framework, the requester, and the intended use |
| DS02 | Identify the Object and Boundary | Effective object, subsystems, higher-level environment, and external channels |
| DS03 | Separate Signal Types | Separate columns for facts, behavior, resources, feedback, narratives, feelings, conjecture, and noise |
| DS04 | Record Participation in Observation | Awareness of being observed, observer power, impacts of publication, and candidate responses |
| DS05 | Identify the Dominant Constraint | Candidate constraints, the selected constraint, evidence for selection, and competing constraints |
| DS06 | Record the State and Window | References to S0–S6 prototypes for applicable objects, positions within the state, parallel subsystems, and contrary conditions. If inapplicable, state why and retain the domain’s own states and windows |
| DS07 | Examine the Core Structure | Anchor, uptake layer, transmission chain, boundary, and feedback write-back |
| DS08 | Identify Key Variables for Protection | Variables to protect, paths to harm, resources, redundancy, and paths for repair/return flows |
| DS09 | Record Uptake and Repayment | Cost bearers, recipients of return flows, rights to stop, long-term overextension, and shifting of harms |
| DS10 | Identify Hidden Risks and Boundary Types | Maintenance debt, mismatches between fast and slow processes, metric substitution, distorted summaries, and candidate object conversions |
| DS11 | Propose Candidate Mechanisms | Competing mechanisms that the material permits and that could change the judgment, the evidence each requires, evidence that weakens them, and rules for eliminating them. If no plausible competing explanation exists, state the scope of the search and the failure conditions. Do not invent mechanisms to meet a quota |
| DS12 | Anticipate Branches | Triggers, early signals, contrary conditions, repair windows, and minimum requirements for action |
| DS13 | Produce the Output and Write Back | Output in ordinary language, triggers for revision, scope of the record, review, withdrawal, and rollback |

When DS06 uses S0–S6, it must do so only for human intentional collectives to which the prototypes apply. The prototypes must not be forced onto other objects. S0–S6 must not be treated as levels of maturity or as fate. Identifying an important variable for protection in DS08 can generate only a requirement for protection. Identifying uptake or overextension in DS09 cannot generate a duty to make sacrifices or establish full responsibility. Candidate mechanisms in DS11 must not bypass the causal contract. Branches in DS12 do not describe a certain future. If DS13 lacks review and withdrawal procedures, the judgment must not be entered into organizational records, eligibility, allocation, or public memory.

## 13.5 The Strong-Judgment Kit {#section-13-5}

Strong judgments are defined by their uses and foreseeable impacts. Tone, length, and confidence of expression do not determine the category. A judgment enters the strong-judgment procedure if it is intended or reasonably foreseeable for use in real-world action or decisions about eligibility, rights, or resource allocation. The same applies if it has a material impact on a specific subject’s reputation and place in public memory, or if it is embedded in automated, organizational, or institutional decisions. Check the actual use of intermediate scores, recommendations, or material screening when they affect these decisions downstream. Public dissemination calls for greater scrutiny. Assess it in light of the object evaluated, factual allegations, identifiability, reach, and consequences. Publication alone is insufficient to raise every analytical output to a strong judgment. Ordinary work can reach definite conclusions when evaluating arguments, explaining mechanisms, or articulating normative views, provided the uses and impacts above are absent and the evidence and review meet the task’s requirements. Calling an output commentary, research, or a draft does not exempt it if it actually meets the strong-judgment conditions. A strong-judgment candidate may be registered only at L3, with all seven gates passed and each of the following eight items passed individually. It still confers no authorization to act in reality.

| ID | Component | Required Question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SJ1 | Contrary conditions | What circumstances would weaken, reverse, or lead to withdrawal of the judgment? |
| SJ2 | Repair window | Where is the window, what supports it, and when does it cease to hold? |
| SJ3 | Evidence requirements | What evidence supports the judgment, what evidence is missing, and what evidence weighs against it? |
| SJ4 | Access to appeal | Who can file an appeal, who is responsible, what is the time limit, and can the outcome change? |
| SJ5 | Anti-retaliation protection | Which activities are protected, how is protection monitored, and how can retaliation be reported independently and remedied? |
| SJ6 | Right to add evidence | How can evidence be supplemented, versions corrected, and downstream records synchronized? |
| SJ7 | Trigger for external review | When is review triggered, who reviews independently, and can they access the materials and stop the result from taking effect? |
| SJ8 | Rollback and write-back | Which state is restored, who carries out the repair, which version is authoritative, and how are updates synchronized? |

If any item is missing, IG6 fails for that strong judgment. The judgment must not enter the category of strong-judgment candidate or retain its original high-impact use. It may be recorded only as an internal hypothesis, an open assertion, or a candidate mechanism within the limits of its materials and actual use. An L2 comparative judgment may remain if it does not depend on the missing item, has independent grounds, and does not trigger a strong-judgment use. Failure of a common premise still blocks all dependent judgments. Renaming or splitting must not be used to evade that limit. Access to appeal does not count as an effective appeal if it cannot change a record or action. Being external in name does not establish independence. A statement, apology, or release of a new version does not amount to actual rollback and repair.

## 13.6 Diagnostic Levels {#section-13-6}

| ID | Level | When It Applies | Output Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| L1 | Lightweight description and exploratory questions | Low complexity, low impact, reversibility, and limited information | Description, questions, and the next observation |
| L2 | Standard reviewable judgment | A structural comparison is needed, but the judgment will not be used for action carrying high responsibility | A comparative judgment within stated limits or a candidate mechanism |
| L3 | Strong-judgment candidate carrying high responsibility | Judgments with substantively high impact or embedded in automated, organizational, or institutional decisions. Public dissemination is reviewed in light of the object, factual allegations, identifiability, and consequences | A strong-judgment candidate after the seven gates and the kit pass |

A higher level adds responsibility for recording and does not make a judgment “closer to the truth.” The level must be downgraded if evidence coverage declines, the object undergoes conversion, reflexivity becomes uncontrolled, or appeals or anti-retaliation protection fail. Urgency does not permit omitting the object, protection, or stopping conditions. When waiting is costly, the tool may only propose the narrowest, withdrawable, monitorable requirement for action, separately from the applicable authorization.

## 13.7 Six Formal Tools {#section-13-7}

| ID | Tool | Purpose and Limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TOOL-EVIDENCE-LEDGER` | Source–Evidence Ledger | Keep sources, materials, observations, evidence, counterexamples, coverage, and uncertainty separate. It does not prove a proposition |
| `TOOL-OPEN-ASSERTION` | Open Assertion Record | Retain a provisional judgment that can be questioned, falsified, or withdrawn. Do not use it for action carrying high responsibility |
| `TOOL-CLAIM-VALIDATION` | Proposition Validation Form | Register proposition type, evidence, counterevidence, falsification conditions, impacts, and ceilings separately |
| `TOOL-FORECAST-REGISTRY` | Forecast Registration | Freeze the prediction, window, triggers, early signals, contrary signals, and targets for revision |
| `TOOL-STRESS-TEST` | Counterexample and Scale Stress Test | Use boundary cases, adversarial explanations, and changes in scale to locate failures and narrow the scope |
| `TOOL-AI-BOUNDARY` | AI Output Boundaries | Require disclosure of sources, identification of missing material and the responsible human, and recording of uncertainty, prohibited uses, review, and withdrawal |

The evidence ledger must not turn “has a source” into “is supported.” An open assertion cannot substitute for proposition validation. A failed prediction must be written back to the relevant proposition. An internal stress test is only a wind tunnel and does not supply external empirical support. Tools may cite one another’s record IDs, but must not validate one another circularly.

Automatic validation is suited to checking closed fields, whether references resolve, consistency of versions and statuses, and whether calculations can be reproduced. It cannot replace assessment of materials, testimony from people affected, causal identification, or choices about values. Programs should do the work that can be done mechanically so that readers can examine the actual grounds for judgments. Complete logs and clear prose can be kept separately with references between them.

</div>

## 13.8 Limits on the Use of AI {#section-13-8}

AI can organize materials, check fields, compare versions, generate candidate questions, list alternative explanations, and find inconsistencies in formatting. Where the evidence and normative premises allow, it should also compare mechanisms, form refutable judgments, recommend options, and draft complete institutional arrangements, plans, and commentary. AI must not invent sources, treat information recalled from training as current evidence, express consent on someone’s behalf, interpret silence as consent, take the place of a deciding subject or authorizing authority, or hide uncertainty. It must not make high-risk real-world decisions independently.

AI outputs must state what materials were used, what is missing, which content is observation/inference/analogy, who is responsible for the final judgment, how it may be used, and when it is to be reviewed or withdrawn. Where disclosure of identity, retaliation risk, or justified opacity is involved, protective omissions must remain distinguishable from “no evidence.”

Responsibility for AI use should also reflect the actual division of work among those who provide materials, decide objectives and permissions, maintain systems, carry out actions, and review results. Facilities and procedures that perform technical steps do not become subjects capable of bearing moral or legal responsibility merely because they output recommendations or execute instructions. Organizations legally capable of bearing a specified responsibility must be assessed separately according to their institutional status and the matter at issue. Greater automation does not put all responsibility on the person who clicks last. Reviewers must have sufficient materials, time, capability, and authority to stop actions in practice. The organization must also provide the resources needed for these conditions.

## 13.9 Run Records and Failure Semantics {#section-13-9}

Each tool run creates a `flow_execution_record` with a closed set of fields. It registers object and scale records, the L1–L3 level, IT1–IT4 results, IG1–IG7 results, whether the five gates and 13 steps were run, whether SJ1–SJ8 were triggered, evidence, alternative explanations, uncertainty, output categories, judgment and action ceilings, normative handoff, appeal, rollback, and status.

The action ceiling uses a closed lattice of output ceilings: `description_only`, `diagnostic_only`, and `requirements_only`. Each level has fixed permitted output categories and has `can_authorize=false` and `can_execute=false`. The caller must not claim “already authorized” in free text or expand the permitted categories on its own. `action_requirements` only specifies conditions for the external choice and authorization procedures. It is not an executable command.

A normative handoff must link to verifiable external choice, C12, and authorization records, or state explicitly that these are missing. References must specify the object, action, scope, time, and actual grounds for verification. Vague labels, self-reports of having passed, or circular references to the same record cannot establish permission. The implementation determines the reference format. The tool is still prohibited from issuing its own authorization.

Outcome statuses must remain distinct. `passed` means the recording requirements at that gate were met. `failed` means the step ran and failed. `not_run` means it was not run because of an earlier gate or a scope restriction. `unknown`, `not_applicable`, `not_observable`, and `withheld_for_protection` remain four distinct missing-data statuses. Failure must not be rewritten as inapplicability. Concealment for protection must not be rewritten as absence.

The run record’s `authorization_effect` is always `none`. Even when external references for normative choice, C12, J, and O exist, this record only checks them and hands them off. It does not issue authorization. All reviewed outputs must retain an accessible appeal and a means of actual withdrawal or correction. A tool conclusion that cannot be withdrawn must not be put to a high-impact use that is difficult to reverse.

## 13.10 The Nine Gates for Multi-Circle Projection {#section-13-10}

The seven gates are the only main workflow for explanation and diagnosis. When a task explicitly calls for dynamic projection, conditional foresight, or bounded choice, elaborate that workflow with the nine gates. The nine gates must not bypass the seven gates’ requirements for the object, evidence, affected positions, power, reflexivity, the strong-judgment procedure, and the action ceiling.

| Gate | Core Check | Output on Failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DF1 Freeze the Facts | Event type, source, time, evidence cutoff, and disputes | List of factual questions |
| DF2 Joint Object | Actors, circles, relations, membership, and K | Candidate groupings or a return to a single focus |
| DF3 Two Channels and Clocks | Material and experience–meaning conditions relevant to the task, clocks, and unknowns. Mark irrelevant items inapplicable with a reason | Retain the state snapshot. Suspend propagation that depends on missing conditions and review independent paths separately |
| DF4 Propagation Through Mechanisms | Channels, thresholds, delay, feedback, and cascades | Candidate mechanisms and observations that can distinguish them |
| DF5 Branching Paths | Conditions, parent and child nodes, and early and contrary signals | Parallel paths without forced ordering |
| DF6 Forecast Registration | Target, horizon, simple baseline, calibration, and write-back | Scenario projection only |
| DF7 Normative Choice | N, PF, affected people, options, and no action | Suspend recommendations blocked by the gap. Retain evaluations that stand independently, conditional recommendations under explicit premises, and requirements for more evidence. This confers no permission to execute |
| DF8 Authorization and Execution | J, O, responsibility, stopping, rollback, and remedy | External handoff without execution |
| DF9 Outcome Write-Back | Results, comparison with the baseline, deviations, downgrades, and retirement | Retain the failure and open a new version |

The tool can propose candidate circles at DF2 and variable candidates at DF5. Every candidate retains the status `candidate`. If DF6 does not pass, outputs must not use forecast language such as “will happen” or “expected to prove accurate.” If DF7 or DF8 does not pass, outputs must not give executable instructions. DF9 does not permit overwriting the original run.

## 13.11 Limits on AI in Dynamic Runs {#section-13-11}

AI can organize materials, check original passages that can be located, find gaps in fields, generate and compare competing paths, check contract consistency, and calculate registered metrics. Under explicit normative premises it can also form well-grounded local judgments and recommendations and draft complete texts. AI cannot certify a real-world event solely by its own generation, self-report, or internal procedure. It cannot diagnose personality, disclose information on behalf of those concerned, impose circle boundaries on others through its own classifications, treat simulations as facts, settle disputed value premises for others, or issue authorization. Whether external material supports a particular fact must be judged separately according to the material’s status, method, counterexamples, and intended use. Faithfulness to a source does not automatically establish that what it says about the world is true. For a variable automatically proposed by AI, the system must record its status as a model candidate, the minimal test, and prohibited uses. It must not be upgraded before the corresponding external evidence is available.

</div>

### 13.11.1 Checking AI Materials and the Chain of Tool Actions {#section-13-11-1}

When checking a generated answer, examine separately whether the source exists, whether the citation details are correct, whether the original passage supports the sentence, and whether the fact asserted in the sentence has adequate grounds. A source’s silence on the matter does not establish that the sentence is wrong. Accurately restating a mistaken source does not establish the facts. Where local knowledge, oral accounts, or protected materials have no public webpage, retain appropriate means of verification and limits on public disclosure. They must not be treated as nonexistent on that basis alone.

Retrieval augmentation can improve access to materials and performance on a task. Consistency or uncertainty checks can also detect certain errors. Their effectiveness remains limited by retrieval coverage, shared sources, and the types of error. An error repeated reliably can appear consistent. Several models repeating the same inaccurate material do not supply several independent pieces of evidence. A formal checker can confirm a derivation under stated premises, but cannot establish that those premises hold in reality.

For automated action, record the recommendation, tool request, application execution, external state, and consequences separately. The model writing “stopped” does not establish that the executor received a stop instruction. An interface showing “deleted” does not establish that downstream copies are gone. A person changing a recommendation does not establish that the change took effect before an irreversible action. Permissions for actual protection and correction must be exercisable in the corresponding system.

### 13.11.2 Task Endpoints and a Verifiable Execution Chain {#section-13-11-2}

The endpoint for verifying a tool chain is determined by the user’s request or the institutional task. If the request is only for a draft, delivery can be complete when the draft has been saved correctly, can be opened, and its content meets the requirements. If the task requires changing an external record, success returned by the tool must still be confirmed by the state of the corresponding external object. If the task requires actual remedy, verify that the resources, rights, or services reach those concerned. Actual completion of a narrower endpoint need not wait for every consequence in people’s lives. A success message in the interface cannot substitute for completion of a broader endpoint.

| Stage | Minimum Object of Verification | Typical Break |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form the recommendation | Objective, materials, object to be changed, and permitted scope | Treating generated text as already adopted |
| Make the tool request | Specific action, parameters, current object version, and permissions | Sending a correct recommendation to the wrong object |
| Execute in the application | Whether the request was received, executed, or rejected, and how repeated requests are handled | A request arrives but is queued, fails, or is executed repeatedly |
| Check external state | Whether the target and dependent objects changed as intended | A local marker changes while actual execution still reads the old state |
| Check the task outcome | The specified benefit or protection endpoint and remaining problems | A corrected record takes effect, but the missed opportunity is not restored |

For concurrent operations across tools, first distinguish operations that can read independently, operations that can modify independently, and operations that write to the same object. Changes to shared state must preserve ordering, version prerequisites, or conflict handling. Before an irreversible action, verify again that permissions are still valid and check the stopping conditions. A request timing out means its outcome is unknown. Do not assume it was not executed and repeat it. Check the request identifier or object state first, then decide whether to retry. These are responsibilities for execution. They do not require every application to use the same log format.

Stopping has its own completion conditions. Record separately the issuing of a stop instruction, acknowledgment by the executor, termination of actions already in progress, and remedy of consequences that have already occurred. If only the first two steps are confirmed, report that scope accurately. If winding down safely requires a limited action under a separate responsibility, continue it on its own grounds. Stopping the main task must not mean abandoning the necessary continuation. When repair fails, keep a record of the object for which repair failed and of the real-world consequences. A new, normal output must not overwrite them.
