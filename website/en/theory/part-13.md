# Part 13 Judgment Procedures and Tools {#part-title-13}

This Part explains how to convert the objects, propositions, scales, mechanisms, actors, multi-circle joint states, and requirements for dynamic projection defined in the preceding twelve Parts into limited judgments that can be observed, questioned, reviewed, and withdrawn. It adds no facts about the world. Filling in a table completely does not establish a fact. Before the tool workflow begins, the candidate object must already have been defined by the seven elements of a structural object (§3.1), and its position on the nine scale axes (§5.2) must have been stated. If the object, boundary, scale, time window, identity criterion, or intended use changes, existing judgments must not simply be carried over.

The tool workflow has two levels. The seven gates (§13.3) are the only main workflow. The five gates and thirteen steps (§13.4) are the layer that elaborates a diagnosis[^tn-diagnosis-14]. The seven gates determine whether a judgment can proceed to the next level. In complex cases the five gates and thirteen steps spell out the material that must be submitted. They do not issue a separate “pass” that competes with the seven gates or produce a second set of workflow results.

The most important boundary is this: the tool layer confers no authorization. Even when the workflow, evidence, and strong-judgment kit (§13.5) are all complete, the outputs are limited to descriptions, candidate mechanisms, comparative judgments, strong-judgment candidates, or requirements for action. Real-world action must still go separately through the normative choice record (§14.7), the ten bridging components required by the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1), one and the same valid itemized authorization, and the four-step operational procedure (§14.6). A tool output cannot substitute for any of them.

The workflow below governs judgments formed according to the structure of this framework, and the handoff of those judgments. Reading, checking against the original text, and clarifying a low-impact question can use concise records suited to the task. Not having gone through the whole tool workflow is no basis for declaring that established facts in the domain do not exist. Formal diagnosis, foresight, or high-impact use must meet all of the corresponding conditions. Renaming an output cannot reduce the responsibilities it carries.

## 13.1 Four Conversions {#section-13-1}

There are four conversions on the way from structural variables to requirements for action. The four conversions do not swap terms from the theory for labels of real-world situations. With each conversion, the responsibility for recording increases. If any step does not pass, the outputs that depend on it stop before that step. The formal completeness of a later step cannot make up for the failure. Other independent judgments are reviewed separately on their own grounds and intended uses.

| Conversion | Required Result | Where to Stop If It Does Not Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural variables to observable signals | A record of signals that specifies the object, scale, observation position, time window, measurement method, and missing-data status | A conceptual mapping or a gap in the material |
| Signals to strength of evidence | An evidence record that separates sources, observations, comparison conditions, coverage, counterexamples, alternative explanations, and uncertainty | Ungraded material or a hypothesis to test |
| Strength of evidence to level of judgment | A judgment that defines its proposition clearly and states limits of applicability, contrary conditions, falsification conditions, and withdrawal conditions | A candidate explanation or an observational assessment |
| Level of judgment to requirements and ceiling for action | A list of affected positions, the protection floor, prohibited actions, stopping, appeal, and restoration, stating which of the normative premises, itemized authorization, operational procedures, and the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1) are still missing | Description, a need for more evidence, or a requirement to take no action |

Each of the four conversions guards against one error. The first, from structural variables to observable signals, prevents a narrative about structure from being treated as an observed fact. The second, from signals to strength of evidence, prevents citations, materials, or declarations of position from being treated directly as evidence. The third, from strength of evidence to level of judgment, prevents correlation, analogy, or one narrative of how a mechanism works from being elevated to a conclusion. The fourth, from level of judgment to requirements and ceiling for action, prevents a leap from a factual judgment to action. Passing all four conversions leaves authorization status unchanged.

If any conversion does not pass, the later conversions that depend on it can only be recorded as “not carried out.” The propositions, comparative judgments, strong-judgment candidates, and requirements for action that they support must also be stopped or downgraded. Downstream conclusions that arise from the same gap must not regain their original ceiling through renaming, splitting, or opening another branch. A later “not carried out” only preserves this chain of failure. Even a complete strong-judgment kit cannot repair the break. A judgment may retain its original standing if it does not depend on the failed step, has independent grounds, and its use meets its own requirements. If the gap affects the overall source status, a shared factual premise, the object boundary, or lawful conditions for use of the entire task, stop or downgrade all dependent outputs together. If a check required for high-impact adoption does not pass, the same judgment must not retain its original use merely by being called an ordinary opinion.

## 13.2 Using the Eleven Basic Structural Concepts and the Eleven Human Variables {#section-13-2}

The eleven human variables of Part 7 (§7.3) apply to the human structured world. The eleven basic structural concepts of Part 3 (§3.5) are general. The tool layer can use both sets side by side in the same case. Matching numbers, equal counts, or similar questions are not taken to establish one-to-one equivalence.

When using them, state at least which basic structural concept or which human variable is used, the effective object, its position on the nine scale axes, the selected support route, the evidence for this case, alternative explanations, missing-data status, the judgment ceiling, and the action ceiling. Three of the six human-domain clauses (§7.1) are empirical hypotheses: “Meaning and Coordination,” “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity,” and “Historical Trace Carriers.” Each may enter the specified routes only through a specific test of its own (§4.2). The four empirical questions can support empirical claims only through formal tests (§4.1) pre-registered before the results appear. The detailed descriptions of the concepts and variables (for the human variables, see Appendix A) can raise questions, but filling in every item cannot establish facts about the object.

## 13.3 The Seven Gates as the Only Main Workflow {#section-13-3}

The seven gates are taken one by one for each specific proposition, in the order of its dependencies. If a gate does not pass, the later gates that depend on it can only be recorded as “not carried out,” and the corresponding judgments and requirements for action stop or are downgraded. A later “not carried out” cannot raise again the ceiling already lowered by the gate that did not pass. Nor can one acknowledge that the object or evidence did not pass a gate while letting the related later procedures claim to have passed. A judgment that does not depend on the failed step must still undergo its own independent review of evidence and intended use. Another proposition’s passing must not be used to conceal this proposition’s failure. Once the overall source status, a shared factual premise, the object boundary, or the lawful conditions for use of the task cease to hold, all outputs that depend on them stop together. When the object, the evidence relied on, affected positions, or intended use changes substantially, start again from the earliest gate affected.

| Gate | Core Question | Output Ceiling If It Does Not Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective Object Gate | Do the object, boundary, position on the nine scale axes, time window, and identity criterion support the current judgment? | Redefine the object, record the scale as “unknown,” or review the object conversion |
| Evidence Traceability Gate | Are sources, materials, observations, evidence, counterexamples, cases, and judgments distinct and traceable? | Organization of materials, candidate questions, or hypotheses to test |
| Affected Positions Gate | Which positions will see their circumstances change through direct, indirect, second-order, cross-domain, or intertemporal effects? | Supplement the accounting of impacts. Suspend strong judgments and high-impact uses |
| Power and Anti-Retaliation Gate | Can people in low-power positions safely add evidence, rebut claims, refuse, stop, and appeal? | Draw no adverse inference from silence. Lower the levels of publication and judgment |
| Mediation and Reflexivity Gate | Do metrics, platforms, AI, public evaluation, or the act of diagnosis change the evidence and the object? | High reflexivity remains unresolved. Restate the judgment as a conditional state transition |
| Strong-Judgment Procedure Gate | Has the complete strong-judgment kit been submitted for the proposition whose intended use and foreseeable impacts trigger the strong-judgment procedure? Public dissemination requires closer scrutiny. It does not by itself elevate all analyses to strong judgments | Stop the relevant strong judgments and high-impact uses. Review separately any comparative judgment that stands independently and meets the requirements for its use |
| Action Ceiling Gate | What is the most that may be required? Which actions are prohibited, and when are stopping, appeal, and restoration called for? | Description, gaps in evidence, no action, or continued deliberation |

Passing all seven gates indicates only that the records required at each gate have no gaps. It does not establish that the conclusion is true. Empirical propositions are still judged by their own definitions and the evidence of specific tests. Normative objectives still require explicit normative premises. Real-world action still requires normative choice, the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1), itemized authorization, and operational procedures (§14.6). The “Action Ceiling Gate” in particular can only record whether the external normative and authorization layers are in place. The tool workflow cannot write the result as “authorized” on its own.

The “Strong-Judgment Procedure Gate” has a specific applicability rule. Ordinary outputs at the “lightweight description” and “standard judgment” levels (§13.6) may record this gate as “not applicable,” but the record must state why the strong-judgment procedure is not triggered on this occasion. Recording it this way does not block the “Action Ceiling Gate.” Once this gate is recorded as “passed,” the same record must include the complete results of the strong-judgment kit (§13.5), with every item passed. Writing only the word “checked,” or leaving the reason blank, cannot substitute for these eight results.

The scope of identification for the “Affected Positions Gate” is determined under §14.4.1. Positions that section requires to be listed in full must be listed in full; for other positions, the record must state how far they were traced and why tracing stopped. For explanation and diagnosis tasks without a comparison of options, the test is whether further tracing could change the conclusion or its level, the scope of use, or protective arrangements.

## 13.4 The Five Gates and Thirteen Steps for Elaborating a Diagnosis {#section-13-4}

Go through the five gates and thirteen steps when the object is complex, responsibility is heavy, reflexivity is high, or the seven gates (§13.3) call for more detailed evidence. The five gates are local checks within this elaboration. They do not produce a second set of main workflow results.

| Elaboration Gate | Material That Must Be Developed | Corresponding Main Gates |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object Gate | Object, boundary, scale, and time window | Effective Object Gate |
| Evidence Gate | Cost of signals, sources, weights, and review | Evidence Traceability Gate |
| Scale Gate | Position on the nine scale axes, objects and evidence within each scale, and changes in scale | Effective Object Gate and Evidence Traceability Gate |
| Responsibility Gate | Effects on rights and resources, contrary conditions, repair windows, appeals, and review | Affected Positions Gate, Power and Anti-Retaliation Gate, and Strong-Judgment Procedure Gate |
| Observation Gate | Baseline before observation, reactions during observation, reactions after publication, and a ceiling on recursion | Mediation and Reflexivity Gate |

The thirteen steps, in order from the real-world question to write-back, form a chain of work that can be checked.

| Step | Minimum Deliverable |
| --- | --- |
| Start With the Question | A real-world question in language outside the framework, the requester, and the intended use |
| Identify the Object and Boundary | Effective object, subsystems, higher-level environment, and external channels |
| Separate Signal Types | Separate columns for facts, behavior, resources, feedback, narratives, feelings, conjecture, and noise |
| Record Participation in Observation | Whether those observed are aware of it, observer power, impacts of publication, and candidate responses |
| Identify the Dominant Constraint | Candidate constraints, the selected constraint, evidence for selection, and competing constraints |
| Record the State and Window | For an applicable object, which of the seven state prototypes (§8.3) it corresponds to, its position within that state, parallel subsystems, and contrary conditions. If inapplicable, state why and retain the domain’s own states and windows |
| Examine the Core Structure | Anchor, uptake layer, transmission chain, boundary, and feedback write-back |
| Identify Key Variables for Protection | Variables to protect, paths to harm, resources, redundancy, and paths for repair or return flows |
| Record Uptake and Repayment | Cost bearers, recipients of return flows, rights to stop, long-term overextension, and shifting of harms |
| Identify Hidden Risks and Boundary Types | Maintenance debt, mismatches between fast and slow processes, metric substitution, distorted summaries, and candidate object conversions |
| Propose Candidate Mechanisms | Competing mechanisms that the material permits and that could change the judgment, the evidence each requires, evidence that weakens them, and rules for eliminating them. If no plausible competing explanation exists, state the scope of the search and the failure conditions. Do not invent mechanisms to meet a quota |
| Anticipate Branches | Triggers, early signals, contrary conditions, repair windows, and minimum requirements for action |
| Produce the Output and Write Back | Output in ordinary language, conditions that trigger revision, scope of the record, review, withdrawal, and restoration |

Several of these steps have limits of their own. When the “Record the State and Window” step uses the seven state prototypes, it can use them only for human intentional collectives to which they apply. The prototypes must not be forced onto objects to which they do not apply, and they must not be treated as levels of maturity or as fate. When the “Identify Key Variables for Protection” step finds an important variable for protection, it can only propose a need for protection on that basis. When the “Record Uptake and Repayment” step finds uptake or overextension, this cannot generate a duty to make sacrifices or establish full responsibility. Candidate mechanisms proposed in the “Propose Candidate Mechanisms” step must not bypass the conditions for causal claims (§2.4). The branches given in the “Anticipate Branches” step do not describe a certain future. If the “Produce the Output and Write Back” step lacks review and withdrawal, the judgment must not be entered into organizational records, eligibility, allocation, or public memory.

## 13.5 The Strong-Judgment Kit {#section-13-5}

Whether a judgment is a strong judgment is determined by its uses and foreseeable impacts. Tone, length, and confidence of expression do not determine the category. A judgment enters the strong-judgment procedure if it is intended or reasonably foreseeable for use in real-world action or decisions about eligibility, rights, or resource allocation. The same applies if it has a material impact on a specific subject’s reputation and place in public memory, or if it is embedded in automated, organizational, or institutional decisions. Check the actual use of intermediate scores, recommendations, or material screening when they affect these decisions downstream.

Public dissemination is one factor that raises the requirements for scrutiny. It should be assessed in light of the object evaluated, factual allegations, identifiability, reach, and consequences. Publication alone is insufficient to raise every analytical output to a strong judgment. Ordinary work can reach definite conclusions when evaluating arguments, explaining mechanisms, or articulating normative views, provided the uses and impacts above are absent and the evidence and review meet the task’s requirements. Calling an output commentary, research, or a draft does not exempt it if it actually meets the strong-judgment conditions.

Only a judgment handled at the “strong-judgment candidate” level (§13.6), with all seven gates (§13.3) passed and each of the following eight items passed individually, may be recorded as a strong-judgment candidate. It still confers no authorization to act in reality.

| Component | Required Question |
| --- | --- |
| Contrary conditions | What circumstances would weaken, reverse, or lead to withdrawal of the judgment? |
| Repair window | Where is the window, what supports it, and when does it cease to hold? |
| Evidence requirements | What evidence supports the judgment, what evidence is missing, and what evidence weighs against it? |
| Access to appeal | Who can file an appeal, who is responsible, what is the time limit, and can the outcome change? |
| Anti-retaliation protection | Which activities are protected, how is protection monitored, and how can retaliation be reported independently and remedied? |
| Right to add evidence | How can evidence be added, how can existing records be corrected, and how are downstream records corrected in turn? |
| Trigger for external review | When is review triggered, who reviews independently, and can the reviewers see the materials and stop the result from taking effect? |
| Restoration and write-back | Which state is restored, who carries out the repair, which version of the record governs, and how are all records brought into agreement? |

If any of the eight items is missing, the judgment does not pass the “Strong-Judgment Procedure Gate” (§13.3). It must not become a strong-judgment candidate or retain its original high-impact use. It may be recorded only as an internal hypothesis, an open assertion, or a candidate mechanism within the limits of its materials and actual use. A comparative judgment at the “standard judgment” level may remain if it does not depend on the missing item, has independent grounds, and does not trigger a strong-judgment use. Failure of a common premise still blocks all judgments that depend on it. Renaming or splitting must not be used to evade that limit. Access to appeal does not count as an effective appeal if it cannot change a record or action. Being external in name does not establish independence. A statement, an apology, or the release of a new version does not amount to actual restoration and repair.

## 13.6 Diagnostic Levels {#section-13-6}

Diagnosis has three levels, called for short “lightweight description,” “standard judgment,” and “strong-judgment candidate.”

| Level | When It Applies | Output Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lightweight description and exploratory questions | Low complexity, low impact, reversibility, and limited information | Description, questions, and the next observation |
| Standard reviewable judgment | A structural comparison is needed, but the judgment will not be used for action carrying high responsibility | A comparative judgment within stated limits or a candidate mechanism |
| Strong-judgment candidate carrying high responsibility | Judgments with substantively high impact or embedded in automated, organizational, or institutional decisions. Public dissemination is reviewed in light of the object, factual allegations, identifiability, and consequences | A strong-judgment candidate after the seven gates and the strong-judgment kit pass |

A higher level adds responsibility for recording and does not make a judgment “closer to the truth.” The level must be downgraded if evidence coverage declines, the object undergoes conversion, reflexivity becomes uncontrolled, or appeals or anti-retaliation protection fail. Urgency does not permit omitting the object, protection, or stopping conditions. When waiting is costly, the tool may only propose, separately from the applicable authorization, the narrowest requirement for action that can be withdrawn and monitored.

</div>

## 13.7 Six Formal Tools {#section-13-7}

There are six formal tools. Each has its own purpose and its own limits.

| Tool | Purpose and Limits |
| --- | --- |
| Source–Evidence Ledger | Keep sources, materials, observations, evidence, counterexamples, coverage, and uncertainty separate. It does not prove a proposition |
| Open Assertion Record | Leave a provisional judgment that can be questioned, falsified, or withdrawn, to serve as a target. Do not use it for action carrying high responsibility |
| Proposition Validation Form | State the proposition type, evidence, counterevidence, falsification conditions, impacts, and ceilings item by item |
| Forecast Registration | Set down in advance the prediction, window, triggers, early signals, contrary signals, and which propositions the result is to be written back to |
| Counterexample and Scale Stress Test | Use boundary cases, adversarial explanations, and changes in scale to locate failures and narrow the scope |
| AI Output Boundaries | Require a statement of sources, missing material, the responsible human, uncertainty, prohibited uses, and review and withdrawal |

The Source–Evidence Ledger must not turn “has a source” into “is supported.” An open assertion cannot substitute for proposition validation. A failed prediction must be written back to the relevant proposition. An internal stress test is only a wind tunnel and does not supply external empirical support. The tools may cite one another’s records, but must not form a circle in which they vouch for one another.

Automatic checking is suited to a few tasks: checking items that can take values only from fixed options, checking whether references correspond to real material, checking whether versions and statuses agree from place to place, and checking whether calculations can be reproduced. It cannot replace assessment of materials, testimony from people affected, causal identification, or choices about values. Work that can be done mechanically should be left to programs so that readers can examine the actual grounds for judgments. The complete record of the process and clear prose can be kept separately, provided each can be found from the other.

</div>

## 13.8 Limits on the Use of AI {#section-13-8}

AI can organize materials, check the items in a record, compare different versions, propose candidate questions, list alternative explanations, and find inconsistencies in formatting. Where the evidence and normative premises allow, AI should also compare mechanisms, form refutable judgments, recommend options, and draft complete institutional arrangements, plans, and commentary.

AI must not invent sources, treat information recalled from training as current evidence, express consent on someone’s behalf, interpret silence as consent, take the place of a deciding subject or authorizing authority, or hide uncertainty. It must not make high-risk real-world decisions independently.

AI outputs must state what materials were used, what is missing, which content is observation, which is inference, and which is analogy, who is responsible for the final judgment, how the output may be used, and when it is to be reviewed or withdrawn. Where disclosure of identity, retaliation risk, or justified opacity is involved, omissions made for protection must remain distinguishable from “no evidence.”

Responsibility for AI use should also reflect the actual division of work among those who provide materials, decide objectives and permissions, maintain systems, carry out actions, and review results. Facilities and procedures that perform technical steps do not become subjects capable of bearing moral or legal responsibility merely because they output recommendations or execute instructions. Organizations legally capable of bearing a specified responsibility must be assessed separately according to their institutional status and the matter at issue. Greater automation does not put all responsibility on the person who clicks last. Reviewers must have sufficient materials, time, capability, and authority to stop actions in practice. The organization must also provide the resources needed for these conditions.

</div>

## 13.9 Workflow Records and What Failure Means {#section-13-9}

Each pass through the tool workflow must leave a workflow record. The items of this record are fixed, and users must not add items of their own. It states the following: the records of the object and scale; the diagnostic level used (§13.6); the result of each of the four conversions (§13.1); the result of each of the seven gates (§13.3); whether the five gates and thirteen steps (§13.4) were gone through; whether the strong-judgment kit (§13.5) was triggered; evidence; alternative explanations; uncertainty; the category of output; the judgment ceiling and the action ceiling; the handoff to the normative layer; appeal; restoration; and status.

The action ceiling has only three levels: “description only,” “diagnosis only,” and “requirements only.” Which categories of output may appear at each level is fixed. At no level can the output authorize or execute anything. Users must not claim “already authorized” in free text or expand the permitted categories on their own. A requirement for action only sets out the conditions that the external choice and authorization procedures need. It is not an executable command.

A handoff to the normative layer must point to external records that can be checked: the normative choice record (§14.7), the record of the check under the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1), and the authorization record. Where these records are not yet in place, this must be stated plainly. Pointing to them must make clear the object, action, scope, and time, and what the verification actually rests on. Vague labels, claims of having passed, or a record that circles back to cite itself cannot establish permission. The specific format of references is determined by the tools and practices actually adopted. This does not change one boundary: the tool must not issue authorization itself.

The results must remain distinguishable. “Passed” means that the records required for that item met the requirement. “Failed” means that the step was carried out but did not meet it. “Not carried out” means that the step was not done because an earlier gate did not pass or because of a limit of scope. “Unknown,” “not applicable,” “unobservable,” and “withheld for protection” remain four distinct missing-data states (§2.9). “Failed” must not be rewritten as “not applicable.” Content withheld for protection must not be rewritten as “does not exist.”

The workflow record itself never has the effect of an authorization. Even when there are records to point to for the external normative choice, the check under “Normative Bridging,” itemized authorization, and operational procedures, the workflow record only checks them and hands off to them. It cannot create an authorization of its own. All reviewed outputs must retain an accessible route of appeal and actual routes of withdrawal and correction. A tool conclusion that cannot be withdrawn must not be put to a high-impact use that is difficult to reverse.

## 13.10 The Nine Gates for Multi-Circle Projection {#section-13-10}

The seven gates (§13.3) are the only main workflow for explanation and diagnosis. When a task explicitly calls for dynamic projection, conditional foresight, or bounded choice, elaborate that workflow with the nine gates. The nine gates must not bypass the seven gates’ requirements for the object, evidence, affected positions, power, reflexivity, the strong-judgment procedure, and the action ceiling.

| Gate | Core Check | Output If It Does Not Pass |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Facts as They Stand | Event type, source, time, evidence cutoff, and disputes | List of factual questions |
| Joint Object | Actors, circles, relations, membership, and the identity criterion | Candidate groupings or a return to a single focus |
| Two Channels and Clocks | Material conditions and experience–meaning conditions relevant to the task, clocks, and unknowns. Irrelevant items are recorded as “not applicable” once the reason is stated | Retain the state snapshot. Suspend propagation that depends on missing conditions and review independent paths separately |
| Propagation Through Mechanisms | Channels, thresholds, delay, feedback, and cascades | Candidate mechanisms and observations that can distinguish them |
| Branching Paths | Conditions, parent and child nodes, and early and contrary signals | Parallel paths without forced ordering |
| Forecast Registration | Target, horizon, simple baseline, calibration, and write-back | Scenario projection only |
| Normative Choice | Normative premises, the protection floor, affected people, options, and no action | Suspend recommendations blocked by the gap. Retain evaluations that stand independently, conditional recommendations under explicit premises, and requirements for more evidence. This confers no permission to execute |
| Authorization and Execution | Itemized authorization, operational procedures, responsibility, stopping, restoration, and remedy | External handoff without execution |
| Outcome Write-Back | Results, comparison with the baseline, deviations, downgrades, and retirement | Retain the record of failure and start a new version |

The tool can propose candidate circles at the “Joint Object” gate and variable candidates at the “Branching Paths” gate, but every candidate always remains only a candidate. If the “Forecast Registration” gate does not pass, outputs must not use forecast language such as “will happen” or “expected to prove accurate.” If the “Normative Choice” or the “Authorization and Execution” gate does not pass, outputs must not give executable instructions. The “Outcome Write-Back” gate does not permit overwriting the original record.

## 13.11 Limits on AI in Dynamic Runs {#section-13-11}

AI can organize materials, check original passages that can be located, find which items are missing from a record, propose and compare competing paths, check whether definitions and arrangements are consistent, and calculate metrics registered in advance. Under explicit normative premises it can also form well-grounded local judgments and recommendations and draft complete texts.

AI cannot certify a real-world event solely on the basis of content it has generated, its own statements, or internal procedures. It cannot diagnose personality, disclose information on behalf of those concerned, use its own classifications to decide circle boundaries that bind others, treat simulations as facts, settle disputed value premises for others, or issue authorization.

Whether external material supports a particular fact must be judged separately according to the material’s status, method, counterexamples, and intended use. Faithfulness to a source does not automatically establish that what it says about the world is true. For a variable automatically proposed by AI, the system must record that it is only a candidate proposed by a model, and state the minimal test and prohibited uses. It must not be upgraded before the corresponding external evidence is available.

</div>

### 13.11.1 Checking AI Materials and the Chain of Tool Actions {#section-13-11-1}

When checking a generated answer, examine separately whether the source exists, whether the citation details are correct, whether the original passage supports the sentence, and whether the fact asserted in the sentence has adequate grounds. A source’s silence on the matter does not establish that the sentence is wrong. Accurately restating a mistaken source does not establish the facts. Where local knowledge, oral accounts, or protected materials have no public webpage, retain appropriate means of verification and limits on public disclosure. They must not be treated as nonexistent on that basis alone.

Retrieval augmentation can improve access to materials and performance on a task. Consistency or uncertainty checks can also detect certain errors. Their effectiveness remains limited by retrieval coverage, shared sources, and the types of error. An error repeated reliably can appear consistent. Several models repeating the same inaccurate material do not supply several independent pieces of evidence. A formal checker can confirm a derivation under stated premises, but cannot establish that those premises hold in reality.

For automated action, record the recommendation, tool request, application execution, external state, and consequences separately. The model writing “stopped” does not establish that the executor received a stop instruction. An interface showing “deleted” does not establish that downstream copies are gone. A person changing a recommendation does not establish that the change took effect before an irreversible action. Permissions for actual protection and correction must be exercisable in the corresponding system.

</div>

### 13.11.2 Task Endpoints and a Verifiable Execution Chain {#section-13-11-2}

How far a series of tool operations is to be verified is determined by the user’s request or by the task an institution sets. If the request is only for a draft, delivery can be complete when the draft has been saved correctly, can be opened, and its content meets the requirements. If the task requires changing an external record, the tool returning “success” is not enough. The change must still be confirmed by the state of the corresponding external object. If the task requires actual remedy, it must further be verified that the resources, rights, or services actually reach those concerned. Actual completion of a narrower endpoint need not wait for every consequence in people’s lives. A success message returned by the interface cannot substitute for completion of a broader endpoint.

| Stage | Minimum Object of Verification | Typical Break |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Form the recommendation | Objective, materials, object to be changed, and permitted scope | Treating generated text as already adopted |
| Make the tool request | Specific action, parameters, the current version of the object, and permissions | Sending a correct recommendation to the wrong object |
| Execute in the application | Whether the request was received, executed, or rejected, and how repeated requests are handled | A request arrives but is queued, fails, or is executed repeatedly |
| Check external state | Whether the target and dependent objects changed as intended | A local marker changes while actual execution still reads the old state |
| Check the task outcome | The specified benefit or protection endpoint and remaining problems | A corrected record takes effect, but the missed opportunity is not restored |

When several tools operate at the same time, first distinguish three cases: operations that can read independently, operations that can modify independently, and operations that write to the same object. Changes to shared state must preserve their order, state which version of the object the change presupposes, or settle how conflicts are handled. Before an irreversible action, verify again that permissions and stopping conditions are still valid. A request timing out means its outcome is unknown. Do not assume it was not executed and repeat it. Check the request identifier or object state first, then decide whether to retry. These are responsibilities for execution. They do not require every application to use the same log format.

Stopping has its own completion conditions. Record separately the issuing of a stop instruction, acknowledgment by the executor, termination of actions already in progress, and remedy of consequences that have already occurred. If only the first two steps are confirmed, report that scope accurately. If winding down safely requires a limited action under a separate responsibility, continue it on its own grounds. Stopping the main task must not mean abandoning the necessary continuation. When repair fails, keep a record of the object for which repair failed and of the real-world consequences. A new, normal output must not overwrite them.
