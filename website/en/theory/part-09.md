# Part 9 Actor States and Personality Hypotheses {#part-title-9}

This Part brings people into multi-circle projection without reducing anyone to a fixed label. An actor in an event brings long-term tendencies that change relatively slowly. They occupy roles and positions in relationships and access to resources that are specific to a period. Immediate emotions, bodily states, information, and opportunities also affect them. The same person may act differently in different circles[^tn-circle-10]. These differences need not be explained by hypocrisy, a split in personality, or a change in essence. They may also arise from changes in role activation, risk structure, visible information, rewards and penalties, ability to exit, and affected positions.

## 9.1 An Actor Is Not a Single Variable {#section-9-1}

The minimum form of a description of an actor’s state is:

`A* = <所指的行动者，观察窗，圈层成员关系，角色，慢变量，中变量，快变量，物质条件，体验—意义条件，未知项，证据状态，隐私与允许用途>`.[^tn-actor-tuple-10]

The asterisk indicates that this remains a candidate state description. An actor’s name, occupation, social status, or a single action cannot automatically fill in their state. Information that is unobservable, has no source, is withheld because those concerned decline to disclose it, or conflicts with other information must be retained as distinct kinds of unknown. The model must not fill the gaps with “common sense.”

Each of the following aspects answers a question, has content that needs to be set out at a minimum, and has things that cannot be inferred from it.

The actor referred to: this answers the question of whom, or which unit of action, the current analysis addresses. At a minimum it needs to set out a stable reference, the time window, and the limits of identity. No authority to represent the actor’s entire life can be inferred from it.

Circle memberships: these answer the question of which relational and institutional positions the actor occupies now. At a minimum they need to set out which circles are involved, the actor’s entry and exit, and on what basis the actor counts as a member. It cannot be inferred from them that the actor identifies with the circle’s goals.

Role: this answers the question of what the actor is expected, permitted, or compelled to do. At a minimum it needs to set out the source of the role, rights, responsibilities, and conflicts. The actor’s actual capabilities and genuine willingness cannot be inferred from it.

State variables: these answer the question of which observable or candidate states there are at present. For each, at a minimum, the time band, source, status, and counterevidence need to be set out. A stable essence of personality cannot be inferred from them.

Conditions: these answer the question of what makes a particular behavior more feasible or riskier. Material conditions and experience–meaning conditions need to be recorded separately. It cannot be inferred from them that a single cause determines behavior.

Unknowns: these answer the question of which key information is unavailable for now. At a minimum they need to set out the kind of missingness, its impact, and the limits on obtaining the information. It cannot be inferred from them that the model may freely fill in values.

## 9.2 Slow, Intermediate-Timescale, and Fast Variables {#section-9-2}

The three time bands are neither personality types nor independent compartments. They are distinguished in order to constrain the rate of updates. Some states can change after one event, others take multiple rounds to change, and still others should be updated only after more time has passed, evidence across contexts is available, or the person’s life has been substantially reorganized.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="9.2" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 9.2.1 Slow Variables {#section-9-2-1}

Candidate slow variables include long-term value priorities, habitual ways of interpreting, long-term capabilities and resource positions, identity commitments, and tendencies of trust and attachment. Calling them “slow” means only that they change relatively slowly within the current research window. It implies neither that they are innate or fixed nor that they express a true self or hold across all contexts.

Calling a variable slow must rest on evidence across time sufficient for the persistence claimed. A further claim of stability across contexts also requires materials covering different contexts. The validity of a measurement scale cited for a single measurement does not establish that the person has been observed in every setting. If a tendency appears in only one high-pressure circle and does not appear in others, the competing explanation that it is “activated by that circle” must be retained. If resource positions, trauma, major changes in relationships, long-term learning, or moves between institutional settings change patterns of action, slow variables should also be updated, split into conditional variables, or retired.

Learned tendencies can develop through repeated practices in families, schools, workplaces, and peer groups. Who has time to practice, who receives correction or encouragement, and which kinds of speech, posture, qualifications, and judgment are rewarded can affect which actions later feel natural, possible, or worth trying. This process of historical formation differs both from a single strategic choice and from an essence of personality. Connect opportunities for training, repeated practice, institutional responses, and differences across contexts. One action cannot be used to infer family background or an entire life history.

The same actor may carry different tendencies from several experiences of socialization. An old tendency may fit poorly in a new environment. A resource recognized in one domain may bring no return in another, and repeated new practices may change earlier tendencies. Slow variables should be open to splitting by context, revision, or retirement. An average association at the group level does not fill in an individual’s state. Deviation from what is typical in the group does not automatically make the individual abnormal.

</div>

### 9.2.2 Intermediate-Timescale Variables {#section-9-2-2}

Intermediate-timescale variables include role identity, goals for the current period, expectations in relationships, positions in alliances, accumulated fatigue, recent reputation, and trust specific to the period. They generally span several rounds of interaction but may change with the end of a project, organizational restructuring, repair of a relationship, a public event, or a change in resources.

Intermediate-timescale variables are the main junction in the mutual constitution of individuals and circles. Circles shape them through roles, metrics, reputation, rewards and penalties, and a sense of belonging. Actors in turn change circles through the fulfillment of responsibilities, resistance, exit, alliance formation, interpretation, and institutional write-back (§7.1). Observing only one side can lead to treating structural pressure as a personality defect or reducing individual choice entirely to determination by the environment.

</div>

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 9.2.3 Fast Variables {#section-9-2-3}

Fast variables include immediate emotions, arousal, attention, fear, impulses, current beliefs, short-term bodily states, perceptions of opportunity, and current strategies. They must be tied to a specific time and event. A single state of a fast variable must not be used to infer long-term personality.

Although short-lived, fast variables may feed into intermediate-timescale and slow variables through thresholds, public traces, irreversible actions, or feedback from others. For example, a brief fear does not by itself amount to lasting distrust. But if the fear leads to a public exit, punishment by the organization, rupture in relationships, and repeated avoidance, it can form a more durable path through multi-circle feedback. A projection must spell out that channel. The result must not be read backward to conclude that “they were distrustful to begin with.”

| Time Band | Typical Rate of Change | Evidence Required | Permitted Updates After an Event | Common Misuses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Slow variables | Slow relative to the current window, possibly spanning months, years, or major periods | Assess persistence using materials across time. A claim of stability across contexts also requires coverage of the corresponding contexts. Record dependence among sources and counterexamples separately | New evidence may change the level of support or the variable’s value. Check separately whether the change persists | Treating a label as innate or prescribing the same update rate for everyone |
| Intermediate-timescale variables | Several rounds of interaction to one period | Repeated observations within the same period, and role and circle conditions | May be updated as relationships, projects, organizations, or resources change | Treating a response to a role as personality |
| Fast variables | Minutes, hours, or several days | Tie to the time, event, bodily state, and information state | May be updated after each event | Using one emotion to predict the entire future |

## 9.3 Personality Only as a Conditional Hypothesis About Slow Variables {#section-9-3}

This framework uses the term “personality” in a limited sense. It treats personality as a set of hypotheses about slow variables that seek to compress variation in behavior across time and contexts. At minimum, a personality hypothesis must state what behavior was observed, in which contexts it was or was not observed, how long it lasted, what sources and counterexamples exist, what simpler explanations are available, and what the hypothesis may be used for.

What enters the analysis falls into two layers. One layer is specific, verifiable behavior, recorded as an “observation record”; it is not a conclusion about personality, nor is it a status of a personality hypothesis. The other layer is the personality hypothesis, whose status can only be supported hypothesis, candidate, disputed, unknown, or retired. A “supported hypothesis” is still qualified by its time window, context, and error. A “candidate” is only awaiting testing. “Disputed” means the evidence or explanations conflict. “Unknown” means no judgment can currently be made. “Retired” means the hypothesis no longer offers a gain or has proved unacceptably misleading.

A personality hypothesis must set out each of the following.

Hypothesis description: describe the tendency in terms that can be distinguished and refuted, without insulting or pathologizing labels.

Behavioral indicators: specific behaviors, choices, or expressions, without substituting the analyst’s impressions.

Observed contexts: circles, roles, pressures, resources, information, and time windows.

Contexts without observations of the tendency: settings that have not been covered, or where contrary behavior has appeared.

Competing explanations: role demands, short-term emotions, material constraints, strategies, cultural norms, measurement bias, and others.

Counterevidence: facts, testimony, and results inconsistent with the hypothesis.

Current status: candidate, supported hypothesis, disputed, unknown, or retired.

Permitted uses: research, communication, scenario projection, or other limited uses.

Prohibited uses: clinical diagnosis, judgments about the essence of personality, discriminatory screening, automatic authorization, or automatic punishment.

This framework does not perform clinical diagnosis, psychiatric diagnosis, or unauthorized psychological assessment. Even if a professional diagnosis exists outside the framework, it can enter only as material with a source, authorization, and limits of applicability. The framework must not generate or expand diagnoses itself or use them to take action incompatible with their original purpose.

## 9.4 Role Activation and Circle Conditions {#section-9-4}

At least six conditions can change when an actor enters different circles. These are visible information, available resources, rewards and penalties, responsibility, identity activation, and the ability to exit. In explaining differences in behavior, these conditions, independently measured individual differences, and the interaction between the two should be considered together as competing explanations. Looking first at contexts that are easier to change does not mean assigning in advance, in the model, all of the part explained jointly by context and individual differences to context.

For example, a person may be silent in their family circle, assertive at work, and open among friends. At least the following competing explanations are possible. Past conflicts in the family may raise the cost of speaking. The role at work may grant formal authority. Friendships may entail lower penalties and greater trust. The three settings may activate different identities. Observers may have access to different information in each circle. The behavior samples may cover different time windows. Comparisons should hold the target, data split, available information, complexity, and measurement budget fixed and test models of individual differences, context, and their interaction separately. If a personality indicator improves prediction of behavior in a new window, it can support a limited prediction. This still does not establish that the indicator is the main causal mechanism, an innate essence, or a priority for intervention. If the same person is always paired with just one setting, repeated observations cannot by themselves separate the contributions of the two.

A claim that a role has been activated should answer these questions: which event activated which role; whether the role comes from formal rules, informal expectations, a commitment by the actor, or a threat; whether the actor identifies with it; whether it conflicts with other roles; what it permits, what it prohibits, and what costs it requires the actor to bear; and what conditions are needed to exit the role.

A claim of stability across contexts must also specify whether it concerns rank order, mean level, the distribution of state fluctuations, or a profile of “how the person responds under specified conditions.” Changes in the proportions of different contexts a person encounters can change their overall mean behavior even if their conditional responses are unchanged. Long-term resources and capabilities are also slow variables. A person-specific random intercept in a statistical model represents only individual differences that have yet to be decomposed. It cannot be directly labeled personality.

## 9.5 How Individuals and Circles Affect One Another {#section-9-5}

Effects in both directions must be separated into two testable chains.

The circle-to-individual chain can be described as follows. A circle’s rule or an event changes information, resources, risk, roles, or conditions of meaning. Through the actor’s perceptions and capabilities, this changes fast variables. The change then reaches intermediate-timescale or slow variables through repeated feedback, public traces, or the reconfiguration of relationships.

The individual-to-circle chain can be described as follows. The actor’s expression, labor, refusal, exit, bridging, alliance formation, or innovation changes the circle’s resource flows, role allocation, shared interpretations, membership boundaries, institutional records, or external interfaces. This in turn changes the states of other members and of the circle.

| Direction | Starting Point | Channel That Must Be Found | Observable Outcome | Main Counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Circle→Individual | Change in rules, events, resources, or meaning | Information, rewards and penalties, physical load, roles, reputation, and belonging | Changes in states, behavior, or probabilities of choices | No change under the same exposure, or an external event explains the change |
| Individual→Circle | Expression, action, refusal, exit, or bridging | Resource flows, imitation by members, institutional write-back, network propagation, and a mechanism of representation | Changes in rules, boundaries, allocation, relationships, or collective states | The action is not received, or the change in the circle precedes the individual action |
| Circle↔Individual Feedback | The two chains above form a continuing closed loop | Return channel, delay, thresholds, and traces | Changes in transition probabilities in subsequent rounds | Only a signal arrives, without changing any subsequent state |

Saying “affect one another” does not complete an explanation. It requires the analyst to go on looking for direction, channels, delays, thresholds, losses, and counterfactuals.

</div>

## 9.6 Evidence Levels and Upgrading Variable Status {#section-9-6}

Actor variables are upgraded in a conservative order. A model can propose candidates from residuals. It must not promote a candidate directly to a fact. A single account by the person themselves, a single account from another person, a single behavioral observation, and a single measurement scale result each carry different biases. Agreement across sources does not automatically rule out a common environment, a shared narrative, or the same measurement bias.

| Current Status | Permitted Use | Requirements for Upgrading | What Must Be Retained |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unknown | Raise questions and flag impacts | Lawful and appropriate new observations | The kind of unknown and the reason for leaving the information uncollected |
| Candidate | Use in scenario projection and design a minimal test | Indicators, time window, and counterexample criterion stated in advance | Competing candidates, privacy, and risks |
| Supported hypothesis | Use for explanation or foresight in limited contexts | Out-of-sample review, tests across contexts, or independent sources | Limits of applicability, counterevidence, and error |
| Observation record | Describe behavior or a state at a specific time | Complete sources and a stated account of the conditions of observation | Do not promote behavior to personality |
| Disputed | Present conflicting materials side by side and lower the strength of the output | Independent review or new discriminating evidence | The positions and interests of all parties |
| Retired | Preserve the history and exclude it from the current projection | Later revisions may append only the reasons for retirement | Impacts caused by the earlier judgment and remedies |

If a variable is proposed only after results are known, it can help explain those results and generate a test for the next round. It cannot retroactively support the original prediction. If the definition, time band, or indicators of the same variable change, a new version should be created. Earlier failures must not be overwritten with it.

</div>

## 9.7 Material and Experience–Meaning Conditions in Actor States {#section-9-7}

An actor’s material conditions include physical load, observable aspects of health and sleep, time, space, money, tools, transportation, care responsibilities, standing under the law, and the actual ability to exit. Experience–meaning conditions include feelings, expectations, memory, identity, trust, shame, hope, fear, commitments, perceived legitimacy, and conflicts of value.

Record the two groups separately according to their relevance to the current question. If the task only needs an adequate technical or quantitative indicator, collection of irrelevant private experiences must not be compelled. If the conclusion concerns life opportunities, meaning, or autonomy, material totals cannot substitute for the corresponding experiences. Material deprivation can increase pressure without automatically determining how the actor interprets meaning. Positive beliefs can increase persistence but cannot remove constraints imposed by resources, the body, or institutions. An inference across channels must specify which material change affects behavior through which experiential mechanism, or which change in meaning alters material conditions through which action.

Status also has an institutional dimension that cannot be reduced to personal experience. Self-identification, classification by others, administrative registration, legal eligibility, and actual treatment must be recorded separately. A designation can alter access to hiring, services, the submission of evidence, or appeals, even if the person does not accept it. Institutional standing and the experience of dignity can affect one another without either establishing the other. The categories and comparison groups used by researchers must be open to challenge by those concerned. Their successive revisions must also be open to review.

A person can still act under constraints. A person signing under a credible threat and a device moving their body to produce the same handwriting involve different processes. The first can still be the person’s own action, even though there is no safe way to refuse and valid consent is absent. Attribution of the action, knowledge, control, feasible alternatives, blame, and remedy each require their own grounds. A causal explanation does not automatically exempt anyone from responsibility or establish that blame is warranted.

## 9.8 Privacy, Absence, and Refusal to Disclose {#section-9-8}

The closer the analysis gets to an individual, the higher the cost of misuse. Actor records must distinguish information that is public, limited to a particular context, sensitive, or highly sensitive, as well as information a person declines to disclose. Permitted uses must be consistent with the purpose of collection, authorization, and protections for those affected. Using material for communication does not by itself qualify it for use in hiring, insurance, law enforcement, medical care, punishment, or public evaluation.

Refusal to disclose is not evidence of hostility, deception, ignorance, or risk. Silence can stem from safety concerns, trauma, privacy, language, trust, differences in power, or reasonable noncooperation. The analyst can only record under §2.9 that no material was obtained (unobservable) and how this limits projection. Personality inferences must not be used to fill the gap.

## 9.9 Actor State Snapshots {#section-9-9}

A snapshot that can be checked needs to set out at least the following aspects. The last sentence of each gives the ceiling it sets on the output.

Observation boundaries: the actor, time window, observation position, and data cutoff. The output is limited to the current sample.

Circles and roles: memberships, roles, conflicts, and the ability to enter and exit. Identification with the circle and common goals are not presumed.

Slow, intermediate-timescale, and fast variables: the status, source, time, and counterevidence of each. No judgment is made about the essence of personality.

Conditions in both channels: material conditions and experience–meaning conditions. Neither substitutes for the other.

Competing explanations: structural factors, roles, strategies, measurement, and chance. Explanations that cannot be distinguished all need to be retained.

Privacy and uses: privacy level, authorization, and prohibited uses. The limits on use are not crossed.

Update conditions: which variables a new event reaches and when to review them. Not every state is refreshed at once.

The snapshot needs to retain its observation cutoff and the time at which information was obtained. When a new event occurs, first determine which channels and time bands it reaches and then update the corresponding variables. Calculate autonomous changes caused by the passage of time itself separately under the stated rules for dynamics. When neither a new observation nor a reliable rule for advancing the state is available, the last observed value and its time can be retained, or the state can be changed to “unknown,” but the old observed value must not be treated as the actual current state. Having the model compute again is likewise no evidence that all variables changed at once.

## 9.10 Example of Actor Analysis in an Event {#section-9-10}

Suppose a project lead suddenly vetoes the team’s proposal in a public meeting. The observation record can establish only the veto, the context of the meeting, what was said, and the visible consequences. Candidate fast variables might include time pressure, physical fatigue, perceived threat, and immediate anger. Candidate intermediate-timescale variables might include project goals, responsibilities of the role, a recent decline in trust, and reputational risk. A candidate slow variable might be long-term tolerance of uncertainty, which would require evidence across contexts.

The circles must also be examined. Has management just changed the metrics? Is the team short on resources? Has public opinion on a platform raised the cost of failing publicly? Do care responsibilities at home reduce the time available? Does organizational culture reward forceful decisions? The lead’s veto may change trust and subsequent expression in the team. The team’s silence may reinforce the lead’s belief that “only coercion works,” creating feedback. Writing only “controlling personality” would obscure multiple channels open to intervention. Writing only “determined by structure” would erase the actor’s choices, responsibilities, and capability to bridge circles.

A reasonable output should be layered. It should separate confirmed facts about behavior from the support each hypothesis about states has received. It should also specify circle conditions and channels in both directions, the next observation that would best distinguish the alternatives, and what remains unknown when evidence is insufficient. Such an output can enter path projection in Part 11. It cannot itself produce a diagnosis of the person, punishment, or authority to compel.

## 9.11 Where This Part Stops {#section-9-11}

The actor state layer can describe which observable states and candidate variables are currently available. It can identify changes that roles and circles may activate and the channels through which individuals and circles constitute one another. It can also describe which time bands a new event may reach first and which information remains unknown or protected by privacy.

This framework does not adjudicate the essence of a person. No professional assessment amounts to an ontological definition of a person. The clinical significance and scope of application of personality descriptions should be assessed by qualified professionals on the basis of relevant evidence, valid authorization, and participation by the people concerned. This framework cannot supply that assessment on its own. Future paths enter conditional foresight. Value and action enter the layer of normative choice and authorization. Prediction, professional status, and labels do not automatically confer authority to act.
