## 8.1 What This Part Explains {#section-8-1}

The seven state prototypes (§8.3) apply only to human intentional collectives. These are teams, organizations, institutions, movements, communities, or governance arrangements that people form around a shared problem, commitment, goal, or arrangement of meaning that they can articulate. They continue to operate through roles, resources, rules, relations, and feedback. The prototypes do not express universal laws and cannot be applied directly to individual personality, a purely physical system, the evolution of a species, or the universe as a whole. Even within their domain of application, the seven state prototypes do not form a ladder of maturity, a required historical sequence, or a ranking of value.

The numbers of the prototypes serve only to distinguish them and make them easy to find. They let us ask, “What structural configuration predominates now in this object, in this subsystem, and within this time window?” They do not warrant saying, “The number is higher, so the people are better, the institutional arrangements more justified, or the civilization more advanced.” A large collective can show the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion at its center, the prototype of development under interconnected constraints at its periphery, and the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance in a technical unit, while its intergenerational uptake still shows the prototype of a generative core taking shape. The same function can also show two prototypes at once. Such cases should be recorded as “parallel” or “mixed” without collapsing the complexity into a single “overall stage.”

A state prototype yields only a descriptive classification that can be withdrawn. It confers no authorization to act. Any real-world intervention must separately state its normative premises and pass the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1). It must compare options including taking no action and obtain itemized authorization (§14.6), that is, a single authorization that covers at once the specific subject, object, action, geographic area, and validity period. It must also be subject to the operational procedures and to appeal and restoration. A state label cannot serve as evidence for any of these requirements.

## 8.2 Minimum Requirements for State Judgments {#section-8-2}

For each judgment, first fix the following: which object is being judged, its identity criterion, its scale profile on the nine scale axes (§5.2), the time window, and the scope of the subsystem. Then record entry conditions, exit conditions, observables, counterexamples, falsification results, and missing data separately. There are only four classification modes: single dominant, mixed, parallel, and unknown. What is fixed in advance here is the criterion for tracking the object in this analysis. It does not require observed membership, actual boundaries, roles, commitments, or self-identification to have stabilized. If the definition of the identity criterion needs to change, a separate record should be created that preserves the original criterion, materials, and judgment, so that the change leaves a history. Object identity should then be checked again. Until that check is complete, records under different identity criteria must not be joined directly as a path of the same object across time windows.

| Item | Required Question | Stopping Point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Object and identity criterion | Are we tracking the same human collective, or has the object changed while keeping the same name? | If the identity criterion has not been fixed in advance, cannot be used to track the object, or leaves object identity unresolved, assess the object and its identity first and assign no prototype. Changes in membership or actual boundaries do not automatically amount to a change in the identity criterion |
| Scale and time | Which units, spaces, time windows, levels, affected positions, and networks are visible? | Wider observation does not expand the scope of authorization |
| Entry and exit | What materials make a prototype a candidate, and what materials show that it no longer predominates? | Resemblance to a prototype does not establish that the object has entered it or must exit it |
| Observables | Which behaviors, records, channels, costs, recovery, and write-back can be reviewed? | A slogan, an aggregate total, or an official self-description alone is insufficient |
| Counterexamples and falsification | What else could produce the same appearance? What result would lead to withdrawal of the judgment? | A label assigned without actively seeking counterevidence counts only as an impression |
| Evidence coverage | What external materials support the judgments about entry, observations, and counterevidence? Does the evidence cited correspond to real material, and does it cover the current object? | Internal definitions, numbering, and the framework’s own accounts cannot substitute for evidence about the object |
| Missing data | Is it unknown (not yet collected, conflicting sources, or still indistinguishable after attempts), unobservable, not applicable, or withheld for protection? | Missing data must not be filled in as “normal,” “absent,” or zero |
| Norms and action | Does the classification confer value, an obligation, or authority to act? | Every state is description only and authorizes no action |

Missing-data states must be kept separate as the four states of §2.9; in a state judgment, “unknown” must also note which case it is: “not yet collected” means that the data can be obtained but have not yet been collected; “conflicting sources” means that qualifying sources conflict with one another about the same object (§2.7.2); “still indistinguishable after attempts” means that the materials already obtained are still insufficient to make the distinction. “Unobservable” is determined under §2.9 and includes cases that cannot be observed under the current permissions, safety conditions, or measurement conditions. Subjects in vulnerable positions must not be required to expose themselves to complete a form. “Not applicable” must pass an applicability check before it can be determined. “Withheld for protection” means that the materials exist and that disclosure would violate an explicit protection; the basis, scope, duration, and review path are recorded under §2.9. A missing key item of entry, counterevidence, or scale by itself supports only recording the classification mode as “unknown” or suspending the relevant classification. Recording “mixed” or “parallel” requires positive evidence of multiple prototypes and of the positions to which each corresponds. Lack of knowledge must not be renamed “mixed.” Local materials already established are retained within their original scope.

### 8.2.1 The Classification Record for Each Judgment {#section-8-2-1}

§8.3 gives a diagnostic card[^tn-diagnosis-09] under each prototype. A diagnostic card yields a reviewable classification only when it is applied to a specific judgment and written up as a record with every item complete. The record must fix all of the following together: the object and its identity criterion, the nine-axis scale, time window, subsystem scope, classification mode, candidate prototypes, candidate paths, the results for the five kinds of conditions (entry, exit, observation, counterexample, and falsification), missing data, evidence about the object, uncertainty, independent review, appeal, restoration, and the boundary that no action is authorized. Each entry, exit, observation, counterexample, and falsification result is written in the same way: which item of which prototype it concerns, what the result is, which evidence it relies on, and what the reason is. The results must cover each candidate prototype item by item. A single overall assessment cannot substitute for them.

Each of the four classification modes has its own requirements. “Single dominant” has exactly one candidate prototype. “Mixed” and “parallel” each have at least two candidate prototypes and need to state the corresponding paths explicitly (§8.4). “Unknown” assigns no supported prototype and must explain which key materials are still insufficient to distinguish the prototypes. Local facts and observations of components already established remain in the evidence about the object. They must not be deleted because the overall prototype is unknown. When “unknown” is recorded and the candidate prototypes offer no place for these supporting materials, they should be kept in the evidence about the object and in the reasons, rather than in a complete prototype fabricated to hold them.

Every candidate prototype requires, at a minimum, evidence about the object for entry and for observation. Once any falsification condition holds, the candidate is excluded. Weaker wording cannot keep it in the record. Each check must also be supplied with a list of evidence materials kept outside the record. At every level of the record, the evidence cited must match the evidence about the object listed in the record and must also match, level by level, real material in that external list. A missing list, a claim of support made only at the level of the overall conclusion, or a citation of material that does not exist cannot pass. Internal numbering, definitions, and self-claimed judgments cannot substitute for evidence about the object.

The review record needs to state who reviewed, whether the review was independent, when it took place, which version of the materials it relied on, and what it concluded. The reviewer must be independent of the object being evaluated; giving the object another name cannot make it its own independent reviewer. Appeal and restoration each need to correspond to an actual procedure, with the current status of that procedure stated. None of these items can be dealt with by a single line such as “reviewed” or “appeal available.” Whatever the outcome of review, the classification remains description only and authorizes no action. When a state label is withdrawn or rewritten, the original evidence, objections, and history of changes should be preserved.

This Part also draws on four other kinds of content, each with a different role. The eleven basic structural concepts (§3.5) supply concepts for describing objects, states, channels, load, and phases. The six human-domain clauses (§7.1) supply adaptations for meaning, uptake, institutional write-back, power, and historical carriers in the human world. The four empirical questions (Part 4) specify when upgrading a pattern to an empirical mechanism requires an independent and eligible formal test. The twelve conclusion rules (§4.10) specify where inference stops. They are listed in the “Related concepts” row of each diagnostic card only as links to related concepts. They supply no evidence about the object at hand.

Describing configurations with the seven state prototypes does not require the multiple-interval pattern of the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism (§6.7). An object that meets the necessary conditions of one prototype throughout the observed time window may still receive a corresponding limited classification. That classification does not establish that a phase transition has occurred. A further claim about multiple intervals, a threshold, or a mechanism of phase transition is checked under the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism and the relevant formal test. Conversely, establishing a formal pattern with two intervals does not by itself show that an object fits a particular human collective prototype.

### 8.3.1 Prototype of Coalescence Around an Anchor (S0) {#section-8-3-1}

The prototype of coalescence around an anchor describes a “candidate collective taking shape.” It confers no designation of “lower-level organization.” A shared problem, candidate goal, or expression of meaning begins to draw together attention, relationships, time, and small amounts of resources. Membership, actual boundaries, roles, ways of making commitments, and expressions of shared identity are still forming or changing. The analysis must still use an identity criterion fixed in advance for this analysis and capable of tracking the object. Changes in a state that is taking shape cannot substitute for a judgment about identity. At least one candidate generative actor repeatedly translates, convenes, tries things out, or bears actual costs. An independent uptake layer has yet to form. A candidate collective may disperse, go dormant, be incorporated into another arrangement, or turn to a new goal at this point. Estimating how often each path occurs requires the relevant population of opportunities and observational material. The rates cannot be inferred from the prototype definition. The process ending does not by itself constitute a finding of failure.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, under an identity criterion fixed in advance for this analysis and capable of tracking the object, a shared problem or arrangement of meaning produces visible local clustering, but membership, actual boundaries, or ways of making commitments have yet to stabilize. Second, candidate generative action recurs and uptake remains limited. |
| Exit | First, while the same identity criterion remains applicable and object identity is traceable within the time windows, the anchor, actual boundaries, and generative roles stabilize across time windows, and traceable commitments and early uptake appear; the prototype of a generative core taking shape may then be tested. Second, the collective disperses, splits, is absorbed, or goes dormant, and need not pass through the prototype of a generative core taking shape. If the original identity criterion no longer applies, assess object conversion or termination separately. |
| Observables | Competing formulations are still under negotiation; resources cluster amid frequent entry and exit; generative actors take on translation, convening, or trial and error; failed attempts and the history of alternatives are still retained. |
| Counterexamples | A trending topic or platform recommendation can create short-lived synchronization. Administrative naming can create a formal team. Observe whether commitments and object boundaries persist after rewards, exposure, or orders are removed. |
| Falsification | Withdraw this classification if shared direction, the clustering of action, and generative roles all fail to differ from those in a matched temporary gathering. If stable roles, uptake, and repeated operation have long been in place, the prototype of coalescence around an anchor does not predominate either. |
| Scale | Distinguish generative actors, early participants, potential leavers, and outside parties who assign the name. Individual personalities must not be aggregated into a collective state. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | A claim that an arrangement of meaning has caused a difference in resources, action, or coordination requires a “Meaning and Coordination” test (§4.2). A claim that a specified channel has brought about the formation requires a channel-effect test (§4.4). Test historical traces under the hypothesis “Historical Trace Carriers” (§7.1), and obtain evidence for future effects of current carriers under §4.5. Add a historical-increment test only for a claim of a historical increment beyond the current representation. |
| Related concepts | Basic structural concepts: Candidate Object; Boundaries and Interfaces; States and Observables; Conditions and Environment; Load, Capacity, and Recovery Margin; Phase, Transition, and Dissolution. Human-domain clauses: Meaning and Coordination; Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility; Historical Trace Carriers. Empirical questions: grouping gain; channel effect; historical increment. Conclusion rules: Grouping Gain and Its Robustness to Boundary Perturbation; Carrying and Channel Constraints; Historical Effects, Path Differences, and Repair Windows; Load, Capacity, and Recovery; Human Uptake and Intertemporal Reproduction; Normative Bridging. |

This prototype permits only the conclusion that, for the specified object, scale, and time window, the situation is compatible with the prototype of coalescence around an anchor. It cannot establish that participants lack capability, value, or willingness. It cannot authorize support, dissolution, screening, or exclusion.

### 8.3.2 Prototype of a Generative Core Taking Shape (S1) {#section-8-3-2}

In the prototype of a generative core taking shape, a candidate anchor, object boundaries, and at least one generative role can already be identified repeatedly across decisions. Basic rules, language, resource channels, and the first carriers of memory are taking shape. The main work of translation, decision-making, resource mobilization, conflict handling, or repair of relationships remains concentrated in one or a few generative roles. Independent parties able to take up the work are scarce. This centrality describes structural dependence and does not establish the legitimacy of leadership.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, the identity criterion, the anchor, and the generative roles can be identified repeatedly. Second, key functions depend on the generative core on an ongoing basis, beyond a single emergency concentration. |
| Exit | First, new entrants form sustained arrangements for uptake, responsibility, and boundary interfaces; the prototype of initial expansion and differentiated uptake may then be tested. Second, the generative core splits, exits, is taken over, or goes dormant, or the object disperses; follow the corresponding path. |
| Observables | The anchor and boundaries show initial stability; key decisions depend on the generative core; rules and carriers of memory begin to recur; entry from outside is limited and targeted. |
| Counterexamples | A founder may still announce decisions publicly in a mature organization even though the actual uptake layer is already extensive. A newly created administrative unit may be only a role unit of a higher-level object. Trace actual execution, rights to stop, feedback, and operation when the core is temporarily absent. |
| Falsification | Dependence on the core is refuted if uptake across multiple centers remains stable with little involvement from the generative core. If the anchor is unstable but the same identity criterion still applies and the object can be tracked, the prototype of coalescence around an anchor may be tested again. If the original identity criterion no longer applies or object identity is unclear, object conversion or termination should be checked first, or “unknown” kept, before the objects that can be identified are classified. |
| Scale | Fix in advance who counts as generative roles, early parties taking up the work, higher-level parties who assign the name, and external supporters. When the organizational chart and the actual channels do not match, record the formal assignment and the actual operation separately and state the gap as it is; record “conflicting sources” (§8.2) only when sources conflict with one another about the same matter. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | Effects of the anchor on outcomes require a “Meaning and Coordination” test (§4.2), and effects of the generative core on outcomes require a channel-effect test. Obtain evidence under §4.5 for effects that early institutions or trauma produce through carriers, test traces under the hypothesis “Historical Trace Carriers” (§7.1), and test historical conditional increments separately in a historical-increment test. |
| Related concepts | Basic structural concepts: Candidate Object; Boundaries and Interfaces; States and Observables; Components, Carriers, and Substrates; Flow, Channels, and Transmission; Feedback and State Updating; Load, Capacity, and Recovery Margin; Phase, Transition, and Dissolution. Human-domain clauses: Meaning and Coordination; Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility; Institutional Write-Back; Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity. Empirical questions: grouping gain; channel effect; historical increment. Conclusion rules: Grouping Gain and Its Robustness to Boundary Perturbation; Carrying and Channel Constraints; Effective Feedback and Feedback-Mediated Learning; Load, Capacity, and Recovery; Observation Channels and Reflexive Responses; Human Uptake and Intertemporal Reproduction; Institutional Write-Back; Normative Bridging. |

This prototype must not be read as “immature,” “backward,” or “should be integrated.” It also cannot confer on generative actors permanent leadership, greater power, or standing to impose institutional arrangements or replace others.

### 8.3.3 Prototype of Initial Expansion and Differentiated Uptake (S2) {#section-8-3-3}

The prototype of initial expansion and differentiated uptake describes continued expansion of boundaries, membership, the population served, geographic reach, platform interfaces, or networks of collaboration. New entrants bring different understandings, skills, resources, and risks. Outside the generative core, positions begin to emerge for taking up work, for exiting, for maintaining dissent, and for those who have not yet decided how to participate. Expansion can improve the capability to coordinate and also adds problems involving translation, feedback, recovery, and power. Disagreement by itself does not amount to destabilization. There are grounds to describe a structural problem only if an observable mechanism persistently blocks uptake, feedback, or recovery.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, at least one kind of boundary continues to expand and changes relations. Second, distinguishable positions of uptake, cost, exit, and undecided participation emerge outside the generative core. |
| Exit | First, repeated loads and pressures produce interconnected constraints that persistently divide channels; the prototype of development under interconnected constraints may then be tested. Second, expansion stops or recedes, or the object jumps, splits, merges, is taken over, goes dormant, or collapses. |
| Observables | The pattern of entries and exits and the composition of interfaces change; roles and responsibilities differentiate; feedback volume, translation costs, time lags, and recovery needs change; identification with the group, dissent, exit, and safety are recorded separately. |
| Counterexamples | Growth in membership or user counts need not change the structure. Conflict becoming public may also reflect improved safety of expression that finally makes old problems visible. |
| Falsification | With the statistical definitions fixed, withdraw this classification if growth is not sustained and changes none of the boundaries, roles, responsibilities, interfaces, or loads. The classification should also be withdrawn if the supposed differentiation comes solely from labels assigned unilaterally by the center. |
| Scale | The whole, the center, new parties taking up the work, leavers, and externalized costs must all be included. Wider observation does not thereby expand the scope of authorization over new members. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | A claim that expansion or a channel has caused differences in outcomes requires a channel-effect test and must meet the conditions for causal claims (§2.4). Cross-scale judgments additionally need to set out the scale transformation (§3.4) and pass the epistemic constraint “Testing Cross-Scale Transfer” (§4.8.5). Claims of closure or conversion take a separate scale-closure test (§4.6). Coordination by an anchor requires a “Meaning and Coordination” test (§4.2). Observational effects of expression, disclosure, and power require a “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. Historical traces require a “Historical Trace Carriers” test, carrier transmission is checked under §4.5, and historical conditional increments take a separate historical-increment test. |
| Related concepts | Basic structural concepts: Candidate Object; Boundaries and Interfaces; States and Observables; Components, Carriers, and Substrates; Flow, Channels, and Transmission; Feedback and State Updating; Conditions and Environment; Load, Capacity, and Recovery Margin; Phase, Transition, and Dissolution. Human-domain clauses: Meaning and Coordination; Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility; Institutional Write-Back; Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity; Historical Trace Carriers. Empirical questions: grouping gain; channel effect; historical increment; scale closure. Conclusion rules: Grouping Gain and Its Robustness to Boundary Perturbation; Carrying and Channel Constraints; Effective Feedback and Feedback-Mediated Learning; Historical Effects, Path Differences, and Repair Windows; Load, Capacity, and Recovery; Scale Closure and Object Conversion; Local Improvement and Cross-Boundary Spillover; Observation Channels and Reflexive Responses; Human Uptake and Intertemporal Reproduction; Institutional Write-Back; Normative Bridging. |

This prototype does not permit labeling people who do not identify with the collective, who leave, or whose participation is unclear as “negative subjects” on that basis alone. A “need for dimensional expansion” cannot authorize enforcing uniform beliefs, restricting exit, or excluding dissenters.

### 8.3.4 Prototype of Development Under Interconnected Constraints (S3) {#section-8-3-4}

In the prototype of development under interconnected constraints, the collective still operates or develops, but several recurring constraints have become connected. Approvals, resources, trust, responsibility, information, platforms, or external dependencies form mutually reinforcing bottlenecks that persistently fragment feedback, uptake, recovery, or the return flow of costs. The question is whether these problems form a connected structure that can be reviewed. The number of problems or the naming of a person as a barrier does not answer that question.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, at least two kinds of constraint reinforce one another and affect channels across time windows. Second, operation continues, but uptake at the downstream end, feedback, resources, or recovery bear a sustained extra load. |
| Exit | First, key relations, permissions, resources, or interfaces begin to be reconfigured in practice; the prototype of a window for structural reconfiguration may then be tested. Second, the object enters regression, a pseudo-steady state, dormancy, takeover, forced rewrite, migration, or collapse. |
| Observables | A network of constraints and bottlenecks; output persists while losses, rework, and hidden labor increase; repair is repeatedly started but does not write back; the center and periphery, metrics and uptake, and nominal and actual arrangements remain divided. |
| Counterexamples | Protective layering and redundancy can improve reliability in a high-risk environment. Deliberate load reduction for reconfiguration can also slow output temporarily. Check whether the restrictions block necessary feedback or provide error isolation and alternative paths. |
| Falsification | After common inputs, measurement bias, and short-term peaks are excluded, the constraints are not connected; or alternative channels, write-back, and recovery at the downstream end of uptake remain effective. If either holds, this prototype does not predominate. |
| Scale | Separate the center, periphery, functional units, downstream uptake positions, and affected positions outside the object. Recording “parallel” is allowed. Disappearance of weak signals cannot be treated as disappearance of the problems. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | A claim that power or mediation has caused masking requires a “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test (§4.2). A claim about specified channels and their mutual reinforcement requires a channel-effect test. Examine current constraints formed through history under §4.5, and test additional historical residuals in a historical-increment test. A claim of failed cross-scale closure requires a scale-closure test. |
| Related concepts | Basic structural concepts: Boundaries and Interfaces; States and Observables; Relations and Constraints; Flow, Channels, and Transmission; Feedback and State Updating; Conditions and Environment; Load, Capacity, and Recovery Margin; Trace, Memory, and Paths; Phase, Transition, and Dissolution. Classification under this prototype cannot authorize “breaking barriers,” purges, takeover, forced reform, or shifting transition costs to vulnerable positions. It indicates only that the constraint network and competing explanations should be distinguished further.

### 8.3.5 Prototype of a Window for Structural Reconfiguration (S4) {#section-8-3-5}

The prototype of a window for structural reconfiguration describes a genuine window for reconfiguring key structures. It gives no assurance that a reform is already right. Old relations, permissions, resource channels, feedback write-back, or boundary interfaces are being dismantled, replaced, or bypassed. Old and new arrangements coexist. With them come conversion costs, duplicated work, knowledge transfer, short-term declines in output, and growing recovery needs. The outcome remains open. It can be improvement, stagnation, alienation, forced rewriting, or collapse.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, at least one key element actually changes, beyond renaming or publicity. Second, old and new arrangements coexist, transition costs can be traced, and the identity criterion continues to be checked. |
| Exit | First, channels, feedback, uptake, and recovery improve across time windows without concealed outsourcing; the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion may then be tested. Second, the new structure is unusable, and the object enters dormancy, regression, takeover, forced rewriting, a pseudo-steady state, or collapse. |
| Observables | Permissions, resources, roles, responsibilities, and stopping conditions are genuinely written back; dual systems and conversion costs; buffers, redundancy, cooldown, stopping, and restoration; outcomes from multiple positions enter actual feedback. |
| Counterexamples | Large-scale renaming, replacing people, or redrawing flowcharts may be symbolic reform. Dismantling old arrangements, overload, and declining output may also reflect a destructive purge or withdrawal of resources. |
| Falsification | Withdraw this classification if actual relations, permissions, resources, and execution do not change. If no usable new structure exists and functions remain disrupted, the process must not be presented as reconfiguration that brings improvement either. |
| Scale | Cover the time windows for the pre-change baseline, transition, cooldown, restoration, and recovery. Distinguish those who initiate reform, those who execute it, those who take up the work, and those who bear the costs. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | A claim that reconfiguration has caused improvement or harm requires a channel-effect test and must meet the conditions for causal claims (§2.4). A claim of object or intervention conversion requires a scale-closure test. Whether failed attempts affect the future through a current carrier is assessed under §4.5, and additional historical residuals take a separate historical-increment test. A “tunneling” analogy cannot substitute for that evidence. |
| Related concepts | Basic structural concepts: States and Observables; Relations and Constraints; Flow, Channels, and Transmission; Feedback and State Updating; Load, Capacity, and Recovery Margin; Trace, Memory, and Paths; Phase, Transition, and Dissolution. Human-domain clauses: Meaning and Coordination; Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility; Institutional Write-Back; Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity; Historical Trace Carriers. Empirical questions: channel effect; historical increment; scale closure. Conclusion rules: Carrying and Channel Constraints; Effective Feedback and Feedback-Mediated Learning; Historical Effects, Path Differences, and Repair Windows; Load, Capacity, and Recovery; Scale Closure and Object Conversion; Local Improvement and Cross-Boundary Spillover; Observation Channels and Reflexive Responses; Human Uptake and Intertemporal Reproduction; Institutional Write-Back; Normative Bridging. |

A “transition valley” is at most a description to be tested. It cannot justify unlimited costs, irreversible harm, the removal of appeal, or sacrifices imposed on those in low-power positions. This prototype confers no authorization for dismantling, purges, or forced transfer.

### 8.3.6 Prototype of Efficient Coordination and Flexible Expansion (S5) {#section-8-3-6}

The prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion describes coordination with relatively low losses among the channels for resources, information, feedback, responsibility, uptake, and recovery in the current environment and time window. Local disturbances usually fail to produce lasting interconnected barriers. Boundaries can expand and contract with some flexibility. Resource advantages, costs shifted through outsourcing, silence, whitewashed metrics, or institutional loopholes can still make this state appear to hold when it does not. High output is therefore insufficient evidence, and long-term persistence does not prove legitimacy.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, key channels operate with low losses across time windows, can be substituted, and permit write-back. Second, expansion, preservation, and dissipation work together without relying on closing off feedback or suppressing dissent. |
| Exit | First, distributed uptake, self-renewal, recovery, and new generation hold across disturbances; the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance may then be tested. Second, loopholes, alienation, a pseudo-steady state, regression, functional transfer, “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” (§8.5), takeover, or collapse cause the prototype to stop predominating. |
| Observables | Alternative uptake arrangements and channels are actually usable; output, feedback, recovery, and maintenance all continue; boundaries are open, protection and exit can be implemented, and costs flow back; checks for loopholes can produce write-back. |
| Counterexamples | Resource abundance, selective metrics, failed complaint channels, compliance out of fear, or shifting maintenance debt into the future can all create the appearance of “success.” |
| Falsification | After maintenance, recovery, exit, and external costs are included, the efficiency advantage disappears; or local failures cascade rapidly when the center is minimally involved. If either holds, withdraw this classification. |
| Scale | Management totals must be broken down to the positions of maintainers, people taking up the work with little power, leavers, and external positions affected. In time, cover high and low loads and the time windows for disturbance, recovery, and maintenance. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | The causes of efficient coordination and the effects of loopholes require a channel-effect test and a “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test (§4.2). Historical traces, sustained carrier effects, and additional residuals are dealt with, respectively, under the hypothesis “Historical Trace Carriers” (§7.1), under §4.5, and by a historical-increment test. Replication across scales requires a mapping of the scale transformation (§3.4), must pass the epistemic constraint “Testing Cross-Scale Transfer” (§4.8.5), and requires independent evidence on the target side. Claims of closure or conversion take a separate scale-closure test. |
| Related concepts | All eleven basic structural concepts. Human-domain clauses: Meaning and Coordination; Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility; Institutional Write-Back; Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity; Historical Trace Carriers. All four empirical questions. All twelve conclusion rules. |

This prototype does not label an object as “good,” “mature,” or “ready to export its model.” Persistence, growth, and selection do not establish legitimacy. They cannot authorize expansion, replication, takeover, or demands that members take on more. Conversely, if coordination is sustained by closing off feedback or suppressing dissent, the second entry condition is not met, and the object does not fall under this prototype; paths such as the “pseudo-steady state” may be checked under §8.4 and §8.6. This does not determine in advance that its output is fake. The output itself is checked against its own materials; whether the advantage in efficiency holds is judged from the materials after maintenance, recovery, exit, and external costs are included (see the “Falsification” row of the table above), and the costs corresponding to the suppressed dissent are also counted where they can be established.

### 8.3.7 Prototype of Adaptive Dynamic Balance (S6) {#section-8-3-7}

The prototype of adaptive dynamic balance describes a collective with distributed uptake, effective feedback, and the capability to recover, update, and generate anew. Ordinary fluctuations do not lock in its direction for long. Autonomous units can stabilize locally, coordinate laterally, and receive backing from a higher level. A pool of potential successors, memory attached to positions, authorization, and handovers allow newcomers actually to enter and modify existing arrangements. The collective maintains function through continual calibration, with fluctuations still present.

| Aspect | Content |
| --- | --- |
| Entry | First, with little involvement from the center, multiple positions can still sense changes, correct errors, recover, and write back. Second, people taking up the work, rules, and boundaries can be renewed continually without dependence on the same irreplaceable individuals. |
| Exit | First, slower recovery, larger fluctuations, failed write-back, dependence on the center, or interruption of uptake leads to regression or another path. Second, the end of the mission, functional transfer, a split, a merger, paradigm replacement, or “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” (§8.5) changes the definition of the object. |
| Observables | Recovery and renewed destabilization after a disturbance; coordination among multiple centers and backup arrangements; new people taking up the work go from training to taking charge of it in practice; feedback, checks, appeals, and rule revisions maintain a closed loop. |
| Counterexamples | Strong control, disappearance of weak signals, protection of resources, or concealed firefighting by the center can all create low fluctuation. Frequent personnel changes and reorganizations may simply treat staff as expendable. |
| Falsification | Under comparable disturbances, withdraw this classification if recovery keeps slowing, local failures cascade, correction depends on a single point, or successors cannot obtain knowledge, permissions, and protection. |
| Scale | Long time windows spanning disturbances, recovery, succession, and slow variables are needed. Overall averages cannot hide harm at the periphery. What autonomy, lateral coordination, backing from higher levels, and actual authorization each refer to needs to be fixed in advance. |
| Evidence for stronger claims | The effects of regulation, feedback, and arrangements with multiple centers require a channel-effect test and must meet the conditions for causal claims (§2.4). For learning, institutional memory, and intergenerational uptake, first check carrier retention and actual continuity. Test traces under the hypothesis “Historical Trace Carriers” (§7.1), and test additional historical increments in a historical-increment test. Cross-scale extrapolation must set out the scale transformation (§3.4), pass the epistemic constraint “Testing Cross-Scale Transfer” (§4.8.5), and have independent evidence on the target side. Claims of closure or conversion take a separate scale-closure test. |
| Related concepts | All eleven basic structural concepts. Human-domain clauses: Meaning and Coordination; Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility; Institutional Write-Back; Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity; Historical Trace Carriers. All four empirical questions. All twelve conclusion rules. |

This prototype does not mark an absolute final state, a society without conflict, or the end of history. A collective in it can regress, split, be taken over, transfer functions, enter “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise,” or collapse. The classification cannot confer decision-making authority over others on any object.

## 8.4 Library of Nonlinear Paths {#section-8-4}

State prototypes must be used together with the path library to avoid reverting to a stage theory. A path describes “how a state configuration or the object’s definition changes.” It does not describe “how better people advance to a higher level.” Several paths can be candidates within the same time window. When evidence is insufficient, keep “unknown.”

| Path | Meaning | Key Distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mixed | Necessary features of two or more prototypes occur together for the same object, scale, and time window | If the differences can be mapped consistently to separate subsystems, the record should use “parallel” |
| Parallel | Different regions, levels, functions, or positions at the center or periphery are in different states at the same time | The states must not be collapsed into an “overall stage.” Preserve the identity criterion, evidence, and exit conditions for each subsystem separately |
| Jump | Adjacent observation windows show a shift from one configuration to a nonadjacent one, with no discernible window of persistence in an intermediate prototype | First rule out overly sparse sampling and missing material. Record skipped functions, costs, and unknowns |
| Regression | One or more functions deteriorate relative to the baseline of the same object | Distinguish abrupt and gradual decline, jumps at thresholds, and cascades. Deliberate load reduction, “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” (§8.5), and functional transfer do not count as regression |
| Split | The original object divides into two or more candidate objects with different identity criteria | Temporary project groupings and modularization need not change the object. Define each new object anew |
| Merge | Multiple objects form a new object or are absorbed by one of them | An alliance need not constitute a merger. The boundary, memory, exit, and outstanding responsibilities of the weaker party must be listed separately |
| Dormancy | Visible activity decreases while memory, relationships, symbols, capabilities, or conditions for reactivation remain | Distinguish protective withdrawal, stagnation from exhaustion, disappearance of material, and actual collapse |
| Takeover | An external subject gains control of key resources, permissions, or interfaces | External support or oversight need not constitute a takeover if genuine autonomy and the right to stop are preserved |
| Functional transfer | A core function moves to another object. The function can continue while the original identity, relationships, or memory disappear | Outsourcing one task does not amount to functional transfer. Functional continuity does not establish continuity of responsibility or memory |
| Paradigm replacement | A new object with a different identity criterion, interfaces, or rules bypasses the old object without repairing it | An upgrade within the same object or a change of brand name need not constitute paradigm replacement. Outstanding obligations remain |
| Trauma-built development | Boundaries, identity, rules, or ways of taking up work form under harm, deprivation, failure, or threat | A narrative of trauma does not automatically establish future effects. Whether the arrangement provides protection and whether it reproduces the original harm must be tested separately |
| Pseudo-steady state | Surface indicators are stable while actual uptake is being exhausted, weak signals disappear, feedback fails to write back, or debts increase | Keep two ledgers, one for appearances and one for actual conditions. Return to costs, recovery, the right to stop, and feedback from the downstream end |
| Forced rewrite | A subject with greater power rewrites the object when consent that can be reviewed and corrected, exit, and protection of memory are lacking | Distinguish this from authorized, negotiated revision that can be withdrawn and appealed. Check for irreversible erasure of identity |
| External carrier migration | When the path to repair is closed off, personnel, capabilities, resources, relationships, evidence, or memory move to a safer system for taking them up | A move with no capability to take up what is transferred is not safe migration. Romanticizing departure or privatizing harm does not establish safety either |
| Collapse | The original identity criterion ceases to hold, and functions, uptake, relationships, or boundaries break down without a plan | Distinguish collapse from “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise,” functional transfer, protective dormancy, and object reorganization. Releasing resources does not justify harm |

Path names likewise generate no value or responsibility. For example, “regression” does not amount to moral decline, “split” need not be failure, “merge” need not be progress, “takeover” is not automatically unlawful, and “external carrier migration” does not require individuals to flee on their own. Paths only allow competing explanations into the record. Mechanisms, justification, and action still each have their own conditions to meet.

</div>

## 8.5 Orderly Exit and Benign Demise (X0) {#section-8-5}

“Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” is not an eighth state. Its number does not follow on from the seven state prototypes, and it is not counted as an eighth stage. It is an independent path in the life cycle of human collectives. It applies when maintaining the original object no longer serves the stated anchor, its core functions can be completed, terminated, or taken up by a safer party, and personnel, relationships, resources, memory, debts, and appeals can all be dealt with explicitly. The path then records how the object closes out its mission, transfers functions, releases resources, preserves memory, settles outstanding responsibilities, and retains limited weak ties.

This path does not require first reaching the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion or the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance. A temporary initiative in the prototype of coalescence around an anchor can close in an orderly way when it finds that the problem is already being taken up elsewhere. An expansion project in the prototype of initial expansion and differentiated uptake can wind down if continuing to expand would only create harm. An institution stuck in the prototype of development under interconnected constraints may also enter exit if repair is infeasible and external uptake is adequate. Conversely, a collective in the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance cannot claim eligibility to disband on the strength of a “higher-stage” label. Capabilities at a stage, normative justification, and operational conditions are three distinct matters. No state number can become a prerequisite for eligibility for self-initiated exit.

This boundary is fixed. The path can be entered from any stated starting position. There is no minimum state requirement, no requirement to be in any particular prototype first, and still less a requirement of the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion or the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance. Its entry and completion conditions must not treat any of the seven state prototypes as an eligibility requirement. The numbers of the prototypes may only describe the starting position and must not become a threshold for exit.

</div>

### 8.5.1 Candidate Entry for Orderly Exit {#section-8-5-1}

First, the anchor has been fulfilled, ceased to apply, or been taken up by a safer object, or continuing to maintain the object chiefly serves the organization’s own persistence. This judgment must be independently reviewable. The center must not certify completion for itself after rewriting the goal. Second, personnel, functions, resources, relationships, records, debts, and irreplaceable values have been inventoried, and there are verifiable receiving parties or protective arrangements. Short-term setbacks, tight funding, leadership fatigue, or evasion of responsibility can create similar appearances. A narrative alone therefore cannot justify a finding of “benign completion.”

### 8.5.2 Completion Conditions for Orderly Exit {#section-8-5-2}

| Aspect of Completion | What Must Be Preserved | Signs of Failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Anchor and object | Independent verification of completion, termination, or invalidity. The time when the identity criterion ends and matters still open | Redefining the goal, deleting failures, or the center unilaterally declaring completion |
| Core functions | Mark each as completed, terminated, transferred, or unresolved. Record the receiving party’s capability, permissions, resources, and arrangements for restoration | A declaration alone, with no continuity for maintainers or users |
| Personnel and relationships | Safety, basic rights and interests, the right to stop, protection against retaliation, and arrangements for continuing care and relationships of dependence | Privatizing organizational obligations by leaving members to bear them on their own |
| Resources | Traceable objects, boundaries, sequence, timing, and destinations to prevent capture by those with greater power | Dividing resources internally first, then leaving the losses to low-power positions |
| Memory and evidence | Preserve successes, failures, repairs, and memory attached to positions. Specify access, privacy, correction, and protection against retaliation | Keeping only a record of victories while deleting or exposing materials from vulnerable positions |
| Debts and responsibility | Continuing responsible parties for contracts, labor, data, maintenance, environmental matters, appeals, and future risks | Responsibility, review, and maintenance all disappear when the object is deregistered |
| Weak ties | Limited to advice, shared experience, and buffering, with clear boundaries and duration | Reviving the original power structure without new authorization |

The outcomes of this path should at least distinguish “candidate,” “in progress,” “partially complete,” “complete,” and “unknown.” As long as the affected groups, outstanding obligations, the capability to receive, appeals, or the safety of memory remain “unknown,” the result must not be recorded as “benign completion.” A transfer of functions without continuity of memory and responsibility may be only “functional transfer.” Unilateral closure and rewriting of identity by an external power may be “takeover” or “forced rewrite.” A breakdown with no uptake is “collapse” and must not be dressed up as “orderly exit.”

Classification under “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” grants no authority to dissolve the object, abolish posts or units, transfer assets, publish archives, waive debts, or consent on members’ behalf. It can tell us only which objects, obligations, evidence, protections, and unknowns must be entered in the same ledger to judge whether an exit is orderly.

## 8.6 How Regulation, Early Warning, and Uptake Enter State Judgments {#section-8-6}

A static snapshot alone cannot establish a state classification. The regulation layer requires observing the target, sensing, comparison, execution, feedback, and time lag. The early-warning layer requires checking whether slower recovery, larger fluctuations, more severe consequences of similar disturbances, and local flickering form a cluster. The repayment layer requires tracing who makes commitments, who bears the costs, to whom returns flow, and whose costs or claims are deferred to the future. If the apparent stability of the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion or the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance rests on off-ledger labor, silence, and future debt, the case should be treated as a candidate for a “pseudo-steady state” (§8.4).

Clues generated by the same sequence or shared channel of change do not become independent pieces of evidence merely because they cluster. Dependent clues can still be used jointly. Their value for early warning must be evaluated under compatible dynamics, noise, sampling, and observation time windows, against fixed targets, horizons, real-time baselines, and signals in the opposite direction (Scheffer et al., 2009). See D.31 and §12.6.

Clustering is not a universal prerequisite for early warning. A single domain-specific signal can be sufficient for a limited judgment. The absence of critical slowing down or a second signal does not prove safety or invalidate an existing specialist forecast. A rule that uses several clues jointly requires a joint distribution or corresponding validation. An increase in its positive predictive value does not guarantee a reduction in total loss given false alarms, missed detections, and action costs.

Uptake cannot be assessed merely by asking whether there is a successor. Check whether a pool of potential successors, shadow roles, memory attached to positions, authorization paths, handover windows, translation of legitimacy, and protective conditions are actually in place. A clean departure by generative actors leaves a vacuum of authority if those taking up the work cannot enter. Frequent changes in personnel do not amount to adaptive renewal if key knowledge and permissions remain locked in the original center. Four human-domain clauses (§7.1) come in here. Use “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility” to distinguish uptake carriers, cost bearers, beneficiaries, rights to stop, and responsible parties. Use “Institutional Write-Back” to determine whether feedback actually changes the relevant content and whether the change is executed. Use “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” to examine power and observation channels, and “Historical Trace Carriers” to verify traces. Whether carriers actually continue is tested under §4.5. If a historical increment beyond the current representation is also claimed, add a historical-increment test. For the candidate mechanism by which this kind of dependence strengthens across rounds, see §7.6.1.

## 8.7 Limits and Exclusions of the Current Prototypes {#section-8-7}

The seven state prototypes are prototypes of human collective states. A proposed classification can be refuted, can lack necessary data, can apply in parallel, and can be withdrawn. Each candidate must be checked item by item for entry, exit, observations, counterexamples, falsification, scale, evidence, missing data, and the boundary that no action is authorized. If these conditions are missing, it can only be kept as an impression or recorded as “unknown.” The numbers do not represent a universal evolutionary path from 0 to 6. States allow regression, parallelism, mixing, jumps, dormancy, splits, mergers, takeover, functional transfer, and collapse.

“Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” (§8.5) is an independent life-cycle path and does not depend on the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion, the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance, or any minimum state. Capabilities at a stage, normative justification, and the operating conditions for exit must be judged separately. A match to any prototype cannot itself authorize dissolution, advancement, elimination, or forced conversion.

A physical metaphor must supply a formal mapping, the dimensions of the quantities involved, falsifiable predictions, and conditions for ending the analogy to enter the formal mechanism library. For example, “quantum tunneling” cannot explain why a human collective jumps to another configuration. In this framework, determining that failed attempts are a historical conditional increment beyond the current representation still requires support from a corresponding historical-increment test (see §4.5). No necessary coordination is presumed between biological death and social renewal, or between love and intergenerational transmission. Intergenerational uptake is recorded only in terms of roles, memory, authorization, and handover.

The seven prototypes make no claim to exhaust the states of human collectives. If cases consistently fall outside them, first preserve the unexplained residuals. Then use independent materials to test whether prototypes need to be added, merged, or split. Cases must not be forced into the numbered categories.

Prototype fit, interobserver agreement, stability across time windows, and practical gains over using variables directly are distinct questions. Clear diagnostic cards can make a classification easier to review but cannot establish that it is reliable or more effective than a specialist description. Without actual comparisons, do not report accuracy, predictive gains, or savings in reading time. If the task calls only for direct description of members, channels, and load, the prototypes may be skipped.

## 8.8 Boundary With Actor States and Multi-Circle Projection {#section-8-8}

The seven state prototypes describe candidate states of human intentional collectives under a specified definition of the object. They do not describe individual personality, psychological maturity, or moral rank. Part 9 covers actors’ slow, intermediate-timescale, and fast variables. Part 10 covers parallel, nested, overlapping, bridging, competitive, and temporary relations among multiple collectives. Classifying a circle[^tn-circle-09] to which a person belongs under the prototype of initial expansion and differentiated uptake does not imply that the person’s personality is in that prototype. A person displaying stable capability likewise does not imply that their circle is in the prototype of efficient coordination and flexible expansion or the prototype of adaptive dynamic balance.

Multi-circle projection can represent different circles as fitting different prototypes at the same time. It can also represent the same circle as having a mixed state on different scale axes. State changes must be tied to specific events, channels, and time windows. An institutional change in an outer layer does not automatically update inner relations, and individual exit does not automatically cause the collective to dissolve. If prototype classification adds no value beyond using the variables directly, the prototypes should be skipped in favor of a simpler joint state.

[^tn-circle-09]: Translator's note: Circles may be parallel, nested, overlapping, or bridged.
