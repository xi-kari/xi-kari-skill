# A.11 Open-Ended Undertaking Action (HV11) {#section-a-11}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: Open-ended undertaking examines only actual costs, voluntariness, direction, alternative explanations, and structural consequences. It does not diagnose whether someone has love.

Scope of application: Undertaking in human relationships, organizations, institutions, and public action.

Conditions for suspension: Suspend naming the action “open-ended undertaking” under route two if voluntariness or the ability to refuse or exit cannot be confirmed safely. Candidate actions, costs, coercion risks, gaps in the right to stop, and protection needs may still be recorded under route one. Diagnosing personality or love is always prohibited.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: None.

Method requirements: The normative premise “Love and Undertaking Must Not Be Conscripted” (§14.3); conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: The human-domain clause “Open-Ended Undertaking” (§7.1); the human-domain clause “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility” (§7.1).

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, record of action and cost. Conclusion level: Normative boundary. Conditions: There are grounds for the action and its related costs. Direction, who benefits, and the consequences can each be recorded as known or unknown, but voluntariness or the ability to refuse or exit has not yet received sufficient support. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Describe candidate undertakings, the distribution of costs, coercion risks, gaps in the right to stop, and protection needs. Ceiling: The action must not be called open-ended undertaking. Love or personality must not be diagnosed, and continued undertaking must not be required.

Route two, bounded voluntary undertaking. Conclusion level: Normative boundary. Conditions: There are grounds for the action and its actual costs, its direction is clear, voluntariness and genuine conditions for refusal, exit, or negotiation receive separate support, and relevant alternative explanations have been checked. The outcome is recorded as “realized,” “unrealized,” “harmful,” or “unknown,” and structural success is not a condition for entry. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record a description of bounded voluntary undertaking and the outcome status. Failure or an unknown outcome does not cancel supported facts about the action and its voluntariness. Ceiling: Description of the action only. Do not raise individual undertaking to a group duty or authorize conscription, protection, or resource arrangements.

Route three, structural consequences. Conclusion level: Conditional effect. Conditions: An eligible channel-effect test (§4.4) shows that the action has an above-threshold effect on the preselected structural outcome through the specified channel. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Determine the action’s structural consequences within the specified channel, object, and time window. Ceiling: A structural effect does not establish voluntariness, love, goodness, normative justification, unlimited responsibility, or authorization to act. Support for an effect under route three cannot fill an evidence gap in route two. Coerced action can also have identifiable effects.

Can infer: A description of bounded undertaking actions and their consequences.

Cannot infer: A diagnosis of whether someone has love; that sacrifice amounts to love; that responsibility amounts to unlimited undertaking; that refusal amounts to moral failure.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers individual acts of undertaking, cases involving actors, populations of relationships or organizations, distributions of costs and voluntariness, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers sites of relationships and care, organizational boundaries, digital public spaces, and the reach of benefits across domains. Time span covers windows for immediate action, sustained undertaking, exhaustion, recovery, and effects across generations. Organizational level covers roles in action, relationships or teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers events of undertaking, mechanisms of interaction between actions and costs, meso-level relational structures, institutional arrangements of responsibility, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original actions and costs, sequences of undertaking, cases involving actors, distributions of costs and voluntariness, consequence metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers direct actors and beneficiaries, dependents, those indirectly taking over, second-order spillovers, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers dependencies in care, links of benefit, alternative arrangements for taking over, exit paths, and cost networks across domains. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for demands to take up work, resource use, refusal, stopping, protection, and remedy separately. Differences in voluntariness, costs, and exit need to be preserved.

Effective object: Human arrangements for undertaking in which action, cost, direction, voluntariness, conditions for refusal, and consequences need to be examined separately. Routes one, two, and three are judged under their own conditions, without first screening actions by their structural consequences.

Kept across scales: Costs, voluntariness, direction, alternative explanations, consequences, and rights to stop.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Distribution of voluntariness; relations of representation; cost spillovers; a genuine means of exit and protection provided through representatives.

Changes with scale: Forms of undertaking, positions bearing costs, and beneficiaries can change.

Objects to which it does not apply: Nonhuman systems without intentionality, voluntariness, responsibility, or the capability for meaning.

Cannot be raised to: Individual undertaking raised to a group duty; the concept of human undertaking transferred into the nonhuman core.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Candidate; voluntary and bounded; risk of coercion; unilateral exhaustion; stopping or exit.

Observables: Time and resources invested, opportunities forgone, and bodily and psychological costs of the action; whether refusal, exit, stopping, and renegotiation are genuinely available; direction of the action, positions receiving benefits, and detectable structural consequences; alternative explanations such as coercion, fear, dependence, interests, and acting for show.

Evidence requirements: Actions and costs; conditions for refusal and exit; alternative explanations; benefits and consequences.

Inputs: Orientation anchor (§7.3.3); action uptake layer (§7.3.5); conditional potential field (§7.3.8); power and safety.

Outputs: Distribution of costs; states of relationships and institutions; stopping and repair.

Time window and lag: State immediate costs, sustained undertaking, exhaustion, and recovery lags.

Uncertainty: Record dependence, fear, hidden coercion, and safety of expression.

Local exclusion zone: Positions where people cannot refuse safely, cannot exit, or are masked by moral pressure.

Affected positions: Actors; beneficiaries; dependents; people taking over.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Specific actors; relational practices; arrangements for care or responsibility.

Responsible parties: Those making demands; parties granting authorization; parties responsible as beneficiaries; parties responsible for remedy.

Normative status: Subject to the normative premise “Love and Undertaking Must Not Be Conscripted.” It cannot be commanded or conscripted.

Judgment ceiling: Apply the limits for routes one, two, and three separately. Route one describes only costs, direction, risks, and candidate actions. With corresponding evidence, route two describes the action’s voluntariness and conditions for refusal and exit. Route three judges structural consequences within a limited scope only if the corresponding channel-effect test and causal conditions hold. Judgments at any level must not diagnose personality, prove love or goodness, infer a duty to undertake, or authorize real-world action. Ethical naming is separately subject to the requirements on normative premises in the governance clause “Keeping Open-Ended Undertaking, Normative Premises, and Metaphorical Drift Separate” (§16.14). Reaching route three does not satisfy that requirement.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only descriptions of voluntariness, actual costs, direction, alternative explanations, structural consequences, and needs for stopping and protection. It does not authorize protective measures, demands to take up work, requisition of resources, or adjudication of personality. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: One-sided sacrifice that cannot be refused is praised as love or responsibility; a claim of undertaking lacks evidence of the stated action or cost. Actual failed attempts and cases with unknown outcomes are recorded separately. Their action descriptions must not be categorically withdrawn for lack of structural success.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), actors can refuse to be represented and explain coercion, costs, alternative explanations, and restrictions on exit through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original judgment about the undertaking or the original chain of demands.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline revoke the undertaking designation and related demands, and actually restore the state of refusal, exit, records, and resources. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.

The “nine-axis scale” item is not a single-axis “micro–macro” label but the complete record of nine axes: aggregation level, which must state units, population, distribution, and aggregation rules; spatial extent, meaning physical space and digital boundaries; time span, meaning time windows, lags, and cycles; organizational level, meaning roles, teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies; causal level, meaning events, interaction mechanisms, meso-level structures, institutions, and systemic conditions; observation resolution, meaning original events, sequences, individual cases, distributions, metrics, and summaries, with compression loss recorded; scope of influence, meaning the direct, indirect, second-order, cross-domain, and intergenerational range of those affected; network topology scope; and scope of jurisdiction and authorization. An expansion of aggregation level, spatial extent, organizational level, or scope of influence does not automatically expand the scope of authorization. Observing more cannot confer greater authority to act.

Recorded identities and the identity criterion belong to the identity-criterion item of the object’s definition. Relationships and channels of effect are written in the “Inputs” and “Uptake carriers” items, or fall under the conditions for causal claims. Membership and affected positions are written in the “Affected positions” and “Local exclusion zone” items and in the items on observation. None of them can stand in for a scale axis. Network topology scope among the nine scale axes (written N) shares a letter with the five normative premises (N1–N5), and the two must not be conflated. Below, “the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion” refers to the latter (§14.3).

All eleven variables share the same limit on action. The variables themselves produce only descriptions, evidence gaps, and needs for action. They do not authorize real-world adjustments. Their output ceilings are as follows.

| Variable | Descriptions or Needs This Variable Can Produce |
| --- | --- |
| Structural Domain | Candidate structural domains, boundary disputes, and needs for more evidence |
| Boundaries and Interfaces | Boundary states, interface barriers, exclusion risks, and testing needs |
| Orientation Anchor | Candidate anchors, diverse expressions, comparison results, and needs for more evidence |
| Generative Node | Candidate classifications by generative condition, generative subject, and generative event, state transitions, and needs for more evidence |
| Action Uptake Layer | Uptake carriers, responsible parties, costs, capacity, rights to stop, gaps in uptake, and needs to reduce load, add resources, or reallocate them |
| Drive–Uptake Chain | Chain connectivity, time lags, losses, interruptions, costs, and uptake needs |
| Feedback Write-Back | Intake, changes in institutional elements, execution, duration, gaps in write-back, and needs for procedural repair |
| Conditional Potential Field | Candidate channels through which conditions could have effects, differences across positions, masking of evidence, and needs to reduce risk |
| Structural Load | Load, capacity, recovery, local overload, and needs to reduce load and add resources |
| Evolutionary Phase | Component applicability, prototype matches, and mixed states under the selected route, limited explanations of phase mechanisms or historical conditional increments, the independent path status of “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise,” uncertainty, and observation needs |
| Open-Ended Undertaking Action | Voluntariness, actual costs, direction, alternative explanations, structural consequences, and needs for stopping and protection |

However obvious these needs may seem, any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures. The variables must not authorize testing, load reduction, adding resources, repair, protection, attribution of responsibility, exploratory action, advancement, exit arrangements, or other action on their own.
