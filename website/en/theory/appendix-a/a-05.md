# A.5 Action Uptake Layer (HV05) {#section-a-5}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: Uptake carriers (§7.2.4) that execute, transmit, maintain, record, provide care, or repair must be stated separately from responsible parties (§7.2.5), cost bearers, beneficiaries, and rights to stop.

Scope of application: Human structures requiring sustained action, maintenance, care, or execution.

Conditions for suspension: People who execute with limited authority are classified by default as primarily responsible, or the capability to take up work is treated as an obligation.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: None.

Method requirements: Conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: The human-domain clause “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility” (§7.1).

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, uptake and classification of responsibility. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: Uptake carriers, bearers of costs of the same type, beneficiaries, rights to stop, responsible parties, resources, and capacity can be stated separately under the clause “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility.” Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record current uptake carriers, tasks, costs, capacity, rights to stop, types of responsibility, and gaps in uptake. Ceiling: Only the current classification and the description of gaps. The capability to take up work does not become an obligation, and an uptake carrier does not become a responsible party.

Route two, functional effect of carriers. Conclusion level: Conditional effect. Conditions: An eligible channel-effect test (§4.4) shows that replacing, interrupting, replenishing, or reducing load on the specified carrier has a channel effect above the threshold on the preselected functional outcome. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Record the candidate uptake effect of the specified carrier on the measured dimensions of function, capacity, delay, or loss. Ceiling: Unmeasured dimensions remain unknown. Functional effects must not directly generate responsibility, a duty to make sacrifices, or authorization to reallocate resources.

Route three, historical-increment intertemporal reproduction. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: The current uptake channel already has support from a channel-effect test, and a historical-increment test (§4.5) shows that its historical variables provide a conditional increment for subsequent uptake or reproduction outcomes. Further inferential premises: The channel-effect test supporting the current uptake channel; this historical-increment test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record a candidate for historical-increment intertemporal uptake or reproduction, beyond the current representation, within the specified time window and carrier. Ceiling: Do not infer historical inevitability, irreversibility, attribution of responsibility, or a duty to continue taking up the work.

Route four, historical carrier trace. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: A “Historical Trace Carriers” test (§4.2) receives support on the specific carrier and durability criterion, with exactly one of each selected in advance. Further inferential premises: This “Historical Trace Carriers” test. Further method requirements: Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Determine durable traces in the human domain within the specified carrier, trace observables, and time window, and submit predefined candidate historical variables to a historical-increment test. Ceiling: This “Historical Trace Carriers” test does not establish future path effects, intertemporal reproduction, a repair window, responsibility, or action. Evidence for these conclusions needs to be obtained separately under the carrier route or the historical-increment route, the corresponding conclusion rules (§4.10), and normative procedures.

Route five, carrier intertemporal reproduction. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: An eligible channel-effect test supports the effect of the specified uptake channel on the stated subsequent outcomes within the stated time window, and the capabilities, rules, resources, or relationships currently serving as carriers, their retention or replenishment across several cycles, actual continuity, and discriminating causal materials are verified under §4.5. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record a candidate for intertemporal uptake or reproduction through carrier transmission within the specified carriers, cycles, and outcomes. Ceiling: Do not determine a historical increment beyond the current representation or extrapolate long-term effects from a single functional effect. The fact of uptake does not generate a duty or authorization to continue taking up the work.

Can infer: Gaps in uptake; mismatches between tasks and resources; distinctions among responsibilities.

Cannot infer: That the most visible person is the one primarily responsible; that someone should take up the work because they can; that facilities or procedures bear responsibility solely because they execute a function. Organizational responsibility is assessed separately under the organization’s standing as a subject and the specific rules.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers individual tasks or uptake events, cases involving carriers, the uptake population, distributions of costs and capacity, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers work sites, team or organizational boundaries, digital systems, and the reach of services across domains. Time span covers task cycles, maintenance windows, recovery lags, and the period in which responsibility applies. Organizational level covers execution roles, teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers events of execution, mechanisms of interaction between tasks and resources, meso-level uptake structures, institutional arrangements of responsibility, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original task logs, sequences of uptake, cases involving carriers, distributions of costs and capacity, performance metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers those directly taking up the work, people dependent on services, positions that indirectly receive benefits or bear costs, second-order spillovers, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers dependencies in uptake, alternative paths, single-point bottlenecks, and service networks across domains. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for task assignment, stopping, resource adjustments, attribution of responsibility, and remedy separately. State the scale separately for uptake carriers and for responsible parties.

Effective object: People, positions, procedures, facilities, or institutions that actually execute, transmit, maintain, record, provide care, or repair.

Kept across scales: An uptake carrier does not amount to a responsible party; costs and benefits are stated separately; rights to stop.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Aggregation of tasks; representation and delegation; six kinds of responsibility; external costs.

Changes with scale: Uptake carriers and responsible parties can change with the level.

Objects to which it does not apply: Nonhuman processes without action by a subject, responsibility, or maintenance requirements.

Cannot be raised to: Uptake by an individual directly equated with organizational responsibility.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Adequate; fragile; overloaded; disrupted; replaced.

Observables: Logs of actual task execution, maintenance, recording, and repair; flows of resources, capacity, time, and costs; records of rights to stop, alternative arrangements, and transfer of uptake; grounds for decisions, authorization, oversight, benefit, and remedy.

Evidence requirements: Task flows; working hours and resources; maintenance records; records of stopping and refusal. If the objective concerns overall burden, check management, being on call, conditions for those taking over, and continuity separately; a judgment about one task need not collect all of these.

Inputs: Needs arising from generation; resources; authorization; roles.

Outputs: Realization of state transitions; cost distribution; structural load (§7.3.9).

Time window and lag: State schedules, maintenance cycles, backlogs, and recovery lags.

Uncertainty: Record hidden labor, informal care, and costs outside the boundary.

Local exclusion zone: People taking up work with limited authority, informally, or without the ability to exit.

Affected positions: People taking up the work; beneficiaries; people served; replacements.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Personnel; positions; procedures; facilities; institutions.

Responsible parties: Parties responsible for conduct; parties responsible for decisions; parties responsible for authorization; parties responsible for oversight; parties responsible as beneficiaries; parties responsible for remedy.

Normative status: The fact of uptake does not generate a duty to continue taking up the work.

Judgment ceiling: Route one is limited to classification of uptake and responsibility. Route two is limited to identified functional effects of carriers. Route four is limited to durable traces supported by the “Historical Trace Carriers” test. Route five explains carrier reproduction under §4.5 and with channel evidence covering subsequent time windows. Route three explains historical increments beyond the current representation only with an additional historical-increment test. Each route is limited by its own materials and time window. No route may substitute for the others or create an unlimited duty to take up the work.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only descriptions of uptake carriers, responsible parties, costs, capacity, rights to stop, and gaps in uptake, along with needs to reduce load, add resources, or reallocate them. It does not authorize changes in tasks, resource allocation, attribution of responsibility, or protective action. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: The most visible person with limited execution authority is not the party responsible for decisions or authorization, or responsible as a beneficiary; an automated facility takes up transmission tasks but cannot bear moral or legal responsibility.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), people taking up work can raise objections to tasks, resources, costs, restrictions on the right to stop, and attribution of responsibility through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original chain of task assignment or attribution.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline revoke erroneous task, resource, or responsibility statuses, actually restore the previous task state and record state, and carry out authorized remedies. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
