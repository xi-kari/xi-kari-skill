# A.1 Structural Domain (HV01) {#section-a-1}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: The definition of a structural object puts forward only a candidate human object. A limited effective structural domain is determined, within the scope of a pre-registered grouping-gain test, only if that test shows that the candidate grouping achieves an out-of-sample or extrapolation gain above the threshold on the preselected outcome relative to a matched null model.

Scope of application: Relationships, teams, organizations, institutions, and public issues.

Conditions for suspension: The object, boundary, scale, time window, or basis for treating it as the same object is insufficient for the selected route. When formal support for a grouping gain is sought, the corresponding null model or pre-registration is incomplete.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: Structural object (§3.1).

Method requirements: Constraint on Object Declaration (§4.8.1); conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: None.

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, candidate object. Conclusion level: Candidate description. Conditions: The object’s definition is complete, but there is not yet an eligible grouping-gain test that has received support. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record it as a candidate human object, and record the set of materials, boundary disputes, and the need for a further grouping-gain test. Ceiling: Candidate object description only. A reference to the object, with its boundary and uncertainty, may be supplied to the other ten variables, but the reference does not count as an effective structural domain with a supported grouping gain.

Route two, effective structural domain. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: Within the same object, scale, time window, identity criterion, and extrapolation unit, a pre-registered grouping-gain test receives support. Further inferential premises: This grouping-gain test. Further method requirements: Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Determine the limited effective structural domain within the scope of this test, and state the strength of support for the grouping gain, the robustness of that gain to tested boundary perturbations, and its applicable window. Ceiling: Limited to the pre-registered scale, time window, identity criterion, and extrapolation unit. Judgments about the object’s ultimate nature, a unified will, or authorization must not be made.

Can infer: Under route one, a candidate object, boundary disputes, and limited descriptive references may be recorded. The limited effective structural domain supported by the gain, and its strength of support, are determined only within the object, scale, time window, outcome, and extrapolation unit pre-registered for the grouping-gain test.

Cannot infer: That naming a group makes it an objective community; that being in the same circumstances means having the same will.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers relational or event units, the candidate member population, distributions inside and outside the boundary, and grouping rules. Spatial extent covers common locations, organizational boundaries, digital platforms, and external environments across domains. Time span covers the identification window, cycles of membership change, and boundary history. Organizational level covers roles, teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers originating events, interaction mechanisms, meso-level relational structures, and institutional and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original interactions, event sequences, individual member cases, boundary distributions, metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers direct members, people excluded, people indirectly affected, second-order spillovers, and positions across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers membership relations, boundary connections, isolated points, and bridging across domains. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for naming the object, adopting the boundary, and subsequent action separately. For an axis that does not apply, the reason it does not apply must be stated.

Effective object: A candidate object put forward by the definition of a structural object. Only after it passes a pre-registered grouping-gain test, reaching the threshold relative to a matched null model, is it determined to be a limited effective structural domain within the scope of that test.

Kept across scales: The object’s definition; participating and affected positions.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Units and population; representativeness; scope of authorization; positions with low visibility.

Changes with scale: Effective membership, relationships, and what counts as the same object can change with scale.

Objects to which it does not apply: Nonhuman systems without intent, institutions, or interfaces for responsibility.

Cannot be raised to: A local group directly representing all affected people.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Candidate; identifiable; boundary disputed; does not hold.

Observables: Differences in relationship density and constraints inside and outside the boundary; records of members entering, exiting, or being excluded; repeated co-occurrence of common problems, resource channels, or institutional rules; whether the grouping gain remains stable after the grouping rule changes.

Evidence requirements: The candidate object’s definition and the records used to determine whether it remains the same object; the pre-registration of the grouping-gain test and a matched null model; training results and out-of-sample or extrapolation results; a comparison of gains for the candidate and competing groupings.

Inputs: The definition of a structural object supplies only the elements of the candidate object and is no evidence that the structure holds; the pre-registered grouping-gain test with its matched null model, threshold, model class, and sample or extrapolation unit; observation positions, competing groupings, and the requirements of object declaration.

Outputs: Route one supplies the other variables with a reference to the candidate object, its boundary, time window, and uncertainty; route two additionally supplies the limited effective scope supported by the grouping gain. The grounds for the object’s existence and for domain facts are kept separate from the grouping gain.

Time window and lag: State the identification window and the lags in boundary changes and in membership changes.

Uncertainty: Record boundary disputes, absent members, and the extent of observation coverage.

Local exclusion zone: Positions where people cannot express themselves safely, or that were not sampled, must not be represented by the aggregate.

Affected positions: Members; people excluded; cost bearers outside the boundary.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Relationship networks; organizational boundaries; institutional records.

Responsible parties: The analyst who proposes the structural domain judgment; the decision maker who uses the judgment.

Normative status: A descriptive variable of the human domain. It confers no normative justification.

Judgment ceiling: A limited effective structural domain at the level of explanation may be determined only if the grouping-gain test passes, and only within the pre-registered object, scale, time window, outcome, and extrapolation unit. Otherwise the grouping gain is not determined to be supported, while the reference to the candidate object and object facts with separate grounds are retained as usual.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only descriptions of candidate structural domains, boundary disputes, and needs for more evidence. It does not authorize inclusion, exclusion, or action. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: People repeatedly present in the same place lack stable relationships or common constraints; a group defined by the analyst disappears as soon as the grouping rule changes.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), members and people at affected positions can raise objections to the boundary, representativeness, and the identity criterion through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original naming or decision chain.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline actually revoke the determination of the structural domain, remove its effect on the scope of downstream objects, and return it to a candidate grouping. Object facts with separate grounds are not revoked along with it. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
