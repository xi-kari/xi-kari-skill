# A.9 HV09 Structural Load (Full Interface Card) {#section-a-9}

A. Identity, Proposition, and Scope

| Field | What to Record |
| --- | --- |
| Interface ID (`id`) | HV09 |
| Qualified ID (`qualified_id`) | `human_variable:HV09` |
| Name (`name`) | Structural Load |
| Claim type (`claim_type`) | H |
| Contract role (`contract_role`) | `human_variable_interface` |
| Proposition (`proposition`) | Structural load in human structures must be recorded together with tasks, coordination losses, maintenance requirements, capacity, recovery margins, and positions bearing costs. |
| Scope (`scope`) | Human structures under conditions of continuing operation, maintenance, care, or high pressure |
| Conditions for suspension (`pause_condition`) | Metaphors of entropy, fragility, or resilience are used without identifying tasks, capacity, and mechanisms of recovery |

C. Nine-Axis Scale and Object Contract

| Field | What to Record |
| --- | --- |
| Nine-axis scale profile (`scale_profile`) | SP=&lt;A,X,T,O,C,R,I,N,J&gt;. A=Aggregation level covers individual task loads, cases of uptake, populations of positions or groups, distributions of load and capacity, and aggregation rules. X=Spatial extent covers sites of work or care, organizational boundaries, digital workspaces, and the extent of outsourcing across domains. T=Time span covers instantaneous peaks, persistent backlogs, recovery lags, and windows across periods and generations. O=Organizational level covers uptake roles, teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. C=Causal level covers task events, mechanisms of interaction between tasks and capacity, meso-level bottleneck structures, institutional allocation, and systemic conditions. R=Resolution covers original task and working-hour records, load sequences, cases of uptake, capacity distributions, delay and error metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. I=Scope of influence covers people directly taking up the work, people dependent on services, indirect substitutes, second-order spillovers, and costs across domains and generations. N=Network topology scope covers task dependencies, key bottlenecks, substitute nodes, recovery paths, and outsourcing networks across domains. J=Scope of jurisdiction and authorization requires separate authorization records for task assignment, resource adjustments, stopping, use of performance evaluations, and remedy. Preserve the distributions of load and capacity |
| Effective object (`effective_object`) | The structural relation of tasks and coordination requirements to available capacity and recovery margins within a given window |
| Scale invariants (`scale_invariants`) | 1. Load, capacity, recovery, and positions bearing costs |

E. Uptake, Responsibility, Normative Status, Ceilings, and Correction

| Field | What to Record |
| --- | --- |
| Uptake carriers (`carrier`) | 1. Personnel<br>2. Positions<br>3. Procedures<br>4. Facilities<br>5. Budgets |
| Responsible parties (`responsible_subject`) | 1. Task assigners<br>2. Resource allocators<br>3. Parties granting authorization<br>4. Parties responsible for oversight<br>5. Parties responsible for remedy |
| Normative status (`normative_status`) | High efficiency or the ability to carry a heavy load does not establish normative justification |
| Judgment ceiling (`judgment_ceiling`) | R0 describes tasks, capacity, and recovery within the same window. R1 uses G2 and CM-LOAD to explain a specified channel within stated limits. R3 uses §4.5 to judge cumulative damage or delayed recovery through transmission by a current carrier. R2 judges historical increments beyond the current representation only with additional G3. Persistent correlation, a temporary overload, or an overall average cannot substitute for the corresponding evidence. |
| Action ceiling (`action_ceiling`) | This variable produces only descriptions of load, capacity, recovery, local overload, and needs to reduce load or add resources. It does not authorize task reduction, resource commitments, personnel action based on performance, or compelling people to bear the load. Any real-world adjustment separately requires C12, explicit N premises at runtime, J authorization, and O procedures |
| Counterexamples (`counterexamples`) | 1. Average capacity overall is adequate, but some key uptake positions remain overloaded<br>2. Metaphors of entropy or resilience alone fail to identify tasks, capacity, or recovery channels |
| Appeal (`appeal`) | Under `appeal_and_rollback_rule`, people taking up work can report hidden labor, overload, and recovery needs through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection. This triggers review independent of the original judgment about load or the chain of performance or task decisions |
| Rollback (`rollback`) | Under `appeal_and_rollback_rule`, once rollback is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline revoke erroneous load judgments and their effects on performance or responsibility and actually restore task, resource, and record states. Preserve versions and verification of completion |
