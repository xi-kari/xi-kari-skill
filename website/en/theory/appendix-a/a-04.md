# A.4 Generative Node (HV04) {#section-a-4}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: Generation must be described as three separate items: the generative condition (§7.2.1), the generative subject (§7.2.2), and the generative event (§7.2.3). Emergent generation can occur without an identifiable subject.

Scope of application: Formation of new actions, organizations, institutions, or state transitions in human structures.

Conditions for suspension: Suspend the relevant claims about the subject or the type when a condition is personified, an event is treated as a subject, or standing as a subject has not been confirmed on any grounds. Route one may still record the candidate generative condition, generative subject, and generative event separately, along with the status “subject not yet identified.” A subject not yet identified is not taken as proof that there is none.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: None.

Method requirements: Conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: None.

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, sorting into categories. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: The items required for the generative condition, the generative subject, and the generative event can each be stated separately, and the two statuses “emergence without a subject” and “subject not yet identified” are retained. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record a candidate generative condition, a candidate generative subject, and a candidate generative event separately. The three do not substitute for one another. Ceiling: Distinction by category only. The existence of a condition, subject, or event does not establish either of the other two or a causal mechanism of generation.

Route two, generative mechanism. Conclusion level: Mechanism explanation. Conditions: A pre-registered channel-effect test (§4.4) identifies an above-threshold effect of the generative condition, the generative subject, or a channel of emergence without a subject on the state transition of the specified generative event. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Record a candidate mechanism of generation within the specified scale, time window, and channel, and its classification by generative condition, generative subject, and generative event. Ceiling: Conditions must not be personified, a subject must not be inferred backward from an event, and normative justification, responsibility, or authorization must not be inferred from the fact of generation.

Can infer: Conditional paths of generation; generation with or without a subject.

Cannot infer: That a technology or a crisis has intent; that a generative subject automatically has continuing authorization.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers units of exposure to conditions, of action by subjects, and of generative events, with their respective populations of cases, distributions, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers sites of generation, organizational or platform boundaries, areas of diffusion, and cross-domain environments. Time span covers the period of accumulation of conditions, the window of the subject’s action, the time of the event, and diffusion lag. Organizational level covers roles in action, generative teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers generative events, mechanisms of interaction between subjects and conditions, meso-level structures of generation, institutional arrangements, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers the original conditions, actions, and events, sequences of generation, individual cases, outcome distributions, transition metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers direct participants in generation, people taking up the work, indirectly affected people, second-order consequences, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers propagation of conditions, collaboration among subjects, diffusion of events, and emergent connections. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for identification of generation, initiation of change, commitment of resources, and action concerning diffusion separately. State the scale separately for the generative condition, the generative subject, and the generative event.

Effective object: A combination of conditions, subjects, and events that forms a detectable state transition.

Kept across scales: Separation of the three categories of generative condition, generative subject, and generative event.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: New units and populations; relations of representation; kinds of responsibility; external impacts.

Changes with scale: Generative subjects, conditions, and events can change with scale.

Objects to which it does not apply: Stable descriptions with neither the formation of a new state nor a claim about generation.

Cannot be raised to: A condition or an event treated as a subject with intent.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Potential; triggered; forming; interrupted; diffusing.

Observables: Records of when candidate conditions appear, change, or are removed; the generative subject’s capabilities, authorization, decisions, and actual actions; the pre-registered state transition from before to after the generative event; in emergence without a subject, records of the bridge between local interaction and aggregate outcome.

Evidence requirements: Records of initiation; windows for conditions; actions by subjects; mechanisms of interaction without a subject.

Inputs: Orientation anchor (§7.3.3); resources and institutional conditions; conditions for causal claims.

Outputs: Need for uptake; state transitions; the start of a chain of responsibility.

Time window and lag: State the accumulation of conditions, triggering events, and lags in formation.

Uncertainty: Record joint generation, emergence without a subject, and subjects that cannot be identified.

Local exclusion zone: Overlooked informal initiators and affected positions.

Affected positions: Initiators; people taking up the work; beneficiaries; people affected.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Initiators; procedures; technical facilities; relationship networks.

Responsible parties: Those who actually act; decision makers; parties granting authorization.

Normative status: The fact of generation establishes neither normative justification nor a complete attribution of responsibility.

Judgment ceiling: When the mechanism chain is complete, judgments may go up to the level of explanation.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only candidate classifications by generative condition, generative subject, and generative event, descriptions of state transitions, and needs for more evidence. It does not authorize starting, spreading, or stopping a generative process. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: A technological condition is incorrectly described as a generative subject with a goal; an emergent process without a unified initiator is forced into an attribution to one visible person.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), people classified as generative subjects can raise objections to attributions of intent, role, and authorization through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original classification or decision chain.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline correct the categories of generative condition, generative subject, and generative event, and actually remove erroneous attributions of intent or responsibility and their downstream effects. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
