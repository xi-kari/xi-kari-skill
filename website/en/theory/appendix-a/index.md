# Appendix A The Human Variables in Detail {#part-title-17}

This appendix describes the eleven human variables of Part 7 one by one. Each is described in five groups: proposition and scope of application; grounds and limits on conclusions; scale and object; state, evidence, and relations between variables; and uptake, responsibility, ceilings, and correction.

The items describe the structure of a variable; specific evidence needs to be obtained according to the selected support route and what the task requires. Record separately what is unknown, not applicable, unobservable, and withheld for protection (§2.9); for what is “unknown,” where the cases can be told apart, note whether it is not yet collected, subject to conflicting sources, or still indistinguishable after attempts. In the cards, a component that is “missing” means that the component does not exist; that is content, not a missing-data state. Filling in every item is not a precondition for providing everyday help or for forming an understanding. “None” written for a kind of grounds means only that nothing is listed of that kind. It does not mean that the evidence is sufficient or that an empirical claim holds. Each support route must be read as a whole, one route at a time. Favorable conditions from different routes must not be pieced together after the fact.
