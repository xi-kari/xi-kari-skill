# A.8 Conditional Potential Field (HV08) {#section-a-8}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: Resources, institutions, relationships, power, safety, metrics, platforms, and historical conditions enter a conditional explanation for that outcome family only if they change, through a detectable mechanism, the single outcome selected in advance by the corresponding route, and receive support from the corresponding “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test (§4.2). The outcome is evidence coverage, safety of expression, object behavior, or reflexive response.

Scope of application: Settings in which human action is affected by context, institutions, and positions of power.

Conditions for suspension: The potential field is treated as an all-purpose background, a subject with intent, or a moral label.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: None.

Method requirements: Observation Position and Model Limits (§4.8.2); conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: None.

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, condition inventory. Conclusion level: Candidate description. Conditions: Resources, rules, positions, safety, metrics, platforms, AI mediation, or historical accumulations can be listed by position, scale, and time window, but there is not yet an eligible “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record candidate conditions, differences across positions, blind spots in observation, competing explanations, and needs for more evidence. Ceiling: Call the output only an inventory of conditions or a candidate channel. The conditions must not be personified, and it must not be said that power, mediation, or reflexive effects have been established.

Route two, position or mediation effect. Conclusion level: Conditional effect. Conditions: A “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test receives support on the single success criterion selected in advance among evidence coverage, safety of expression, and object behavior. Further inferential premises: This “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Determine channels of masking, amplification, or behavioral response within the test’s position, mediation, outcome family, and time window. Ceiling: Do not extrapolate to unselected outcome families. Do not infer malice, responsibility, or automatic action from a position or mediation effect.

Route three, reflexive response. Conclusion level: Mechanism explanation. Conditions: A “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test has reflexive response as its single criterion selected in advance, observation, naming, scoring, or publication reaches the object through an actual channel, and the test receives support. Further inferential premises: This “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. Further method requirements: Conditional Observational Participation (§4.8.3); conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Determine reflexive response within the specified observation or publication channel and time window. Ceiling: One response does not count as persistent reflexivity. The result must not be used to conceal observation, suppress expression, or broaden authorization.

Route four, carrier reflexive persistence. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: A “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test receives support on its single reflexive-response criterion selected in advance, within the stated scope across time windows. In addition, it is verified under §4.5 that the response enters a current carrier, is retained or updated there, and has a sustained effect, with repeated stimuli, continuing observation, shared changes, and material supporting other explanations compared. Further inferential premises: This “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. Further method requirements: Conditional Observational Participation; conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Determine sustained reflexive response within the specified observation or publication channel, current carrier, and time window. Ceiling: Do not extrapolate persistence from the immediate effect obtained in the “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. This does not qualify as a historical increment in the sense of a historical-increment test (§4.5). Do not use the result to conceal observation, suppress expression, or broaden authorization.

Can infer: Conditional opportunities, constraints, masking, and amplification.

Cannot infer: That conditions determine individual behavior; that a position of power proves malice; that an environment has intent.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers individual exposure or evaluation events, cases at particular positions, the population subject to the conditions, distributions of responses, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers sites of interaction, organizational or platform boundaries, public digital spaces, and environments across domains. Time span covers windows for accumulation of exposure, response, reflexive change, and fading. Organizational level covers roles in action and evaluation, teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers exposure events, mechanisms of interaction between conditions and behavior, meso-level power structures, institutional rules, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original exposures, expressions, and behaviors, time series, cases at particular positions, response distributions, platform metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers people directly evaluated, indirectly affected people, second-order reflexive consequences, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers links of power and information, mediation nodes, areas of masking, amplification paths, and propagation across domains. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for setting rules, using metrics, public evaluation, human review, and action separately. Differences across positions need to be compared.

Effective object: Route one lists a set of potentially relevant conditions. Routes two and three judge their effects on the preselected outcomes based on the corresponding evidence. The term “potential field” presupposes no potential function, common unit, or physical conservation law.

Kept across scales: The mechanism chain from conditions to behavior or evidence.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Distribution of positions; heterogeneity of conditions; externalities across domains; scope of authorization.

Changes with scale: Key conditions and the strength of effects can change with scale.

Objects to which it does not apply: Nonhuman systems without intentional action, power, or institutional positions.

Cannot be raised to: Local conditions generalized directly into the motives of all subjects.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Support; constraint; masking; amplification; mixed.

Observables: Differences in behavior before and after changes in resources, rules, platforms, or conditions of disclosure; safety of expression, evidence coverage, and absence rates at different positions; changes in visibility and action taken before and after metrics, scoring, or AI mediation; whether the candidate channel effect exceeds the predetermined threshold under the comparison conditions.

Evidence requirements: Resources and rules; differences across positions; changes in platforms or metrics; behavioral responses.

Inputs: Boundaries and interfaces; observation positions; conditions for causal claims.

Outputs: Feasible paths; expression and evidence; generation and destabilization.

Time window and lag: State lags in the accumulation of conditions, in response, and in fading.

Uncertainty: Record unobservable conditions, collinearity, and reflexive changes.

Local exclusion zone: Positions invisible due to barriers involving safety, identity, or platforms.

Affected positions: Advantaged positions; low-power positions; mediators; people evaluated.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Institutions; resource allocation; platforms; metrics; relationship networks; historical accumulations.

Responsible parties: Rule makers; platform operators; resource allocators; decision makers for action.

Normative status: Advantages under the conditions or outcomes of systemic selection do not establish normative justification.

Judgment ceiling: Route one only lists candidate conditions. Routes two and three form judgments about position-related effects or reflexive responses within the outcome and time window selected for the “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test. Route four must also have evidence of a current carrier, retention across time windows, and a sustained effect. Immediate effects are not extrapolated into persistence. Establishing a channel produces no conclusion about malice, responsibility, or authorization.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only descriptions of candidate channels through conditions, differences across positions, masking of evidence, and needs to reduce risk. It does not authorize changing rules and platforms, allocating resources, evaluating subjects, or taking action concerning them. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: Under the same institutional conditions, people at positions with different safety and power take opposite actions; using a label such as “power” or “platform” in place of an actual causal channel leaves the state change unexplained.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), people at different positions can submit differences in mechanisms, absence signals, and impacts on safety through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original judgment about the potential field or the original decision chain.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline actually revoke unsupported attribution to a potential field, remove labels on positions and their downstream effects on evaluation and action, and restore the unresolved status. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
