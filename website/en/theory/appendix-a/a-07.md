# A.7 Feedback Write-Back (HV07) {#section-a-7}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: An appeal, audit, or piece of feedback constitutes institutional write-back in a human structure only if it changes records, rules, resources, roles, responsibilities, memory, or stopping conditions and the corresponding change has actually been executed.

Scope of application: Human structures with feedback, appeals, audits, or governance procedures.

Conditions for suspension: Suspend the claim that “institutional write-back holds” if there is only a receipt, statement of position, or publication without a state update, or if a change to institutional elements such as records and rules has not actually been executed. Limited statuses such as submitted, intake complete, and institutional elements changed are still classified under route one.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: Effective feedback (§3.3).

Method requirements: Conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: The human-domain clause “Institutional Write-Back” (§7.1).

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, write-back classification. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: Input channels, receipts, the content of institutional elements before and after the change, execution records, time of entry into effect, duration, and the status of stopping or restoration can be checked separately. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Distinguish the write-back statuses of not submitted, submitted, intake complete, institutional elements changed, executed, continuing, or no longer in effect. Ceiling: Only a change to institutional elements that is actually executed counts as institutional write-back. A single write-back does not count as learning, and write-back does not amount to successful correction. Retain the procedural value of upholding the original decision for reasons, and any other changes that have occurred, according to their own evidence.

Route two, causal feedback. Conclusion level: Mechanism explanation. Conditions: An eligible channel-effect test (§4.4) shows that an institutional return channel changes the preselected subsequent state or transition relative to a condition without a return or with the channel blocked. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Determine effective feedback and effects of institutional write-back within the specified institutional elements, channel, and time window. Ceiling: Learning, long-term repair, normative justification, or broader authorization must not be inferred from the presence of feedback.

Route three, historical-increment feedback-mediated learning. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: Effective feedback already has support from a channel-effect test, and a historical-increment test (§4.5) shows that a retainable update provides a historical conditional increment for a predetermined task over repeated rounds. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test; this historical-increment test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record a candidate for historical-increment feedback-mediated learning, beyond the current representation, limited to the specified task, rounds, and time window. Ceiling: It must not be claimed that the institution as a whole has learned, that repair is complete, or that its direction is normatively right.

Route four, carrier feedback-mediated learning. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: Effective feedback already has support from an eligible channel-effect test, and the current carrier of the update, retention and actual use across rounds, a comparison for the predetermined task, and the causal basis for that comparison are verified under the carrier route of the conclusion rule “Effective Feedback and Feedback-Mediated Learning” (§4.11.3) and under §4.5. The test of immediate feedback does not automatically cover later task outcomes. Further inferential premises: This channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record a candidate for feedback-mediated learning through carrier transmission within the specified update carrier, task, and rounds. Ceiling: This does not qualify as a historical increment in the sense of the historical-increment route of the “Feedback-Mediated Learning” mechanism (§6.3) or of a historical-increment test. Do not directly call training, archiving, write-back, or one success learning. Do not claim the institution as a whole has improved.

Can infer: Effective write-back, blocked write-back, and feedback in appearance only.

Cannot infer: That the presence of a channel brings learning; that one update amounts to long-term repair; that silence amounts to consent.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers individual feedback events or appeals, cases of write-back, the feedback population, distributions of intake and execution, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers submission channels, organizational or platform boundaries, institutional jurisdictions, and the reach of appeals across domains. Time span covers submission, intake, changes in institutional elements, execution, duration, and review lags. Organizational level covers feedback roles, intake teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers feedback events, mechanisms of interaction in write-back, meso-level procedural structures, institutional rules, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original feedback, processing sequences, cases of write-back, outcome distributions, timeliness metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers people directly appealing or taking up the work, indirectly affected people, second-order institutional consequences, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers feedback channels, intake nodes, review paths, blockages, and connections across levels. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for intake, changes to institutional elements, execution, stopping, restoration, and remedy separately. Paths across levels need to be stated.

Effective object: A return channel that changes a subsequent institutional state or transition.

Kept across scales: Source and channel of feedback, the institutional elements written back, and subsequent changes.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Representativeness of feedback; write-back paths across levels; aggregation loss; external review.

Changes with scale: Write-back carriers, lags, and responsible parties can change.

Objects to which it does not apply: Processes without records, rules, resources, roles, or stopping conditions.

Cannot be raised to: Feedback from an individual case directly representing the population’s views.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Arrival; intake; write-back; blockage; distortion.

Observables: Evidence of submission and intake of feedback or an appeal; differences in records, rules, resources, roles, responsibilities, or stopping conditions before and after the change; execution records, time of entry into effect, and duration of the change; review, reversal, remedy, and subsequent state changes.

Evidence requirements: Original feedback; intake trail; the content of institutional elements before and after the change; subsequent changes to rules or resources.

Inputs: Feedback sources; safe channels; responsible parties; review procedures.

Outputs: Updates to records, rules, resources, roles, responsibilities, memory, or stopping conditions.

Time window and lag: State time limits for submission, intake, decision, execution, and further review.

Uncertainty: Record feedback that did not arrive, anonymity for protection, and handling that cannot be observed.

Local exclusion zone: Positions without safe submission or digital access, or under threat of retaliation.

Affected positions: People submitting material; people evaluated; people carrying out the action; beneficiaries of the institution.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Appeal systems; audit procedures; meeting records; repositories of rules; chains of responsibility.

Responsible parties: Parties responsible for intake; decision makers; parties who execute write-back; parties responsible for oversight.

Normative status: Whether feedback is effective and whether its contents are normatively justified are judged separately.

Judgment ceiling: Route one judges how far the write-back process has actually gone. Route two judges the effect of the return channel with support from the corresponding channel-effect test. Route four judges carrier feedback-mediated learning from retention of the update, its use across rounds, and a task comparison. Route three judges historical-increment learning only with an additional historical-increment test. Conclusions about a single write-back, learning in general, transfer, and net benefits are drawn separately.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only descriptions of intake, changes in institutional elements, execution, duration, and gaps in write-back, along with needs for review or procedural repair. It does not authorize rewriting records or rules, carrying out repair, or closing appeals. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: An appeal gets a receipt, but records, rules, resources, and stopping conditions all remain unchanged; an audit report is published without a responsible party, deadline, or subsequent state update.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), people who submit feedback can request its status, time limit, responsible party, and write-back outcome through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original intake, write-back, or decision chain.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline revoke erroneous updates and actually restore the previous states of records, rules, resources, roles, or stopping conditions. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
