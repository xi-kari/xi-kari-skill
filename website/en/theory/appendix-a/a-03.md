# A.3 Orientation Anchor (HV03) {#section-a-3}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: A goal, identity, memory, commitment, fear, or common problem may first be recorded as candidate material about meaning. A conditionally effective orientation anchor is determined, within the scope of a “Meaning and Coordination” test (§4.2), only if that test provides support on the preselected outcome for resource allocation, action choice, or coordination.

Scope of application: Human structures involving intentionality, coordination, or common problems.

Conditions for suspension: Do not determine a conditionally effective orientation anchor under route two when there are only slogans, an interpreter’s projections, or coerced uniformity of expression. Sourced expressions, diverse expressions, and risks of coercion may still be recorded under route one. Do not infer a common will from them.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: Structural domain (§7.3.1).

Method requirements: Observation Position and Model Limits (§4.8.2); conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: None.

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, candidate anchor. Conclusion level: Candidate description. Conditions: A goal, identity, memory, commitment, fear, or common problem has traceable ways of being expressed and carried, but there is not yet an eligible “Meaning and Coordination” test. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record candidate material about meaning, diverse expressions, disputes over representativeness, and the need for a further “Meaning and Coordination” test. Ceiling: Candidate expressions of meaning only. They must not be called an effective orientation anchor or a common will.

Route two, effective anchor. Conclusion level: Conditional effect. Conditions: A pre-registered “Meaning and Coordination” test receives support on the single criterion selected in advance among resource allocation, action choice, and coordination outcomes. Further inferential premises: This “Meaning and Coordination” test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Determine a conditionally effective orientation anchor within this test, outcome family, scale, and time window. Ceiling: Do not extrapolate to unselected resource, action, or coordination outcomes. Do not infer genuine consent or a unified inner state, or treat the result as grounds for imposing uniformity of meaning.

Can infer: Conditional directions of coordination and anchors of conflict.

Cannot infer: That a group has a unified inner state; that shared language amounts to genuine consent; that the goal is normatively justified.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers individual expressions or actions, cases of particular subjects, the population of potential participants, distributions of stances, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers sites of relationships, organizational spaces, digital public spaces, and the reach of propagation across domains. Time span covers the window from expression to action, cycles of commitment, and lags in drift and decoupling. Organizational level covers roles in action, teams, organizations, institutions, and public governance ecologies. Causal level covers events of expression, mechanisms of interaction between meaning and action, meso-level coordination structures, institutional arrangements, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original expressions and actions, event sequences, individual cases, distributions of stances, coordination metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers direct participants, dissenters, indirectly affected people, second-order consequences for coordination, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers propagation of expressions, links for coordination, heterogeneous clusters, and bridging nodes. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for naming the anchor, adopting a judgment of representativeness, and requiring coordination or uniformity separately. Diverse anchors must be preserved.

Effective object: A goal, identity, memory, commitment, fear, or common problem that may change resource allocation, action choice, or coordination outcomes.

Kept across scales: The bridge from meaning to the preselected resource allocation, action choice, or coordination outcome.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Rules of representation; heterogeneity; distribution of costs and benefits; scope of authorization.

Changes with scale: The content and strength of an anchor and the subjects who carry it can change.

Objects to which it does not apply: Nonhuman systems without intentionality, meaning, or capability for commitment.

Cannot be raised to: Local expression taken directly as a common will.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: Dispersed; coalescing; competing; solidified; decoupled.

Observables: Differences in resource allocation before and after the pre-registered expression of meaning appears; changes in action choices, rates of completion in collaboration, or conflict patterns; acceptance, rejection, and alternative expressions of the anchor at different positions; whether the outcome difference under the comparison conditions exceeds the predetermined threshold.

Evidence requirements: Resource adjustments; sequences of action; commitment and exit records; evidence of conflict.

Inputs: Positions of participation; safety of expression; data on resources and actions.

Outputs: Generative events (§7.2.3); mobilization of uptake; agendas for normative choice.

Time window and lag: Distinguish short-term slogans, long-term commitments, and intergenerational memory.

Uncertainty: Record silence, coerced agreement, and internal heterogeneity.

Local exclusion zone: Different goals at positions where people have limited safety must not be erased in aggregation.

Affected positions: People who identify with the anchor; dissenters; people represented; cost bearers.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Narratives; commitments; shared memory; institutional goals; problem definitions.

Responsible parties: Those making claims of representativeness; those allocating resources on that basis.

Normative status: The existence of an anchor does not establish that it is normatively justified.

Judgment ceiling: Limit the two routes separately. Route one only describes candidate expressions of meaning. When the corresponding “Meaning and Coordination” test supports the effect on the selected outcome, route two may explain a conditional anchor within a limited scope. Do not extrapolate to unselected outcomes.

Action ceiling: This variable produces only descriptions of candidate anchors, diverse expressions, comparison results, and needs for more evidence. It does not authorize unifying meaning, representing others’ wishes, or coordinating action. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: A repeated slogan does not change any allocation of resources or action; uniform expressions under high pressure conceal genuine goals that conflict.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), members can contest representativeness, put forward different goals, or refuse to be anchored through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original chain of judgment about the anchor.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline actually revoke the naming of the anchor, remove its force as a representation and its downstream effect on coordination, and restore the state of diverse expression. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
