# A.10 Evolutionary Phase (HV10) {#section-a-10}

1. Proposition and Scope of Application

Proposition: The seven state prototypes (§8.3) and “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” (§8.5) apply only to human intentional collectives that are taking shape or operating. Orientation, generation, uptake, and institutionalization are aspects to be checked. They need not all be mature. Skipping stages, parallelism, mixing, regression, splits, mergers, dormancy, absorption, and functional transfer are possible.

Scope of application: Human intentional collectives that meet the conditions of applicability.

Conditions for suspension: Suspend the relevant prototype assertion when a stage is given a moral ranking or its title conflicts with the criteria. If an applicability condition is missing, do not draw conclusions about prototypes or paths that depend on it. Inapplicability, missing components, and observation needs may still be recorded under the complete conditions of route one.

2. Grounds and Limits on Conclusions

Inferential premises: Orientation anchor (§7.3.3); generative node (§7.3.4); action uptake layer (§7.3.5); feedback write-back (§7.3.7).

Method requirements: Conditions for evidence (§2.5); conditions for sources (§2.7).

Domain restriction: None.

Objects of application: None.

Support routes:

Route one, component applicability. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: Orientation anchor, generative node, action uptake layer, and feedback write-back all have verifiable assessment records. The recorded result may be “missing,” “not applicable,” or “not supported”; the four components need not be established empirically. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: None. Can conclude: Record applicability, inapplicability, missing components, mixed states, and needs for further observation. Ceiling: Component checks alone do not establish a match to the seven state prototypes or complete a judgment about the path of “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise.”

Route two, prototype match. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: Applicability, the object’s identity criterion, the observation window, and the necessary checks for entry, observations, counterexamples, and falsification for the selected prototype are completed under §8.2–8.3. A specific criterion that needs evidence across time windows must meet its own requirements. A second operating interval need not be established first, nor a phase pattern of the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism (§6.7). Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals (§4.8.4). Can conclude: Record a single, mixed, or parallel configuration of the seven state prototypes. Other descriptive paths are recorded separately under their own conditions in §8.4, and “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” is judged as an independent exit path. Ceiling: Description of prototype configuration only. This does not automatically qualify as a phase pattern of this mechanism, a causal phase transition, or a historical increment. Prototypes do not rank health, success, normative justification, or suitability for elimination.

Route three, causal phase transition. Conclusion level: Mechanism explanation. Conditions: A match to the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism is established, and a channel-effect test (§4.4) identifies the effect of the specified phase-transition channel on the preselected state change. Further inferential premises: The “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism; this channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims (§2.4); Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record a candidate causal phase transition within the specified object, time window, and channel. Ceiling: A necessary sequence of stages, a direction of value, or authorization to advance or eliminate anything must not be inferred from the transition mechanism.

Route four, historical-increment path dependence. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: A match to the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism is established, and a historical-increment test (§4.5) shows that historical phase variables provide a conditional increment for subsequent states, hysteresis, or regression. Further inferential premises: The “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism; this historical-increment test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record candidates for historical-increment path dependence, hysteresis, or historical phase differences, beyond the current state, within the pre-registered time window. Ceiling: This must not be called fate or absolute irreversibility, and it must not be used to prescribe options for repair, exit, or wind-down automatically.

Route five, orderly exit path. Conclusion level: Descriptive classification. Conditions: The candidate entry and the completion conditions for “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” are checked item by item under §8.5, and the original object’s identity criterion, functions, personnel, relationships, resources, memory, debts, appeals, and continuity status are recorded separately. No minimum prototype among the seven state prototypes needs to be obtained first, and no prior match to the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism is required. Further inferential premises: None. Further method requirements: Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Make an independent judgment of the life-cycle path, recording “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” as “candidate,” “in progress,” “partially complete,” “complete,” or “unknown.” Ceiling: The outcome must not be judged a “benign completion” while the affected groups, outstanding obligations, receiving capability, appeals, or safety of memory remain unknown. “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” is not an eighth stage and confers no authorization for dissolution, transfer, publication of archives, or relinquishment of responsibility.

Route six, carrier phase persistence. Conclusion level: Intertemporal explanation. Conditions: The conditions for a phase pattern of the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism are met; the current carrier, persistence across time windows or delayed recovery, and discriminating evidence required by the carrier route of this mechanism are all in place; and an eligible channel-effect test covers the specified outcome and time window. Further inferential premises: The “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism; this channel-effect test. Further method requirements: Conditions for causal claims; Retaining Competing Explanations and Residuals. Can conclude: Record a candidate mechanism of phase persistence or delayed recovery maintained by a current carrier under the same identity criterion and within the specified time window. Ceiling: Do not directly treat phase persistence, regression, or a curve shape as a mechanism. Do not determine a historical increment in the sense of a historical-increment test. It produces no required sequence of stages, direction of value, or authorization to act in reality.

Can infer: Conditional state coordinates; nonlinear paths; orderly exit and benign demise.

Cannot infer: That all systems must pass through the seven state prototypes; that a higher stage is more normatively justified; that dissolution amounts to failure.

3. Scale and Object

Nine-axis scale: Aggregation level covers individual state events, cases of local groups, populations of intentional collectives, phase distributions, and aggregation rules. Spatial extent covers sites of action, organizational or institutional boundaries, digital spaces for collaboration, and the reach of evolution across domains. Time span covers state windows, transition lags, regression, dormancy, hysteresis, and generational cycles. Organizational level covers member roles, teams, organizations, institutions, and governance ecologies. Causal level covers state events, mechanisms of interaction in transitions, meso-level phase structures, institutionalization, and systemic conditions. Observation resolution covers original state events, transition sequences, local cases, phase distributions, state metrics, and summaries. Record compression loss as well. Scope of influence covers direct members and people taking up work, people who exit, indirectly affected people, second-order consequences, and impacts across domains and generations. Network topology scope covers local phase clusters, connections across levels, splits and mergers, functional transfers, and succession paths. Scope of jurisdiction and authorization states the authorization for naming phases, adopting monitoring, trials, advancement, orderly exit, and elimination separately. Mixed phases need to be preserved.

Effective object: States of human intentional collectives that are taking shape or operating. Each component may be “present,” “forming,” “missing,” or “not applicable,” as judged under the specific prototype conditions.

Kept across scales: Conditions of applicability; phase criteria; nonlinear paths; “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” not counting as an eighth stage.

Must be added when moving to a larger scale: Distribution of local phases; transitions across levels; continuity of uptake through succession; protection and exit.

Changes with scale: Those taking up work, institutional carriers, and effective objects can change with the phase.

Objects to which it does not apply: Populations, purely technical systems, or categories based on similarity when there are no grounds for identifying a human intentional collective. Lack of mature uptake during formation is in itself no basis for categorical exclusion.

Cannot be raised to: The phase of one case directly representing the population; human stages transferred into the universal core.

4. State, Evidence, and Relations Between Variables

States: The prototypes are coalescence around an anchor; a generative core taking shape; initial expansion and differentiated uptake; development under interconnected constraints; a window for structural reconfiguration; efficient coordination and flexible expansion; and adaptive dynamic balance. The classification modes are single dominant, mixed, parallel, and unknown. The independent path is “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise,” which is recorded under route five and not listed as an eighth state.

Observables: Concurrent states of the orientation, generation, uptake, institutionalization, and feedback variables; records of repeated matches to phase criteria across observation windows; trajectories of skipped stages, parallelism, mixing, regression, splitting, merging, and dormancy; records of functional transfer, carryover of responsibilities, and orderly exit in “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise.”

Evidence requirements: Phase variables; transition records; traces; uptake and institutional status.

Inputs: Orientation anchor; generative node; action uptake layer; feedback write-back; structural load (§7.3.9). A phase pattern does not require a historical-increment test. A causal transition additionally needs to meet the conditions for causal claims. Route six checks persistence or delayed recovery transmitted by a current carrier under §4.5. Only route four additionally needs a historical-increment test to test the extra historical conditional increment. The human-domain clause “Historical Trace Carriers” (§7.1) supports only the corresponding candidate traces.

Outputs: Phase judgments; continuity of uptake through succession; needs for orderly exit and repair.

Time window and lag: State phase observation windows, transition lags, regression, and dormancy.

Uncertainty: Record mixed phases, splits, mergers, and scale differences.

Local exclusion zone: Local groups and roles that an overall phase cannot represent.

Affected positions: Members; people taking up the work; dissenters; people who exit; successors.

5. Uptake, Responsibility, Ceilings, and Correction

Uptake carriers: Collective action; organizational structure; institutional records; shared memory.

Responsible parties: Analysts who make the phase judgment; decision makers and parties granting authorization for action based on it.

Normative status: Phases do not rank health, success, or normative justification.

Judgment ceiling: Each route is limited separately by its own complete conditions. Route one classifies only component applicability and missing components. Route two matches the seven state prototypes under §8.2–8.3 without strictly requiring the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism. Route three gives a limited explanation of a candidate causal phase transition under the conditions of the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism and a channel-effect test. Route four explains historical conditional increments under the conditions of the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism and a historical-increment test. Route five independently judges the path status of “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” under §8.5, without presupposing a match to the seven state prototypes or to the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism. Route six judges mechanisms maintained by a current carrier across time windows on the basis of the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism, a channel-effect test, and evidence under §4.5. Routes cannot supply one another’s missing conditions, and none authorizes action in reality.

Action ceiling: Depending on the selected route, this variable produces component applicability, prototype matches, mixed states, limited explanations of phase mechanisms or historical conditional increments, the independent path status of “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise,” uncertainty, and observation needs. It does not authorize trials, advancement, merger, orderly exit, or elimination. Any real-world adjustment must separately pass normative bridging (§4.14.1) and have in place the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, itemized authorization, and operational procedures.

Counterexamples: The same collective simultaneously exhibits a mixed phase, with uptake taking shape as in the “Initial Expansion and Differentiated Uptake” prototype and vulnerabilities accumulating as in the “Efficient Coordination and Flexible Expansion” prototype; “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” preserves functional transfer and does not amount to failure of a stage.

Appeal: Under the common rule for appeal and restoration (§7.4), members can raise objections to phase evidence, linear assumptions, and uses that impose a moral ranking through a safe, accessible channel with anti-retaliation protection, and initiate a review independent of the original phase judgment or decision chain.

Restoration: Under the same rule, once restoration is authorized, the designated responsible party must within the stated deadline revoke the phase designation and its effects on downstream decisions, and actually restore variable-level description and the unresolved status. Changes leave a record, and their completion is verified.
