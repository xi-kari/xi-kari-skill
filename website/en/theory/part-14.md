# Part 14 Normative Choice {#part-title-14}

The preceding structural grammar, empirical question families, scale transformations, mechanisms of operation, and human variables address “how objects exist, operate, and change, and what grounds we have for explaining them in these ways.” This Part asks a different question. Among several possible directions, who may choose, on what grounds, whom does the choice bind, and how do we stop and repair if it fails?

The two kinds of question must be kept separate. An explanation can be highly reliable and still be insufficient to confer authority to act. A goal can receive wide support and still breach rights floors, shift costs to low-power positions, or exceed the decision maker’s jurisdiction. Normative choice has its own contract[^tn-contract-15] between explanation and real-world action. It cannot be treated as an appendix to diagnosis.

The requirements in this Part belong to three levels with different scopes. Principles of argument state what a normative judgment must distinguish: facts and what ought to be, the value premises used and their conflicts, and beneficiaries and those who bear the costs; where options are chosen, they also include the options, no action among them. Analytical recommendations for deliberation are bound by them as well. Substantive safeguards state what real-world adoption must actually have: valid authorization covering the action, and the substance of the protections referred to by PF-1–PF-10, including identification of affected and low-power positions, safe submission and anti-retaliation, genuine refusal and exit, accessible appeal and independent review, adding evidence and correcting records, rollback and remedy, and action ceilings and expiry. The record format states how this framework registers the above for its own `authorized` status, for example by pointing to the no-action option through a unique `no_action_option_id` and having exactly ten `protection_floor_bindings`. Other implementations that carry equivalent responsibilities for protection, tracing, and review may adopt different formats. Whether they are equivalent must be confirmed by independent review, not self-reported by the implementer. If deleting a requirement would change who is protected, who bears the costs, who has the right to decide, who can stop, or who can correct errors, it does not belong to the record format.

## 14.1 Facts Do Not Entail What Ought to Be Done {#section-14-1}

Factual propositions describe “what is.” Normative premises state “what is worth pursuing, protecting, or prohibiting.” Claims that “a structure is stable,” “an option is more efficient,” “the system retains a certain type,” or “a risk is rising” cannot directly entail “therefore this should be done.” Facts have a defined role in choice. They determine whether an option is possible, what its consequences might be, and the extent of risk and uncertainty. They do not choose values or create authorization on their own.

Any process that moves from explanation or diagnosis to real-world action must therefore go through four conversions. O1 freezes the facts, evidence, scale, and uncertainty to be used. O2 makes explicit the N1–N5 premises adopted at runtime, conflicts of value, beneficiaries, and those who bear the costs. O3 compares options including no action and reviews the J axis, authorization, jurisdiction, validity period, and conflicts of interest. A separate normative choice record then freezes the decision. Only then can O4 turn the approved option into bounded execution that is reversible, monitorable, stoppable, subject to appeal and rollback, and ends on expiry.

The severity of a diagnosis provides no direct route to action. High model confidence provides no route to broader permission. Stronger evidence can raise the ceiling for descriptive judgments, but cannot raise the action ceiling by itself.

C12 checks the validity of the normative bridge. C12 does not choose values. It checks only whether the descriptive conclusion, current evidence, explicit normative premises, conflicts of value, the protection floor, the J axis, options, no action, O1–O4, the action ceiling, and conditions for correction all meet their requirements at the same time. Least harm and proportionality must be explicitly selected, explained, and reviewed in the specific choice record. They must not be left implicit in C12.

## 14.2 Three Distinct Processes of Selection and Choice {#section-14-2}

Selection and choice cover at least three structurally distinct processes. Conflating them personifies natural or distributed processes, expands personal preferences into public power, or reduces public procedures to the will of those with power.

`SEL-SYS` has no unified deciding subject. It refers to variation or differences producing different outcomes through environments, feedback, competition, replication, and retention mechanisms. Examples include natural selection, differential retention in markets, and content spreading as a result of algorithmic rewards. The unit of selection, environment, retention mechanism, time window, and stochastic null model must be registered. One may say that a form is retained, replicated, or eliminated. One must not say that the system “considers it better.” Retention by itself does not establish normative justification, nor do survival, stability, popularity, or efficiency. The action ceiling for `SEL-SYS` is a description of the selection process. It cannot enter `authorized` or include an `external_action` option.

`SEL-AGT` is decision-making by a subject under constraints. The deciding subject must be identifiable and can decide only within their own permissions, genuine options, territory, and validity period. An apparent “choice” must not be recorded as free consent if it arises from coercion, lock-in through defaults, deprivation of information, insufficient capability, unsafe refusal, or blocked exit. Failure to exit does not amount to consent. Entering a process does not amount to continuing consent. One subject’s choice does not automatically bind others.

`SEL-GOV` is collective governance authorized through a procedure. It addresses common affairs. The fact that they are common does not establish that the majority can represent everyone or that the scope of impact equals the scope of jurisdiction. A governance decision must make it possible to trace its boundaries, how the agenda was formed, who has authority to represent, which positions can participate, how dissent is preserved, how minorities are protected, how conflicts of interest are handled, who executes and reviews it, and when authorization expires. Numbers, loudness, efficiency, claims to represent, and legal form cannot by themselves substitute for bounded public authorization.

The three types have strict boundaries. `SEL-SYS` cannot acquire intent or normative justification by borrowing the language of subjects. `SEL-AGT` cannot decide for others beyond the subject’s own permissions. `SEL-GOV` cannot bypass procedure and present governance as an enlarged version of personal choice.

## 14.3 Registering N1–N5 Premises at Runtime {#section-14-3}

N1–N5 do not operate automatically as background assumptions. Each choice record must list in `public_value_premises` the IDs actually adopted for this choice and say whether each is an objective, a constraint, or a veto gate. A normative premise that was not registered must not appear silently in the conclusion.

N1, “Explanation Does Not Confer Authority to Act,” vetoes any direct move from facts to action. On its own, N1 can yield only no action, a need for more evidence, or continued deliberation. It cannot generate an affirmative proposal.

N2, “Protection Must Not Be Downgraded,” requires scale elevation and action to maintain or strengthen existing protections at low-power positions. If the infrastructure for protection is missing, narrow the action and preserve the protection.

N3, “High-Impact Judgments Must Be Correctable,” requires genuine access to appeal, independent review, stopping, withdrawal, rollback, remedy, and version write-back.

N4, “Love and Undertaking Must Not Be Conscripted,” prohibits invoking love, responsibility, care, or the persistence of a system to demand unilateral, unlimited sacrifice without an option to exit.

Bounded responsibility under N4 precludes demands made in the name of love, care, or system persistence for unilateral, unlimited sacrifice without an option to exit. It does not cancel bounded commitments, obligations of support, contractual duties, or remedial responsibilities that have independent grounds. Ending or transferring a responsibility should specify who will actually take over for dependents, for how long, and with what remaining risks. This requirement must not be used to force those harmed to remain exposed to harm. Capability, causal contribution, benefit, fault, and duties of remedy should be justified separately. 〔Studies 06.06, 09.02, 10.07, 21.06, 23.01–23.07〕

N5, “Persistence and Systemic Selection Do Not Establish Normative Justification,” limits stability, efficiency, coherence, and outcomes of systemic selection to evidence about consequences. They must not substitute for a judgment of normative justification.

Premises drawn from N can conflict. For example, quickly limiting losses can conflict with full participation. Expanding public benefits can entail risks to the rights of those in minority positions. A short reversible trial can conflict with a long-term goal of stability. Conflicts cannot be smoothed out with a total score. Each conflict must have a stable ID and list the conflicting premises, affected positions, objections, decision rule, and current status. An unresolved substantive conflict can lead to suspension, a narrower trial, or retention of no action. Deleting objections cannot “resolve” it. When every feasible option (including suspension and no action) leaves unmet legitimate claims or irreversible losses, see §14.9.1.

`rights_floor` records the PF IDs actually in force for the current choice. It sets a boundary each option must pass first. It must not be treated as a preference freely exchangeable for efficiency. The rights floor and N premises have distinct roles. N states the grounds for choice, and PF states the conditions of protection that those grounds cannot override.

## 14.4 Generating Options, Consequences Across Scales, and Distribution {#section-14-4}

Choice begins by generating a set of comparable options. It does not consist of finding reasons for a preselected option. Each option has a stable `option_id` and freezes its `option_kind`, action type, target object, territory, validity period, reversibility, expected impacts, and distributional impacts. A change to an option’s action, object, territory, or validity period should produce a new version or ID. The same ID must not be quietly repurposed after review.

Each set of options must have exactly one `no_action`, uniquely referenced by `no_action_option_id`. The no-action option is the baseline path in which the proposed new action under consideration is not carried out. Events can still occur. Existing maintenance, statutory duties, actions already under way, and the conditions for their continuation must be recorded separately. Choosing `no_action` does not remove existing obligations. The resources and arrangements needed to actively maintain the status quo are compared as a separate option. Use the same time window to account for natural changes, accumulating risks, opportunity costs, and distributional consequences on the baseline path. Whether intervention is necessary can be judged only if no action is included. Removing it often amounts to assuming in advance that “something must be done.”

Another option type for `SEL-SYS` is `system_variant`, which compares different outcomes under different environments or retention mechanisms. It does not let the system decide. `SEL-AGT` and `SEL-GOV` may include `external_action`, but a single external action option must be frozen on entering `authorized`. The external `selected_action` record must then match the same option, subject, object, action, territory, and validity period field by field.

Consequences must be recorded across scales. For every option, use at least the nine axes A, X, T, O, C, R, I, N, and J to specify the object level, spatial extent, time span, organizational level, causal level, resolution, scope of influence, network topology, and scope of authorization where consequences occur. An immediate local benefit cannot represent long-term system benefits. An average improvement cannot cover a local exclusion zone. Stability at a higher level cannot make up for actual losses suffered by subjects at a lower level.

The distribution ledger must record benefits, costs, risks, impacts on rights, and shifts in burdens at each position. It must explicitly mark `power_position` as low, medium, high, or unknown. `affected_positions` lists the positions that §14.4.1 requires to be listed in full, as well as positions affected indirectly, at second order, across domains, and across generations that are identified under that section. `low_power_position_ids` identifies in particular the positions where it is harder to speak, exit, provide evidence, or shift costs. Every option must state who benefits, who bears the costs, who has decision-making authority, who is unable to refuse, whether costs shift from positions with more power to those with less, and whether unobservable positions are being mistaken for absent ones.

Least harm and proportionality are two explicit principles of choice. Each record must specify the principle ID and version, selected option, all options compared, evaluation dimensions, adequate reasons, evidence references, reviewer, time of review, and status. `least_harm` must instantiate `NSP-LEAST-HARM`. It must explain which harms can be avoided across all feasible options and no action, and why the selected option is the narrowest. `proportionality` must instantiate `NSP-PROPORTIONALITY`. It checks suitability, necessity, benefits and the costs imposed on rights, intensity, scope, and duration separately. Neither principle is a synonym for a “good objective.” Neither can be reduced to a slogan that cannot be reviewed or a token carrying only a name.

Evidence for an instance of a principle must resolve to the evidence set for the current run. The reviewer must be distinct from the deciding subject and be linked to an independent party responsible for review. Review must take place no later than the transition to authorization. The version may refer only to the current registered version. Expiry, the validity of all ten PF items, and the timing of further review must all cover the validity period of the selected option. An expired review record must not be used to maintain `authorized`.

### 14.4.1 The Scope and Limits of the Responsibility to Identify {#section-14-4-1}

Impacts that are indirect, second-order, cross-domain, or intergenerational have no natural endpoint, so identifying them is a responsibility proportionate to risk. Directly affected positions, low-power positions that are known or have been pointed out by others, and positions that application modules require to be listed separately must all be listed in full; the proportionate standard below does not apply to them. For other positions, identification traces outward along the declared channels and the nine axes. The more severe, irreversible, widespread, or likely to accumulate the possible harm is, and the lower the power position of those possibly affected, the farther the tracing should go. Tracing may stop when continuing it can no longer reasonably change the judgment, the comparison of options, or the protective arrangements. Undeclared channels pointed out by others must be added to the tracing, or reasons for not adopting them must be stated. This limit is recorded together with the channels already searched, the positions consulted, and what remains unknown, so that others can challenge it.

Unknowns are handled in two ways. An unknown for which there are reasonable grounds to think it may reveal a failure of the protection floor, or that is sufficient to change the comparison of options (including least harm, proportionality, and distribution), blocks the part of adoption it affects until evidence is added, the option is narrowed, or protection with separate grounds is in place. Other unknowns are registered as they are, with conditions set for observation and renewed identification. Positions identified only after the fact enjoy the same appeal, correction, and remedy and need not first prove that they could have been foreseen.

The limit of identification, the division between direct and indirect, and the classification of unknowns cannot be decided solely by a party that benefits from a narrower limit. Their grounds are published in advance and open to appeal, and they are referred to independent review in high-impact cases or when there is a conflict of interest. The same responsibility applies to no action and to maintaining the status quo. Those harmed by the status quo are not exempt from identification because the harm has long existed. The proportionate standard applies only to identifying indirect and more distant positions. It does not reduce the protection that identified positions receive under PF-1–PF-10, nor does it postpone protection against known harm on the grounds that identification cannot be exhaustive.

## 14.5 Coercion, Defaults, Agendas, Representation, and Minority Protection {#section-14-5}

Before a subject says “yes,” check the conditions under which they can say “no.” Both `SEL-AGT` and `SEL-GOV` must create a `coercion_and_choice` record. It must examine whether direct or indirect coercion exists, whether the default locks in the result through inertia, complexity, or penalties, whether information is understandable and symmetric, whether the subject has decision-making capability, whether refusal is safe, and whether exit is genuinely feasible. Each check must give a status, rationale, and evidence references. `unknown` does not count as passing. `not_applicable` requires a reason why it genuinely does not apply.

Default design is not a neutral background condition. Who sets the default, how much switching costs, and how silence is interpreted can all change the distribution of choices. Coercion also takes forms beyond commands. Tying access to essential resources to compliance, retaliation through relationships, identity exposure, withholding information, unaffordable exit costs, or moralizing care responsibilities can turn formal options into a single path.

The evidential weight of consent varies with conditions. Not exiting does not by itself amount to continuing consent. If an initial choice also changed the conditions of later choices, for example through interrupted skills, housing or services tied to relationships, debt, or dependence on care, continuing to stay provides weaker evidence. Check separately the conditions under which consent was originally given and the conditions under which refusal, negotiation, and exit are still possible now. This does not cancel bounded commitments and obligations of support that have independent grounds; their ending and transfer are handled under N4 in §14.3. Nor does it require every future choice to remain open forever. The refusal, stopping, and exit referred to by PF-3 and PF-9, however, are not closed by past consent.

`SEL-GOV` must also create a `governance_procedure` record. Agenda formation checks who can raise questions, who can exclude options, and when emergency procedures end. Representation checks the source and scope of authorization, whether it can be withdrawn, and who is unrepresented. Participation checks safety, accessibility, information, and actual capability to influence decisions. Minority protection checks rights that majority decisions cannot cancel, local exclusion zones, and conditions for suspension. The conflict-of-interest check asks whether the people who decide, execute, evaluate, and benefit are the same. Accountability for execution checks whether responsible parties can be reviewed, stopped, and replaced.

Responding to specific views and allowing rules to change are different capabilities. Whether it is feasible to veto a proposal, propose an alternative, amend agenda rules, or replace those who hear a matter or make the final decision must be registered separately. When responses are possible only within a set topic while the agenda and the final decision cannot be challenged, record truthfully that the rule layer cannot be changed. Do not record this as an absence of feedback, nor as adequate capacity for correction already being in place. This registration does not substitute for the conditions for correction. In high-impact cases or when there is a conflict of interest, if the final decision cannot be challenged and there is no external independent review, N3 and PF-7 are not deemed satisfied. What evidence or dispute over rights is sufficient to trigger review at the rule layer should be published in advance.

Formal participation does not amount to genuine participation. A public meeting, a complaint channel, or a majority vote cannot by itself establish that people in low-power positions can safely express views and influence the decision. Preserve each objection’s stable ID, claim, position, evidence, reasons for how it was handled, deadline for response, and safe submission channel. Before entering `authorized` within the framework, address substantive objections capable of changing key facts, minimum protections, option feasibility, or valid permissions. Suspend the affected part if the reasons raised have not received a response suited to them. Determine what is decisive using criteria published in advance and open to appeal. Refer conflicts of interest to independent review. Objections must not be excluded because few people raise them, because a majority deems them “unimportant,” or because they are inconvenient to handle. When differences in values persist after a reasoned response, preserve the minority view under an explicit decision rule that meets the requirements. Passing does not require fabricating unanimity.

Within the framework, `authorized` marks the scope of use the framework endorses. It does not substitute for legal validity under real-world institutions, official powers, or consent of those concerned. The procedures above cannot override matters that applicable law or the protection floor makes nonwaivable. A procedure record cannot supply a legitimate decision rule where none exists. Emergencies require separately specified temporary authority, the narrowest scope, expiry, and subsequent review. “Objections have been answered” does not confer emergency power. Record separately the existence of opposition, reasons for opposition that remain unaddressed, and failure of protection conditions. 〔Studies 14.06, 19.02, 21.04, 25.02〕

## 14.6 The J Axis, O1–O4, and Bounded Authorization {#section-14-6}

The J axis specifies who may decide, under what source of authority, for which object and action, and within which territory and validity period. Authorization must be atomic. One and the same valid J tuple must cover the deciding subject, target object, single action, territory, and entire validity period. One tuple covering the subject and another covering the action cannot be combined to create authorization. An increase in the scope of observation, scope of influence, or technical capability cannot be taken to expand jurisdiction.

`jurisdiction_axis` fixes the source, scope, and validity period. Each option’s `territory` must equal that scope, and its `validity_interval` must fall within that period. To proceed to external action, the choice record, selected option, `selected_action`, and J tuple must match on each corresponding field. The existence of a source of authorization does not establish legal validity. Legal validity does not establish normative justification. O3 must register them separately.

O1–O4 are required. O1 prevents facts, thresholds, and evidence levels from being changed after seeing the options. O2 keeps value premises, those who bear the costs, and conflicts visible. O3 prevents omission of no action, conflicts of interest, and the limits of authorization. O4 prevents a bounded decision from expanding during execution into an indefinite, irreversible action for which no one is responsible. If any step is missing, the record can go no further than `under_review` or `paused`.

To enter `authorized`, the N premises must be resolvable. N1 alone is insufficient. Conflicts of value and objections must be recorded. Every PF item must pass. Affected and low-power positions must be listed and identified under §14.4.1, with the limit of identification recorded. The selected option must be a feasible `external_action`. Least harm and proportionality must be explicitly selected. A valid atomic J tuple must cover the action, and O1–O4 must cross-reference one another. Stopping, appeal, independent review, rollback, remedy, and expiration must all be capable of changing the status.

Atomic authorization specifies sufficient coverage for a particular action. Actual institutions can still consist of multiple authorized actions. Intake, adjudication, preservation measures, service of process, and enforcement may be carried out in succession by different subjects under the law. Check each action’s own authorization and prerequisites separately, then explain how the preceding result serves as valid input to the next step. Several incomplete tuples cannot be combined to create authority for the same action. The fact that no one person holds all permissions does not invalidate an entire lawful procedure with multiple steps.

Personal consent, private appointment, public authority, and an organization’s legally established standing to make a request can also be valid separately. When a public action is lawful without a private appointment, that does not establish personal consent or permit expansion into other matters. The framework’s internal issuance status determines only the scope of use it is willing to certify. The legal effect of external procedures, normative disputes, and actual effects of protection must still be assessed separately on their respective evidence.

## 14.7 Status, Action Ceilings, and the Correction Loop {#section-14-7}

A choice record has only seven statuses: `draft`, `under_review`, `authorized`, `paused`, `stopped`, `rolled_back`, and `closed`. Every transition must append the previous status, time, responsible party, and grounds. It must not overwrite history.

`draft` permits organizing materials and comparing options within existing valid permissions. It grants no new authority for real-world intervention. `under_review` means that O1–O3, normative conflicts, protection, or authorization are still under review. A need for preservation measures may be raised, but actual protection, freezing of resources, collection of further evidence, or reversible trials still require applicable, independently valid authorization. Actions under existing statutory duties or emergency powers are recorded within their own scope. Review status confers no additional permissions. Only `SEL-AGT` and `SEL-GOV` can enter `authorized`, and the action ceiling covers only the specific option selected and authorized.

Enter `paused` when a key fact, protection, authorization, procedure, or monitoring condition ceases to hold. Stop the part of implementation affected by that failure. Review or suspension status by itself does not authorize freezing resources, restricting access, collecting information, or continuing intervention. Action needed to avoid immediate harm requires a separately valid duty or authorization, a specified scope, and conditions for stopping and review. Enter `stopped` when a stop trigger is reached, authorization is withdrawn, the validity period expires, or a prohibited action is taken. Terminate the affected actions under the applicable stopping procedures, and verify safe wind-down and any remedial duties that remain independently valid. Do not wait for new theoretical grounds. Enter `rolled_back` after completing the prescribed restoration and initiating verifiable remedy and version write-back. Enter `closed` only when outstanding harm, appeals, remedies, and responsibilities for records have been settled or formally handed off. Any later new action requires a new record.

For each action, pre-register its maximum level, prohibited actions, stop triggers, and the responsible party with authority to stop it. The final action ceiling is the narrowest intersection of the descriptive ceiling, normative principles, protection floor, selected option, J tuple, O4 trial scope, reversibility, and expiry. Tightening any item tightens the overall ceiling. A failure or unknown in any item cannot be offset by “high scores” elsewhere.

Appeal must be accessible, have a time limit, and be capable of changing a record or an action. It must lead to external independent review in high-impact cases or when there is a conflict of interest. Anti-retaliation mechanisms protect submission of evidence, objections, refusal, stopping, appeal, and exit. Rollback goes beyond withdrawing an order. It includes restoration to an acceptable state, correction of authoritative records, synchronization of downstream versions, remedy of real harm, and tracking of residual harm. Every authorization has an `expiry`. It lapses automatically at expiry. Continued execution requires a new authorization process and must not be renewed by default.

Correction has four distinct endpoints. Views are heard safely. Relevant evidence is understood. An authority with jurisdiction actually changes the erroneous decision. Restored resources and rights reach the people concerned. An earlier step can have independent value without closing a later one. A higher rate of overturned decisions is not automatically better. Review whether substantiated errors can be corrected, who could not enter the process, and what actually reaches people after correction. A rollback record cannot erase harm that occurred, opportunities that were missed, or costs incurred by others. 〔Studies 21.04, 21.08, 28.04〕

## 14.8 How PF-1–PF-10 Connect to the Choice Record {#section-14-8}

PF-1–PF-10 remain a complete protection floor. Scale elevation, object aggregation, option comparison, and convenience of execution do not reduce it. A choice record must have exactly ten `protection_floor_bindings`, with each PF ID appearing once and all IDs covered. Each entry records applicability status, rationale, evidence, mechanism, responsible party, stop trigger, appeal, review, rollback, remedy, and expiry. `rights_floor` must equal the set of PF items that are currently `active`. A general statement that “protection has been considered” is insufficient.

Each `mechanism_ref` must resolve to a mechanism in the same record for safe submission, anti-retaliation, appeal, independent review, rollback, remedy, residual harm tracking, or version write-back. The responsible party must match the owner of the mechanism. Bare strings, references that resolve to nothing, and self-reported independence do not count as protection. Human-specific PF items may be marked inapplicable only if the enclosing object and the downstream use together establish exclusively nonhuman impacts. If the enclosing context involves humans, sentient subjects, mixed objects, or effects on rights or resources, rewriting `impact_domain` as `nonhuman_only` does not lower the protection floor.

PF-1 enters the affected positions and low-power positions listed and identified under §14.4.1 in the options and the distribution ledger.

PF-2 provides anonymous submission, submission through a representative, or low-exposure submission, so a high-risk channel requiring a real name is not the only route.

PF-3 makes refusal and stopping genuinely effective within the process. Past consent does not amount to permanent consent.

PF-4 provides effective appeal with a time limit and the ability to change the outcome.

PF-5 protects submission of evidence, objections, refusal, stopping, appeal, and exit against retaliation.

PF-6 allows evidence to be added, disputes to be marked, source records to be corrected, and downstream versions to be synchronized.

PF-7 provides external independent review when impacts are high, conflicts of interest exist, or internal correction fails.

PF-8 requires actual capability for withdrawal, rollback, repair, and write-back to the authoritative version.

PF-9 provides genuine exit and transfer. Where exit is impossible, it provides representation and alternative arrangements to take over.

PF-10 freezes the action ceiling, prohibited actions, stop triggers, responsible party, and review time.

When the objects affected are humans, sentient subjects, mixed objects, or unknown objects, every item in PF-1–PF-10 must be `active`. Any `pending`, `failed`, or `not_applicable` item blocks entry into `authorized`. Human-specific mechanisms may be marked `not_applicable` with reasons only when the evidence limits the object and downstream use to purely nonhuman processes. PF-10 and the requirements for J, responsibility, stopping, review, rollback, and expiry for real-world action always apply. A change of use requires a new review. The previous inapplicability status does not carry over.

The protection floor cannot be lowered. Moving from a local to a wider scale, from the short to the long term, or from a single relation to an institution or platform can only preserve or strengthen protection. If a wider scale hides low-power positions, restore the local record, narrow the action, and strengthen protection. Aggregate benefits must not cancel local rights. Preserving or strengthening applies to these protections themselves, including the protective arrangements that low-power positions actually enjoyed before the action. It does not guarantee that no position’s outcomes in resources, opportunities, or life will worsen. When it is disputed whether an existing arrangement is a protection or an outcome, treat it first as a protection. Whether losses in outcomes are acceptable is argued separately through the N premises and the least harm and proportionality records under §14.4. Burden shifts falling on low-power positions or positions unable to exit must be listed position by position and must not be offset by aggregate or average benefits (§14.9). When every feasible option leaves unmet legitimate claims or irreversible losses, record this under §14.9.1.

How the world “ought to choose” cannot be reduced to a single objective function. The framework provides a structure for tracing a choice. It first distinguishes systemic selection, decision-making by a subject, and collective governance. It then records facts, values, rights, options, consequences, distribution, authorization, and procedure separately. The narrowest action ceiling, execution that can be stopped, and actual repair keep any decision bounded, open to challenge and correction, and subject to expiry.

The protection floor provides minimum procedural requirements and limits on burdens. It does not exhaust substantive rights. For each matter, still specify the relevant rights, claimants, duty bearers, applicable grounds, conflicts, and remedies. The ten mechanisms do not automatically establish that a distribution of income, eligibility, care, or property is justified. Conversely, a right with independent grounds does not come into existence only when the framework’s forms are used.

## 14.9 Distribution Across Circles and Responsibility for Inaction {#section-14-9}

Multi-circle options must record local benefits, cross-boundary spillovers, and costs in different time windows separately. Better metrics in the primary circle cannot offset harm to rights in another circle. An overall average cannot cover people unable to exit. Positions where memberships overlap may bear costs more than once. Bridging parties may bear translation costs and risk of conflict. Responsibilities and histories can remain after a temporary circle dissolves.

No action is also an option. State which rules and allocations of resources it retains, who continues to benefit or suffer harm, which clocks keep advancing, when to reopen evaluation, and who is responsible for observation and protection. If there is ongoing harm, a statutory duty, irreversible lock-in, or a position from which exit is impossible, “waiting for more evidence” cannot be extended indefinitely. Even without the main action, minimal protection, appeal, disclosure, or temporary uptake may be needed.

The prediction layer cannot compare options automatically. Foresight provides conditional consequences, the normative layer discloses conflicts of value and the protection floor, and the authorization layer determines who may act. Predictive accuracy, complexity, and technical capability do not confer a qualification to rule.

### 14.9.1 Bounded Judgment When Every Option Involves Losses {#section-14-9-1}

Sometimes, within the available resources and time, every feasible option, including no action, leaves unmet legitimate claims or irreversible losses. Suspension and waiting then have costs as well (§12.14 and §14.9), but the fact that waiting has costs does not generate authorization to act (§13.6). In such cases, do not invent an option with zero cost, and do not stop at a list of questions on the grounds that the conflict remains unresolved. Within what the evidence and explicit normative premises allow, the analysis should still give a reasoned, bounded judgment for deliberation. It is an analytical recommendation and does not decide on behalf of qualified subjects (§12.10).

The judgment states the recommended option and the N premises it relies on; what the decision rule in the conflict record is, who proposed it, and who must adopt it (§14.3); who bears the unmet claims or losses, and why those claims still hold; the responsibilities that continue afterward and the responsible parties for them, including mitigation, remedy, explanation, and review; and what changes in evidence, resources, or authority would change the judgment, and when to reassess. A decision rule is a normative position that those who propose and adopt it must publicly own, for example not treating one person’s life or basic safety merely as a replaceable input into others’ aggregate benefit. Making it public does not make it qualified; whether it qualifies is judged under §14.5. When the rule itself is disputed, the analysis presents the judgments under different rules side by side and does not select one on behalf of qualified subjects.

Three limits apply together. First, before claiming there is no alternative, show that the constraints are real: who controls the scarcity, and whether it comes from a prior decision that can itself be questioned (§2.14); whether the option set was generated under §12.9 and §14.4, and whether any option was deleted because of costs to the decision maker. When authority is insufficient, refer the matter to a subject with authority rather than treating this as grounds for having no alternative (§12.11). This showing is open to appeal and is referred to review independent of the decision maker. Second, matters that applicable law or the protection floor makes nonwaivable do not lapse because every option involves losses. A claim that even these matters cannot all be preserved at once must state which of them conflict and which options have been ruled out and on what grounds, and must be confirmed by review independent of the decision maker. If the claim is not confirmed, return to options that do not cross these matters. If it is confirmed, the framework still does not endorse any option with `authorized`, and the protections that can still be preserved continue to apply. A subject with the corresponding duties and authority may decide only under its own statutory or emergency authority, within the narrowest scope and bearing responsibility for the decision, with expiry and subsequent review (§14.5 and §14.7). Third, after a subject with authority has chosen, the claims of those harmed do not disappear. The record retains these claims and how they are subsequently handled, and does not delete the costs from the ledger.

## 14.10 Resources, Standing, Representation, and Non-Domination {#section-14-10}

A normative evaluation can ask about actual control of resources, equal standing within institutions, and rights over agendas and representation at the same time. An account of how these reinforce each other must explain whether resources make it possible to refuse dependence, whether standing changes rules of admission and evidence, and whether representatives can change budgets and execution. These can also be out of sync. Greater benefits can come with humiliating barriers to access. An identity can be affirmed while resources remain inaccessible. Representation by people with the same identity can increase while dissent within the group remains excluded. An improvement in one cannot settle the other two.

The absence of actual intervention does not guarantee the absence of domination. If one party retains the power to change another party’s key options arbitrarily, the other may have to appease them in advance, remain silent, or limit their own actions. When non-domination is adopted as the principle for an evaluation, state which powers can be constrained and how, whether decisions can be challenged, and how those imposing constraints are themselves held accountable. This normative choice cannot be inferred automatically from a low rate of conflict or present satisfaction. It does not require treating every intervention under the law as domination.

The scope of those affected, the scope of those subject to a governance structure, legal membership, and the scope of formal representation need not coincide. The first two help identify overlooked problems and needs for protection. The latter two must be determined under the specific institution and authorization. None can confer all voting rights or jurisdiction merely by applying one principle. The framework’s own review checks belong to its contract for bounded action. They do not establish the only legitimate system of decision-making for every community. How ordinary disagreements are resolved, which rights cannot be canceled by a majority, and when emergency procedures end should be stated publicly and be open to review.
