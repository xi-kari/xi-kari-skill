# Part 7 The Human Structured World {#part-title-7}

Rather than pushing human experience directly into the universal core, this Part adds over the universal structural grammar (Part 3) a further layer of concepts designed specifically for the human world, referred to below as the human adaptation layer. The eleven basic structural concepts describe broadly conceived empirical structures. The human adaptation layer alone handles intent, meaning, power, institutions, authorization, responsibility, and value. The eleven human variables and the eleven basic structural concepts are managed separately. Similar names or functions, or an equal number of terms, do not establish one-to-one equivalence between the two. Judgments made specifically for the human world must not be applied back to nature as general rules.

The eleven human variables all come under the human-domain proposition role (§2.2), but they are variables, not the six human-domain clauses. The eight categories of generative condition, generative subject, generative event, uptake carrier, responsible party, source of destabilization, mechanism of destabilization, and behavior of destabilization (§7.2) are category definitions. The variables and the category definitions carry no empirical truth on their own. Formal support under this framework for empirical questions and human empirical hypotheses must come from one of two kinds of test. One is a formal test of one of the four empirical questions, which needs to meet the requirements for the pre-registration of formal tests (§4.1). The other is a specific test of one of the three human empirical hypotheses (“Meaning and Coordination”; “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity”; “Historical Trace Carriers”), which needs to meet the corresponding requirements for such tests (§4.2). Existing domain research still supports limited conclusions according to its own objects, designs, and evidence. That support must not be revoked wholesale merely because the research has not obtained eligibility for a formal test under this framework. The other three clauses (“Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility”; “Institutional Write-Back”; “Open-Ended Undertaking”), the variables, and the category definitions must not be presented as empirical support on the strength of fully completed items or a successful classification.

Applying this Part requires first defining the object clearly by the seven elements of a structural object (§3.1) and stating its scale profile. Any judgment about the human world must specify the effective object, comparison conditions, time window, and observation position. It must also specify evidence coverage, affected positions, and the scope of authorization. Technologies, crises, resources, institutional conditions, and statistical trends do not acquire intent by having impacts. Visibility, disagreement, or proximity to a disturbance does not automatically make a person, group, or dissenter a mechanism, source, or responsible party.

## 7.1 Role Boundaries of the Six Human-Domain Clauses (H1–H6) {#section-7-1}

The six human-domain clauses have different roles. Three are empirical hypotheses that await specific tests, two are operational or classification rules, and one is a normative boundary. How each clause can hold depends on its role.

| Clause | Role | What May Be Determined | Limits That Cannot Be Crossed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning and Coordination | Empirical hypothesis | If a candidate meaning arrangement specified in advance produces a difference beyond the threshold in resource, action, or coordination outcomes relative to the comparison condition, determine it to be a conditional orientation anchor | Do not infer a unified inner state, genuine consent, or a common will from slogans, shared language, or an interpreter’s projections |
| Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility | Operational rule | State the uptake carrier, bearers of costs of the same type, beneficiaries, the right to stop, and the responsible party separately | Classification alone does not establish intertemporal reproduction. The ability to take up the work does not become an obligation to do so |
| Institutional Write-Back | Classification rule | If an appeal, a check, or feedback changes records, rules, resources, roles, responsibilities, memory, or stopping conditions and actual execution is documented, determine it to be institutional write-back | Intake does not amount to write-back. A single write-back does not establish learning, and a single update does not establish long-term repair |
| Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity | Empirical hypothesis | If position, mediation, disclosure conditions, outcome measure, comparison conditions, and threshold are all specified in advance, determine the channels of masking, amplification, or reflexivity that the evidence supports | Labels such as “power,” “platform,” “metric,” or “AI” must not substitute for a mechanism chain and a counterfactual. Silence does not amount to consent |
| Historical Trace Carriers | Empirical hypothesis | If the historical event, candidate carrier, baseline, trace measure, threshold, and durability window are all specified in advance and a durable difference exceeds the threshold, determine only a candidate trace | Traces do not establish path effects. Obtain separate evidence under §4.5 for the subsequent effects of the current carrier. Only a claim about an additional historical increment requires a historical-increment test. Repair or protective action needs to pass the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1) separately, with the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, the corresponding authorization, and the operational procedures |
| Open-Ended Undertaking | Normative boundary | Describe actual costs, voluntariness, direction, alternative explanations, structural consequences, and the right to stop | Do not diagnose whether a person “has love,” treat sacrifice as proof of love, or coerce one party into continuing to bear the burden |

The outputs of the six clauses are limited to empirical descriptions, classification results, gaps in evidence, or normative requirements. These outputs do not themselves authorize any real-world adjustment. Classification under “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility” does not authorize redistribution or attribution of responsibility. Classification under “Institutional Write-Back” does not authorize changing records or rules. The normative boundary of “Open-Ended Undertaking” does not itself implement protection or stopping measures. Any real-world action must in every case separately pass the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1), with the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, the corresponding authorization within the scope of jurisdiction and authorization, and the operational procedures.

The evidence status of all three empirical hypotheses is “awaiting evidence from a specific test.” Their specific tests follow the same procedure as the formal tests of the four empirical questions (§4.1). They have two stages, pre-registration and results: the target, criteria, and rules are set down before the results are seen, and the results are appended after the test is carried out. They use the same four outcome statuses. They likewise have a positive criterion and a null-conclusion criterion. Any deviation is disclosed. The same discipline applies to evidence references and analysis materials. The only difference is that such a test can test only one of these three hypotheses.

A “Meaning and Coordination” test must select in advance exactly one outcome criterion among resource allocation, action choice, and coordination outcomes. A “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test must select in advance exactly one criterion among evidence coverage, safety of expression, object behavior, and reflexive response. A “Historical Trace Carriers” test must select in advance exactly one carrier family, one specific carrier belonging to that family, and one durability criterion. A list of multiple carriers, or a specific carrier that does not match the selected family, makes the test ineligible. Criteria that were not selected must not be used to rescue a test after the results. Failure on the positive criterion does not automatically mean that the null model (a comparison model without the claimed factor) is supported. A “Historical Trace Carriers” test establishes only a trace within the specified carrier and time window. Obtain separate evidence under §4.5 for future effects of the current carrier. A historical increment beyond the current representation requires a separate, independent historical-increment test. Internal definitions, concept labels, and the framework’s own statements do not count as external empirical support.

## 7.2 Three Sets of Categories That Must Be Kept Distinct {#section-7-2}

The eight categories fall into three sets: generative condition, generative subject, and generative event; uptake carrier and responsible party; and source, mechanism, and behavior of destabilization. Separating the categories keeps the subsequent questions, evidence requirements, and limits on responsibility distinct. The categories specify what materials a case must supply. They do not assert that the corresponding facts about the world already hold. Each category requires grounds directly relevant to the claim in question. A single record can support several distinct facts among them, but their dependence on that same source must be retained, and the record must not be counted repeatedly as independent validation. A single case can be placed in several categories at once. Establishing one category cannot settle the others on its own. When stating formally that categories hold independently of one another, identify each category exactly, one by one. A collective name for the set must not be used in their place, and a short name that could coincide with a name used elsewhere once taken out of context must not be given on its own.

</div>

### 7.2.1 Generative Condition (GC) {#section-7-2-1}

The generative condition asks “What conditions make generation possible or more likely?” Resources, technologies, institutions, relationships, trauma, crises, and windows of opportunity can all be candidate conditions. Conditions have no intent. When compiling the condition inventory, first record when the conditions arose, changed, or were removed, and what the actual exposure was. A claim that a condition makes generation more likely also requires a comparison suited to that claim, a difference in outcomes, a candidate channel, and competing explanations. Generative conditions, generative subjects, and generative events are tested separately. Observing a condition neither identifies the generative subject nor establishes that a generative event has occurred.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.1" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.2 Generative Subject (GS) {#section-7-2-2}

The generative subject asks “Who can understand and carry out acts of initiation, translation, or handover?” The identity of the subject, capability, resources, actual actions, and authorization status must be verified separately. Technologies, crises, platforms, metrics, and resource windows must not be counted as subjects. Evidence of subject status cannot be inferred merely from the occurrence of an event. The generative subject does not automatically correspond to the full set of responsible parties.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.2" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.3 Generative Event (GE) {#section-7-2-3}

The generative event asks “What state transition took shape, and when?” First use the same measurement conventions to record the state before generation, the time of the event, the state afterward, and the criteria used. Competing events during the same period and a bridge to the mechanism are used to explain further how the event formed. The absence of an identified mechanism must not be written up as nonoccurrence of an observed event. A generative event can result from action by a subject or from emergence without a subject. A generative subject must not be invented for emergence without a subject. The event itself must not be personified.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.3" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.4 Uptake Carrier (CV) {#section-7-2-4}

The uptake carrier asks “Who or what actually executes, transmits, maintains, records, provides care, or repairs?” Personnel, positions, procedures, facilities, and technical systems can all be carriers. Evidence comes from task logs, flows of resources and capacity, costs, rights to stop, and changes in function after carrier replacement. An uptake carrier is not a responsible party. Facilities and procedures perform functions. Doing so does not by itself make them morally or legally responsible parties. Whether an organization can bear institutional or legal responsibility must be judged separately according to its standing as a subject and the specific rules. An organization must not be put in the same category as equipment.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.4" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.5 Responsible Party (RS) {#section-7-2-5}

The responsible party asks “Who bears which kind of responsibility for what, and on what basis?” Responsibilities arising from conduct, decisions, authorization, oversight, benefit, and remedy must be stated separately. Specify knowledge, control, scope, period, appeal, and remedy. Evidence for determining the responsible party must be independent of who is most visible, who actually takes up the work, and who bears the consequences. Someone carrying out the work with limited authority must not become the default primary responsible party merely because they are the most visible.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.5" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.6 Source of Destabilization (IS) {#section-7-2-6}

The source of destabilization asks “Where does a disturbance enter, or where does it accumulate?” The record must include where the disturbance first appeared or accumulated, its point of entry, temporal order, differences in exposure, and alternative sources. Finding a source does not establish malice, a mechanism, or full responsibility. A dissenter’s status must not be counted directly as a source.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.6" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.7 Mechanism of Destabilization (IM) {#section-7-2-7}

The mechanism of destabilization asks “Through what causal path does a disturbance change a state, boundary, feedback, rhythm, or orientation?” The evidence must show, at each step, the mediating links, temporal order, evidence for the bridge, conditions under which the claim can fail, and competing mechanisms. People, groups, and dissenters must not be directly labeled as mechanisms. A story about a mechanism must not substitute for causal evidence.

</div>

<SectionLesson section="7.2.7" />

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.2.8 Behavior of Destabilization (IB) {#section-7-2-8}

The behavior of destabilization asks “What actions, failures to act, or outputs were actually observed?” Original records, logs, or observations from multiple sources must retain the subject or carrier, context, scale, and time window. Observed behavior does not establish its source or mechanism and does not by itself establish malice or full responsibility. For silence and absence, also distinguish nonoccurrence, unobservability, and concealment for protection.

The source, mechanism, and behavior of destabilization are not attachments belonging only to the variable “Structural Load.” They are a classification of destabilization that cuts across several variables. They all apply to six human variables (§7.3): “Boundaries and Interfaces,” “Drive–Uptake Chain,” “Feedback Write-Back,” “Conditional Potential Field,” “Structural Load,” and “Evolutionary Phase.” A case may state source, mechanism, and behavior separately under these variables, but independent evidence must be submitted for each of the three. The source must not be inferred from visible behavior. A person or dissenter must not be labeled a mechanism. Malice and full responsibility must not be inferred directly from the location of a source.

</div>

## 7.3 A Unified Description of the Eleven Human Variables {#section-7-3}

All eleven variables belong to the human adaptation layer; they are not a universal ontology. Every variable is described by the same set of items, and the complete detailed descriptions are in Appendix A. The items, in order, are: name; proposition; scope of application; the proposition role it belongs to; conditions for suspension; what can be inferred; what cannot be inferred; inferential premises; method requirements; domain restriction; objects of application; support routes; nine-axis scale profile; effective object; states; observables; evidence requirements; input dependencies; output effects; uptake carriers; responsible parties; time window and lag; uncertainty; local exclusion zone; affected positions; normative status; items kept unchanged across scales; items that must be added on scale elevation; items that change with scale; objects to which it does not apply; elevations that are prohibited; judgment ceiling; action ceiling; counterexamples; appeal; restoration. For a specific application, fill in the required material according to the selected support route. Mark the remaining items under §2.9 as unknown, not applicable, unobservable, or withheld for protection, and give the reasons. Completing the items does not require unlimited collection of personal information. Nor is it a prerequisite for ordinary understanding, seeking help, or accessing lawful services.

The four kinds of formal dependency must be written separately (§2.3). Inferential premises list only the formal premises indispensable at every conclusion level. Method requirements list only the epistemic, normative, or procedural requirements that every level must pass. Domain restriction states which definitions, classification rules, or normative boundaries restrict the variable. Objects of application state only the objects to which the variable applies.

Conditions needed at only one particular level go in the support routes. Each route states in full which conclusion level it belongs to, in what circumstances it applies, which further inferential premises it needs, which further method requirements it needs, what conclusion can be drawn, and where the ceiling on that conclusion lies. This separates candidate descriptions, classifications, conditional effects, mechanism explanations, and intertemporal explanations. Missing prerequisites for a higher level must not wrongly block conclusions at lower levels, and complete items at a lower level cannot be used to slip a higher-level conclusion through.

Formal tests (whether of the four empirical questions or of human empirical hypotheses), human variables, and conditional mechanisms may be listed only as inferential premises and must not be disguised as method requirements. The method requirements item lists only the epistemic constraints (§4.8), the conditions for causal claims (§2.4), the conditions for evidence (§2.5), the conditions for analogy (§2.6), the conditions for sources (§2.7), or explicitly identified requirements concerning normative premises and operational procedures. The three clauses “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility,” “Institutional Write-Back,” and “Open-Ended Undertaking” (§7.1) provide no empirical support. They remain in the domain restriction item as classification or normative restrictions. Any reference to an item of content must indicate which kind it belongs to: a human variable, a category, a scale axis, a scale transformation, or a basic structural concept. A reference to the operational procedures must specify which of the four steps is meant. It cannot be written in general terms, and still less can it be confused with the organizational-level axis because the two share a short name. The input dependencies and output effects items record only what passes between variables and the direction in which variables flow. They do not replace the four kinds of formal dependency above.

</div>

### 7.3.1 Structural Domain (HV01) {#section-7-3-1}

A definition by the seven elements of a structural object (§3.1) only puts forward a candidate object. It does not establish that a structural domain holds. A limited effective structural domain may be determined within the scope of a test only if a pre-registered grouping-gain test (§4.3) shows that a candidate grouping achieves an out-of-sample or extrapolation gain beyond the threshold relative to a matched null model, under the preselected outcome, model class, sample or extrapolation unit, and threshold. Observable signals include differences in relations and constraints inside and outside the boundary, entry and exit records, repeated co-occurrence of common problems or institutional rules, and whether the grouping gain remains stable after changing the grouping rules.

Without a passing grouping-gain test, the variable “Structural Domain” can still supply the other ten variables with a reference to a candidate object, with its boundary, time window, and uncertainty stated, for factual description, applicability checks, and judgments in the relevant domain. It cannot label the grouping as an effective structural domain already supported by a grouping gain. The existence of a legally constituted organization, a traceable relationship, or a specific service recipient can have separate documentary and observational grounds. Whether such a grouping has out-of-sample value must be compared separately. Naming does not create a community, and shared circumstances do not establish a common will.

</div>

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.3.2 Boundaries and Interfaces (HV02) {#section-7-3-2}

The effective object of this variable is a boundary that selectively affects flows of resources, information, rights, or responsibilities. Membership status, admission and exit, cross-boundary flows, gatekeeping nodes, grounds for refusal, and discrepancies between formal and actual boundaries must be observed together. The basic route depends only on the object supplied by the variable “Structural Domain.” It may list an inventory of boundaries, accessibility, and the risk of exclusion, with no strict requirement for a grouping-gain test or a channel-effect test. The selective-effect route may determine the selective effect of a boundary for a specified channel, time window, and position only if it also has an eligible channel-effect test (§4.4) and meets the conditions for causal claims (§2.4). A subset of members, formal representatives, or highly visible positions must not be taken directly as representative of everyone affected.

</div>

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.3.3 Orientation Anchor (HV03) {#section-7-3-3}

The effective object of this variable is a goal, identity, memory, commitment, fear, or common problem that may change outcomes involving resources, action, or coordination. The candidate route has no strict dependency on the hypothesis “Meaning and Coordination” (§7.1). It may first record materials on meaning, heterogeneous expressions, disputes about representativeness, and the need for further evidence. Only when a “Meaning and Coordination” test (§4.2) is supported on the single criterion selected in advance among resource allocation, action choice, and coordination outcomes does the analysis enter the effective anchor route. An outcome family that was not selected must not be used to rescue the claim after the results. Expression alone does not constitute a structural anchor. A few affirmative expressions do not establish that the group shares the meaning. The variable “Orientation Anchor” provides no authorization to impose uniformity of meaning.

Meaning acts through more than an individual’s private beliefs. Forms of address, narratives, rituals, commitments, and classifications can provide publicly recognizable forms of action. They allow people to tell whether a particular expression is a request for help, a refusal, an act of entering an agreement, an act of mourning, or a challenge to eligibility. The analysis should separately identify the context in which the parties understand the form, who has authority to interpret it in a way that carries institutional consequences, and what changes in action or resources occur through that interpretation. An interpretation can be reasonable while its causal effect remains undetermined. Coordination can occur without participants sharing ultimate beliefs.

When translating concepts from different traditions into the framework, the questions they answer in their original practice, the authority to interpret them, the obligations, and the disputes should be preserved. Similar names alone are insufficient. Religious identities, sources of norms, legal qualifications, and personal beliefs sometimes share the same translated term and can still perform different functions. Comparison may yield partial comparability, an inability to align some local features, or insufficient material. There is no need to conclude that entire traditions are closed off from one another. Assessing task-relevant loss of meaning in translation must take into account both the analyst’s task and the practical questions faced by those affected. A classification can suffice for an administrative count without necessarily explaining who can draw water, inherit, speak, or refuse.

</div>

### 7.3.4 Generative Node (HV04) {#section-7-3-4}

A generative node must be split into three items, the generative condition, generative subject, and generative event (§7.2), each described independently, with the positions of the condition, subject, and event on the nine scale axes stated separately. The route of sorting into categories has no strict dependency on the variable “Orientation Anchor.” It also does not claim that causal generation has occurred merely because every item of the three categories has been filled in. Only when the mechanism route also has an eligible channel-effect test does it determine a candidate mechanism of generation within the specified channel. Observable signals include changes in conditions, the subject’s capability and actual actions, the state transition from before to after the event, and the bridge from local to aggregate levels in emergence without a subject. Technologies, crises, and windows of resources can only be candidate generative conditions and acquire no intent. Generative events and generative subjects cannot substitute for one another.

</div>

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.3.5 Action Uptake Layer (HV05) {#section-7-3-5}

The effective object of this variable is a person, position, procedure, facility, or institutional arrangement that actually executes, transmits, maintains, records, provides care, or repairs. Basic classification has no strict dependency on the variable “Generative Node.” Under the clause “Action Uptake and Classification of Responsibility” (§7.1), task logs, resources and capacity, costs, rights to stop, and alternative arrangements must be observed separately, as must the respective bases for decisions, authorization, oversight, benefit, and remedy.

Further claims each carry additional requirements. A claim of a functional effect of the carrier requires a separate channel-effect test. A claim of carrier intertemporal reproduction requires a channel-effect test covering subsequent outcomes and time windows, together with evidence of maintenance, replenishment, and replacement across rounds and of actual continuation. A claim of historical-increment reproduction additionally requires a historical-increment test. Historical carrier traces follow a separate route. A durable trace within the specified carrier and time window is determined only when a “Historical Trace Carriers” test (§4.2) is supported on the one specific carrier and the durability criterion selected in advance, and the family to which that carrier belongs matches the selected subtype. The trace is then submitted to a historical-increment test as a candidate historical variable. A “Historical Trace Carriers” test does not by itself establish future path effects.

Facilities and procedures do not become responsible parties merely by executing a function. Organizational responsibility is assessed separately according to the organization’s institutional qualifications. The capability to take up work creates no unlimited obligation to do so.

Uptake of care work requires distinguishing the execution of tasks from management work. Management work includes identifying needs, scheduling, reminding, checking, and being on call. Add records of these burdens only if they are relevant to the current objective. Someone else doing one task can be genuine help. That alone does not establish that all the management work has been taken over. The resources and support available to those taking over and the conditions under which they may refuse must be included in the same evaluation. A heavy burden may result from shared scarcity and does not by itself establish that anyone was maliciously compelled to take it on. The scope and duration of existing duties, responsibility for continuing the work, and the safety of those harmed should be argued separately.

</div>

### 7.3.6 Drive–Uptake Chain (HV06) {#section-7-3-6}

The effective object of this variable is a traceable chain that converts orientation and generation into sustained action. Local chain segments may first be recorded on their own. Only the complete candidate chain route requires the records of the three variables “Orientation Anchor,” “Generative Node,” and “Action Uptake Layer” to be linked segment by segment under the same object, the same scale, the same time window, and the same mapping of quantities. The effective channel route additionally requires an eligible channel-effect test. Every segment must record inputs, outputs, delay, loss, points of interruption, resources, and costs. A claim that “there is drive” must not fill a gap in uptake. One pass through the whole chain does not establish intertemporal reproduction. An intertemporal historical increment must be tested separately in a historical-increment test. Historical carriers can be put forward as candidates only by a “Historical Trace Carriers” test (§4.2).

An effect at each segment is still insufficient to establish an effect for the chain as a whole. Assessment of the whole chain must retain correspondence within the same case, the shared scope of support, joint response, temporal order, capacity, and the endpoint outcome. Segment means obtained separately cannot be multiplied directly. See §11.6 for a formal counterexample in which the mean effects of two segments are both positive while the effect of the chain as a whole is zero. This restriction applies to unconditional combination. It does not invalidate a limited total effect already supported by a design that meets the requirements.

</div>

<div v-pre class="theory-source">


### 7.3.7 Feedback Write-Back (HV07) {#section-7-3-7}

The effective object of this variable is a return channel capable of changing a subsequent institutional state or transition. Basic write-back classification relies on the definition of effective feedback (§3.3) and the clause “Institutional Write-Back” (§7.1), and has no strict dependency on the variable “Action Uptake Layer.” Evidence of submission and of intake, how the changed content looked before and after the change, actual execution, the time of entry into effect, and duration must be observed separately. Signal arrival alone, intake alone, a statement of position alone, and a change to recorded content without execution all fall short of effective write-back. The causal feedback route also requires a channel-effect test. The carrier learning route additionally requires an update that can be retained, repeated use, a comparison against a prespecified task, and corresponding causal evidence. Only historical-increment learning adds a historical-increment test. A single write-back does not establish learning.

The number of decisions changed does not define the quality of review. Upholding the original decision for stated reasons can fulfill the relevant procedural responsibility and may improve explanations, records, or subsequent handling. These changes should be recorded under the object that actually changed. If the target state does not change, do not record it as effective write-back for that target. This does not negate the value of hearing the parties and of fair procedure. An erroneous change of decision can still constitute write-back without constituting a correction.

</div>

### 7.3.8 Conditional Potential Field (HV08) {#section-7-3-8}

The effective object of this variable is a set of conditions that change the probability of action, safety of expression, or distribution of evidence through testable channels. The basic inventory of conditions is constrained only by the observation position and evidence requirements. It has no strict requirement for the hypothesis “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” (§7.1). It can only list candidate conditions, heterogeneous positions, blind spots, and the need for further evidence. The route for position or mediation effects requires a “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” test (§4.2) that is supported on the single criterion selected in advance among evidence coverage, safety of expression, and object behavior. The reflexive response route must also meet the epistemic constraint “Conditional Observational Participation” (§4.8.3). It must establish that observation, naming, scoring, or publication did reach the object through an actual channel. For a claim that a reflexive response is sustained, also verify that the response enters a current carrier, is retained across time windows, and continues to have an effect. One response does not warrant extrapolating a sustained response. A claim of an additional historical residual takes a separate historical-increment test. The conditional potential field is not a subject with a will. It does not “want,” “choose,” or “punish.” Labels such as “power” or “platform” cannot substitute for a mechanism.

“Potential field” here is only a working name for this set of conditions. It presupposes no common unit, conserved quantity, potential function, or additive forces. These mathematical structures may be used only in a specific model that supplies variables, units, mappings, and predictions that can fail. The name itself provides no physical mechanism.

The effects of power extend beyond masking existing facts or suppressing existing wishes. Eligibility classifications, examinations and records, task training, spatial arrangements, and rewards and penalties can change which options are actually feasible, which capabilities are recognized, what behavior is treated as normal, and how actors understand themselves. The proposal of categories, adoption by institutions, allocation of resources or opportunities, responses by those concerned, and subsequent feedback must be traced step by step. The existence of a category does not establish that it has been applied. Its application does not establish that everyone accepts it.

A person can accept, use strategically, conceal, reinterpret, or reject a given status. Researchers must not infer that a person identifies with that status directly from public compliance. They must not prejudge all classifications as false or harmful. Capability, communication, legal effect, belief in legitimacy, and normative justification each require separate evidence. An institutional arrangement may provide services and a status with recourse to appeal. It may also create exclusion. The specific channels, alternative arrangements, and consequences at different positions should be compared. These are questions about candidate mechanisms under the hypothesis “Power, Mediation, and Reflexivity” and the related variables. They cannot replace the conditions that the hypothesis and the variables need to obtain empirical support.

</div>

### 7.3.9 Structural Load (HV09) {#section-7-3-9}

The effective object of this variable is the relation of task and coordination demands to capacity and recovery margin within a given time window. The instantaneous task–capacity route has no strict requirement for a channel-effect test or for the “Load, Capacity, and Recovery” mechanism (§6.6), and no strict dependency on the variables “Action Uptake Layer” and “Drive–Uptake Chain.” It records margins, backlogs, local gaps, and recovery status only under the same time window, the same position, and the same type mapping. Only the overload mechanism route requires both the “Load, Capacity, and Recovery” mechanism and an eligible channel-effect test. For a claim of carrier cumulative damage or delayed recovery, use §4.5 to add the current state of damage or capacity, its formation process, and comparisons across time windows. The route that claims a historical increment beyond the current representation additionally requires a historical-increment test. Observations must cover task volume, backlog, delays, errors, staffing resources, hidden labor, exit, and recovery curves. Local overload masked by averages must be retained. Load here is defined by the relation between tasks and capacity. It does not generalize “entropy” into one additive entity shared across social, psychological, and institutional domains.

Task duration, simultaneous activities, being on call, cognitive management, sleep loss, and future opportunities have different units and also overlap with one another. Comparisons of burdens must specify what can be added and what would be double-counted. A mean cannot be assigned to every bearer as their actual load. Reducing a burden at one position may shift it to other households, posts, or future periods. Conclusions about the net burden should stay within the scope of the present question.

The same total hours can correspond to very different continuous blocks of available time. Responsibilities as a default contact, being on call, and filling in at short notice can cut up the uninterrupted time needed for study, work, rest, or preparing to exit, even if they are rarely actually invoked. Add this record only when the task in question depends on continuous blocks of time. It records the length and predictability of the usable uninterrupted blocks; it does not convert them into hours and does not add them to clock time already occupied.

### 7.3.10 Evolutionary Phase (HV10) {#section-7-3-10}

The seven state prototypes (§8.3) and the path of “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” (§8.5) apply only to human intentional collectives that are taking shape or operating. Meaning, generation, uptake, and the formation of institutions are the component aspects to be checked. They need not all be mature before description can begin. Populations without grounds for identifying a human intentional collective, technical systems, and categories drawn solely from similarities do not directly qualify for the use of these prototypes.

Here the four variables “Orientation Anchor,” “Generative Node,” “Action Uptake Layer,” and “Feedback Write-Back” serve as four components, and their records are shared inputs to the checks for all routes. The dependency is on four kinds of assessment record that can be checked. It does not require the four components to be established empirically. A record may state “missing,” “not applicable,” or “not supported.” Accordingly, the basic route determines only applicability, inapplicability, or whether components are missing. For descriptions under the seven state prototypes, directly check the entry conditions, observations, counterexamples, and falsification materials under §8.2–8.3. No prior observation of a second range of operation is required. Check the multiple intervals and thresholds that the “Phase and Threshold Transition” mechanism (§6.7) requires only for a further claim that a phase pattern and transition meeting that mechanism exist. A claim of a causal phase transition separately requires a channel-effect test. For a claim of phase continuation or delayed recovery sustained by the current carrier, obtain separate evidence under §4.5. A claim of historical-increment hysteresis or path differences requires a separate historical-increment test.

Between phases, skipping, parallelism, mixing, moving back, splitting, merging, dormancy, absorption, and functional transfer are all allowed. “Orderly Exit and Benign Demise” is a conversion path and carries no “lower” rank. Naming a phase does not authorize advancement, elimination, or moral ranking.

### 7.3.11 Open-Ended Undertaking Action (HV11) {#section-7-3-11}

The effective object of this variable is a human undertaking arrangement in which action, cost, direction, voluntariness, conditions for refusal, and consequences must be reviewed separately. It has no strict dependency on the variable “Action Uptake Layer” and is used along three routes. Route one is a record of action and cost: it may first record the facts of the action, costs, direction, risk of coercion, and gaps in the right to stop. Route two is a description of bounded voluntary undertaking. It requires evidence of the action and its costs and a clear direction. Voluntariness, and genuine conditions for refusal, exit, or negotiation, require separate support. Competing explanations such as dependence, fear, interests, and acting for show must also be checked. The outcome must be recorded according to what happened as “realized,” “unrealized,” “harmful,” or “unknown.” Structural success is not a prerequisite for route two. Route three is a causal judgment about structural consequences: it additionally requires a channel-effect test and corresponding causal evidence. A causal effect cannot retroactively supply the grounds for voluntariness, and route two holding does not guarantee in advance that route three holds.

This variable is constrained by the clause “Open-Ended Undertaking” (§7.1) and the normative premise “Love and Undertaking Must Not Be Conscripted” (§14.3), and does not authorize any protective measure on its own. It must not be used to diagnose personality or love, or to elevate an individual undertaking to a group obligation.

Open-ended undertaking action is a limited category for reviewing particular actions and arrangements for undertaking. It does not define love, detect it, or rank personalities. Relaxed companionship, expressions of concern by people whose capabilities are limited, unsuccessful acts of creation, private mourning, and refusals that produce no new structure retain their experiential significance and value even without high costs or detectable structural consequences. Conversely, high costs, sustained contributions, and statements of good intent do not automatically establish free consent, fair distribution, or justified obligations. When materials are insufficient, labels applied from outside should be stated less strongly. The parties still have standing to be heard, to be protected, and to refuse.

## 7.4 Elevation, Evidence, and Action Ceilings {#section-7-4}

When human variables are transferred across scales, compare the source scale and the target scale axis by axis. State what is preserved, changed, and lost, as well as positions with low visibility and local exclusion zones. The following elevations in inference are prohibited without exception. Individual experience must not directly represent the state of a group, and a local mechanism must not directly represent the aggregate. Undertaking by members must not be turned directly into a group obligation, and a carrier’s function must not be turned directly into responsibility. Behavior must not be treated directly as a source or mechanism, and a stage or a load must not become a moral rank.

Every variable retains three items: counterexamples, appeal, and restoration. For appeal and restoration, all variables follow the common rule for appeal and restoration. An appeal channel must be safely accessible and provide anti-retaliation protection. Depending on the risks and context, ways to submit anonymously, through a representative, with low exposure, or after a delay should be available. These allow people in low-power positions, people being evaluated, people taking up the work, people excluded, dissenters, and people unable to speak publicly to submit counterevidence. An appeal must also trigger a review independent of the original judgment or decision chain. The original decision maker must not close it by simply reaffirming the decision. The variables can only put forward these procedural requirements. They cannot directly set up or operate the channels.

Once restoration is authorized, it must actually change or restore the state of the object, records, rules, resources, tasks, rights, or downstream judgments. Specify who is responsible for carrying it out and the deadline for completion, and keep a history of the changes and verification of completion. Publishing a statement, accepting an appeal, or promising future improvements does not count as completing restoration.

Judgment levels cannot exceed the range covered by the evidence, and action levels cannot exceed the scope of authorization. Even when the three human empirical hypotheses are supported by specific tests, they only strengthen description or explanation and do not automatically generate a normative conclusion. Any protection, harm reduction, repair, attribution of responsibility, resource reallocation, or formal action must still separately pass the conclusion rule “Normative Bridging” (§4.14.1), with the normative premises stated explicitly on this occasion, the corresponding authorization, and the four-step operational procedure.

## 7.5 Actor–Circle Mutual Constitution {#section-7-5}

When the eleven human variables are used in multi-circle analysis and projection, do not treat people as passive components of circles[^tn-circle-08] or circles as shells for personality. Each actor retains slow, intermediate-timescale, and fast states (§9.2) and multiple roles. Each circle retains its own boundary, members, channels, and identity criterion. Record the effects in both directions separately. Specify how a circle changes an actor through resources, information, roles, rewards and penalties, reputation, status, and exit conditions. Specify how an actor changes a circle through labor, expression, refusal, forming alliances, exit, bridging, and institutional write-back.

| Aspect of Mutual Constitution | Record for the Individual | Record for the Circle | Evidentiary Ceiling |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Entry and roles | Basis for membership, identification with the circle, conflict, and ability to exit | Qualifications, expectations, rights, and responsibilities | Membership does not establish that a person identifies with the circle |

| Feedback and write-back | Action, appeal, refusal, and exit | Whether records, rules, roles, and resources actually changed | Receiving a submission by itself is insufficient for effective write-back |

Personality hypotheses may serve only as candidates for an actor’s slow variables. The seven state prototypes may serve only as state prototypes for human intentional collectives. The two must not be applied to one another. An individual must not be labeled as a “lower stage,” and a member’s personality must not be used to infer a circle’s state. Materials about individuals may enter a judgment about a circle only through an explicit mechanism of aggregation, representation, or propagation, or an explicit institutional mechanism.

## 7.6 Practice, Institutional Reproduction, and Institutional Change {#section-7-6}

Human structures can change outside formal reforms and can be sustained without changes to records or rules. Actors continually interpret and invoke rules, allocate resources, handle exceptions, teach skills, and confirm eligibility. These practices can reproduce existing relations or gradually change how the structures actually operate. Written rules, public claims, everyday working rules, and actual consequences must be kept distinct. Practices can change while documents stay the same. Practices can also continue after documents change. These possibilities must be recorded separately. Formal write-back is still determined under the clause “Institutional Write-Back” (§7.1). Everyday institutionalization and habit formation must not be equated with a single write-back on the basis of naming alone.

In analyzing a round of change, first reconstruct separately the rules, resources, and relations already in place, the actors’ projects and the range of options available to them under those conditions, the interactions that followed, and the arrangements later retained, reorganized, or interrupted. The next round may inherit results from the previous one. Later success must not be entered retroactively as an initial condition. Temporal sequence cannot by itself establish causation. Those involved may be unable to articulate fully the knowledge they exercise in skilled practice. Reflection and innovation are also constrained by time, resources, power, and others’ responses.

Generation and reproduction admit competing mechanisms. Compliance can arise from acceptance, dependence, the risk of punishment, or convention. Similar organizational forms can result from technological effects, coercive requirements, occupational training, or imitation of exemplars. Each explanation must identify the process materials that can distinguish it from competing explanations and state its failure conditions. Individual action does not disappear merely because it is subject to conditions. A structural explanation does not automatically absolve people who are informed and genuinely able to choose of responsibility.

### 7.6.1 Mutual Reinforcement of Allocation and Capability {#section-7-6-1}

Among the reproduction mechanisms described in §7.6, one candidate can be proposed and must be tested against counterexamples: the allocation of tasks, training, and opportunities affects who gains practice, practice forms capability, and the current distribution of capability in turn becomes the reason for the next round of allocation. On the concentrating side, critical tasks are continually given to the most skilled person without allocating time for handover, training, and documentation. The short-term completion rate rises, critical knowledge becomes more concentrated, the cost of replacement rises, and continued reliance on the same person gains a new reason (see §8.6). On the excluding side, restrictions on eligibility or opportunity keep some people from gaining practice; a capability gap forms as a result and is in turn cited as grounds for continued exclusion (see §7.3.8 and §10.6.1). Success achieved under the original goal can also change the conditions of the next round through this mutual reinforcement.

Claiming that it holds in a given case requires materials spanning at least two rounds. The allocation is made by a rule or decision that can be located. The capability involved really does depend on practice to form, and practice is obtained mainly through that allocation. The later round’s allocation actually differs because of current capability, rather than merely mentioning capability among its reasons. The gap or degree of concentration widens relative to the starting point. Evidence on the formation, retention, and invocation of capability is obtained under §4.5. When materials are insufficient, the mechanism is only a candidate, used to arrange discriminating observations (§6.14). Applying for formal mechanism support additionally requires evidence along the carrier route of the conclusion rule “Human Uptake and Intertemporal Reproduction” (§4.13.3).

Competing explanations include the following: the gap already existed before the allocation; pay or incentives are insufficient; the successor’s authority is incomplete; the task itself requires long-term training; eligibility restrictions come from external regulation or certification; the choices of those involved, or attrition of trainees; overall scarcity of resources; and allocation actually being determined by preference, discrimination, relationships of trust, or incumbents’ interests, with capability serving only as the nominal reason.

Three observations are discriminating. First, whether the gap or dependence appears only after the allocation and widens round by round, or already exists before the allocation and stays roughly unchanged. Second, whether people of comparable capability receive comparable allocations; if the allocation still does not change after the gap is eliminated, the link by which capability becomes the reason for the next round of allocation does not hold. Third, whether, after handover time, training resources, or access opportunities are granted according to standards written in advance and independently verifiable, the dependence or gap declines within a time window commensurate with the period over which that capability forms; this observation needs to be compared with units whose resources are equally tight but whose allocation rules differ. If any one of these runs contrary to expectation, narrow or withdraw the claim for the corresponding link. It must not be said, only after no decline is seen, that the conditions granted were not genuine.

That this mechanism holds does not mean that anyone arranged it maliciously or that the dependence or gap cannot change, and it does not cancel earlier genuine results; whether the gap will continue to widen or will be hard to change requires separate evidence. Professional capability can be genuinely scarce, and rotation and redundancy also have costs. Whether to adjust the allocation, and who pays the price, is still handled by Part 14.
