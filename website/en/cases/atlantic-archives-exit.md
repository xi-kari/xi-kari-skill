# Atlantic’s archives: custody is one part of orderly exit

The Atlantic Philanthropies was a limited-life foundation. Cornell University Library’s collection description records a September 2015 grant to collect, organize, and promote Atlantic’s archives. Cornell’s contemporary news account identifies Atlantic as the funder. The collection description still describes closure in 2020 as forthcoming, while the foundation’s website states that it completed its work in 2020. Keep those source positions distinct.

## Two collections and two dates

The description distinguishes Atlantic’s corporate and grant records from the Columbia University oral-history project. The former include records of the limited-life decision, steps toward closure, grant files, assessments, and lessons. Some material has access restrictions identified in finding aids. The overview does not give a date for transferring Atlantic’s own archives to Cornell.

The oral histories appear under related collections. Cornell received digital copies of recordings and transcripts from Columbia in 2018, while originals remained at Columbia. The second interview phase was funded by Atlantic. The finding aid records restrictions for particular interviews, including periods and approval conditions.

The 2015 grant and the 2018 oral-history copy transfer therefore cannot fill the missing transfer date for the foundation’s own archive. Similar institutional names do not make two record groups the same object.

## What orderly exit asks beyond custody

[§8.5.2](/en/theory/part-08#section-8-5-2) checks orderly exit across the object and its orientation, core functions, people and relationships, resources, memory and evidence, debts and responsibility, and weak connections. Having a receiving archive addresses only part of memory and evidence.

That part also asks about preservation of successes, failures, repair records, and role memory, together with access, privacy, correction, and protection from retaliation. The collection descriptions establish explicit restrictions, but do not answer every one of those questions. Restricted access is not itself a failure signal: disclosing vulnerable people’s material can itself be harmful.

The other dimensions require separate evidence. Cornell’s custodial role does not by itself identify who bears any remaining foundation obligation. Its receipt of foundation funding also means the site should not treat the collection description as independent verification of the foundation’s overall closure.

## A bounded conclusion

The material supports a narrower fact: there is an identified receiving institution for records and a described custodial arrangement. Overall completion of the orderly exit remains undetermined by these sources. Unknown does not mean failed; the same gap does not support a claim of failed exit.
