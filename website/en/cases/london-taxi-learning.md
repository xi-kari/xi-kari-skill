# London taxi trainees: no qualification does not mean no learning

Woollett and Maguire’s 2011 study tested London taxi trainees near the start of training and again three to four years later. It combined memory tests and structural MRI. The results help distinguish different outcomes rather than treating a license, a task score, and an imaging measure as interchangeable.

## Participants and follow-up

The initial sample comprised 79 male trainees and 31 male controls. At follow-up, 39 trainees had qualified and returned, 20 had not qualified and returned, and 20 did not return. Two of the non-returners had qualified. All 31 controls returned. The follow-up analyses therefore used 39 qualified trainees, 20 unqualified trainees, and 31 controls, rather than the entire original trainee sample. See the [paper](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2011.11.018), pages 2109–2111.

## Different measures gave different answers

At follow-up, both trainee groups outperformed controls on a ten-item judgment of spatial relations between London landmarks: mean scores were 8.51 and 8.75, compared with 7.32 for controls; both comparisons had p = 0.003. The paper did not report a direct comparison between the two trainee groups for this task.

Qualified trainees performed worse than controls on delayed recall of a complex figure (21.93 versus 25.77, p = 0.01). The unqualified group’s comparison with controls was not statistically significant. Significant increases in bilateral posterior hippocampal gray-matter volume were found in qualified trainees, but not in the other groups.

The behavioral comparisons are between groups at follow-up. Different test versions were used at the two assessments; the paper and supplement do not provide each individual’s before-and-after score change. These distinctions matter when describing “improvement.”

## What the evidence supports, and what remains open

The unqualified trainees’ landmark performance cannot be erased by the absence of a license or a significant imaging change. Conversely, performance on ten questions does not establish all competence required for professional qualification.

Training and qualification were not randomized. The sample was entirely male, twenty trainees were lost to follow-up, and qualified and unqualified trainees differed in weekly training hours and examination exposure. The authors also left innate differences open. MRI did not directly identify the cellular mechanism behind the volume changes; several possible biological processes were discussed, not observed as established mechanisms.

## Connect to the theory carefully

[§6.3](/en/theory/part-06#section-6-3) allows ordinary learning to be supported by appropriate domain evidence without prior formal recognition by this framework. Its narrower feedback-mediated-learning claim requires a return causing an update, retention and later use, and relevant task evidence. This paper does not trace individual feedback events through that chain.

[The historical-increment question](/en/theory/part-04#section-4-5) asks whether history adds information or effects beyond the stated current representation. A longitudinal design is not automatically that test. The study does not establish either a positive or a null result on that question, and this absence does not cancel its reported behavioral and imaging findings.
