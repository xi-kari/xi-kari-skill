# Flint complaints: receipt, reply, and closure mean different things

The EPA Inspector General’s 2018 investigation and 2022 follow-up provide records of resident complaints and subsequent administrative changes. They support specific findings about those processes; they do not turn every recorded status into evidence of a completed remedy.

## What happened to the complaints?

The 2018 report states that EPA Region 5 received 87 resident complaints concerning Flint drinking water between May 2014 and January 2016, when EPA issued an emergency administrative order. Thirty mentioned lead. Officials replied with form letters directing residents to state or local water officials; six replies were more than a year late, and eleven complaints had no documented reply. Complaint volume was not treated as an indicator of a water-system problem. See [report 18-P-0221](https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2018-07/documents/_epaoig_20180719-18-p-0221.pdf), printed pages 23–24.

Receipt and replies are supported facts. The audit also identifies a specific missing connection to risk assessment. Neither finding entails that every complaint had no downstream effect of any kind.

## Two intake systems, different findings

Recommendation 8 called for a system to track complaints, assess risks, and promote resolution. In 2022, the Inspector General distinguished two public-tip channels:

- The **Safe Drinking Water Hotline**, managed by the Office of Water, had 2019 procedures requiring tracking, risk assessment, and recording of dispositions. The audit found these responsive to the recommendation.
- **Report a Violation (RAV)**, managed by the Office of Enforcement and Compliance Assurance, could refer tips, produce reports, and open or close cases, but lacked functions to assess tip risks and track outcomes.

These findings came from procedural documents, system-function review, and staff interviews. The follow-up did not report the actual outcome of any individual tip. See [report 22-P-0046](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2022-05/_epaoig_20220517-22-P-0046.pdf), pages 5–6 and 14–17.

## Agreement on a plan is a distinct status

The enforcement office disputed a proposed RAV change on duplication, workload, state-reporting burdens, and authority grounds. The Inspector General narrowed the recommendation to tips retained for EPA handling. In July 2022, the office accepted that narrower recommendation with a planned completion date of April 30, 2023, and the Inspector General changed its status to “resolved.” In this audit vocabulary, “resolved” meant agreement on corrective action; completion remained pending.

The [July 18 response](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-03/Response%20to%20Final%20Flint%20Follow%20Up%20Report%20OA-FY21-0123_7-18-22.final_.pdf) and [July 27 Inspector General comment](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2022-07/_epaoig_22-P-0046_IG_Comment_on_Response.pdf) establish this later administrative step. This page does not establish whether the planned work was subsequently completed.

## Read the evidence without upgrading it

[The definition of effective feedback](/en/theory/part-03#section-3-3) requires a return channel to change a later state, probability, timing, or constraint relative to no return. A closed case in a tracking system is not the same as a resolved hazard. A documented hotline rule change is not proof of implementation for every tip or safer drinking water.

[§6.3](/en/theory/part-06#section-6-3) requires further evidence for feedback-mediated learning: retained updates, later use, and a specified task comparison. The audit’s supported procedural findings remain valid even though it was not designed as a pre-registered channel-effect test or historical-increment test. [§2.1.1](/en/theory/part-02#section-2-1-1) explicitly preserves independently supported domain evidence.
