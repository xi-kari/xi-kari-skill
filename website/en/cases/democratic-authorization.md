# Democratic authorization has a defined scope

Winning an election can confer an office and the powers attached to it. It does not, by itself, settle which decisions that office may make, which procedures apply, or whether an adverse decision can be ignored. Conversely, an unelected body’s claim to independence or expertise does not create unlimited authority over elected government.

This English page is a **v2 reading guide to the question**. The site also preserves a [longer Chinese research report](/cases/democratic-authorization), with an evidence cutoff of October 14, 2026, produced using skill 5.3.1 and its October theory text. That report has not been fully translated here or presented as a newly verified v2 case study. Its dated litigation accounts must be read with their original sources, procedural stages, and cutoff.

## Ask which authority is at issue

| Question | Relevant distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Who holds office? | The applicable electoral and constitutive rules. |
| What can that office do? | The specific legal competence, object, action, territory, and validity period. |
| Does the public support a policy? | Evidence of political support, without inventing each person’s consent. |
| Can an oversight body intervene? | Its own mandate, standards, procedure, and limits. |
| Can an error be corrected? | A usable review path and evidence that a decision or remedy can reach execution. |

[§2.4.3](/en/theory/part-02#section-2-4-3) separates constitutive standing from causal effects. A valid office or right can exist even when implementation is obstructed. Actual control can also exist without valid authority. Neither fact alone proves normative justification.

## Appointment, reform, and immunity are different claims

An elected institution may lawfully appoint some oversight officials or reform a review system. The inquiry must identify the applicable power and the safeguards that remain. The mere involvement of an elected actor does not prove capture; the existence of a formal review body does not prove independence.

The framework’s normative concern is whether affected people can challenge facts, reasons, and decisions through a process capable of correcting them. [§14.5](/en/theory/part-14#section-14-5) also distinguishes responses to individual issues from the ability to challenge the agenda, rules, or final adjudicator. A system can answer complaints at one level while leaving a different, decisive level outside effective review.

This is an explicitly normative inquiry. An analyst should not invent a prediction of inevitable inefficiency or collapse to make an unjustified arrangement appear empirically impossible. Stability, efficiency, lawful standing, and what deserves to be maintained can diverge.

## Oversight itself must remain bounded

A request for records or an investigation can be too broad for its lawful purpose. Limiting a particular method may correct that mismatch; it does not automatically abolish oversight. Likewise, limiting one method does not establish a general exemption from accountability. Compare the purpose, relevant material, authority, intrusion, and available alternatives for the specific action.

The framework’s own publication or authorization statuses are not substitutes for external law. [§14.6](/en/theory/part-14#section-14-6) and [D.21](/en/theory/appendix-d/d-21#section-d-21) require the appropriate source of authority at each stage. A sound analytical recommendation does not issue a real-world mandate.
