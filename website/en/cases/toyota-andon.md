# Toyota Andon: what happens after an abnormality becomes visible?

A visible warning can be a notification or part of an operational response. Toyota’s public factory explanation describes workers using a call button when they find a problem, an Andon display notifying the person in charge, and work resuming after resolution. This is the company’s account of its own process.

## The connections differ by process

Toyota’s [production-system explanation](https://global.toyota/en/company/vision-and-philosophy/production-system/) distinguishes equipment stopping on an abnormality and then signaling staff from assembly work without equipment, where pulling a stop cord can light an Andon and call a supervisor. A light does not have one universal relationship to immediate machine stoppage. The actual links among detection, display, stopping, response, and resumption must be read in their specific setting.

The company’s historical account records that in 1955 the Crown assembly line at the Honsha plant introduced an Andon linked to stop buttons so that the process causing a line stop could be identified. This documents a stated equipment/process connection, not a measured effect across all later factories.

## Read the process through effective feedback

[§3.3](/en/theory/part-03#section-3-3) asks what earlier state or output returns, through what channel, to which receiving position, and with what change to a later field. A signal’s visibility answers only part of that inquiry. In the process description, the useful next question is how the signal changes response and the conditions for continuing work.

The sources support an account of the published arrangement. They do not by themselves estimate the proportion of calls handled correctly, response times at a particular facility, or the independent causal contribution of Andon to quality. Those claims need appropriate observations and comparisons for a declared site, period, and task.

## Handling one problem does not finish the learning question

[§6.3](/en/theory/part-06#section-6-3) distinguishes feedback from feedback-mediated learning. To support the latter, follow an update into a carrier, examine its retention and actual use in later rounds, and compare the specified task outcome. A successful detection–response–resumption sequence does not alone establish lasting learning, general transfer, or a positive net outcome.

The same distinction protects partial achievements. An effective response to one abnormality need not be dismissed because evidence of longer-term learning is absent. State what the material actually supports and leave the additional claim open.
